# Creator outreach — supplying music instead of waiting to be found

Everything else in this project is passive: post daily, hope someone notices.
This is the active version, and it is the only strategy with evidence behind it.

## The evidence

Two independent events, same shape:

- **Bayanihan**, 212,000 plays — 95% of everything PK has ever done — from other
  people's Facebook Reels using the licensed audio.
- **@BambryanPH**, *"Drowning in Plastic in the Philippines. Beach Trash Cleanup"*
  — a Filipino creator who put a PK track in a community-cleanup video, unprompted.

Both are **Filipino creators making community content**. Neither came from PK
posting. Both came from someone needing music and reaching for his.

The Bambryan video does not mention Philosophical King in the title. I searched
YouTube three different ways and could not find it. Jordan only knew because he
happened to see it. **There may be more right now and no way to know** — see the
Content ID section, which is the only fix.

## The offer

Creators have a real, constant problem: they need music that will not get their
video copyright-claimed or muted. PK has 251 tracks and no reason to hoard them.

That is a genuine exchange, not a pitch. Lead with the thing they need.

**Message — comment or email, kept short:**

> Hey — I make philosophy-rap under the name Philosophical King. I've got 251
> tracks and you're welcome to use any of them in your videos, free, no strings.
> A credit in the description is appreciated but not required.
> Everything's here: https://hyperfollow.com/PhilosophicalKing
> The Filipino ones might fit what you make — Bayanihan and Kapwa especially.

Do not ask for anything back. Do not mention follows, streams or algorithms. The
value is entirely theirs; the return is that their audience hears the music.

## BEFORE SENDING ONE OF THESE — check Content ID

**This will blow up in Jordan's face if he skips it.** If DistroKid's YouTube
Content ID is switched on, PK's music automatically claims any YouTube video
containing it. So a creator who accepts "free, no strings" gets a copyright claim
on their video, loses monetisation on it, and quite reasonably never works with
him again.

So: open DistroKid, find whether Content ID is enabled on the catalogue.

- **Content ID OFF** — send the message as written above.
- **Content ID ON** — either whitelist the channel first, or change the wording
  to be honest: *"heads up, my distributor's Content ID may auto-claim the video —
  tell me and I'll clear it."* Less appealing, but survivable. Silently claiming
  someone who took you at your word is not.

The royalty export cannot answer this: YouTube (Ads) shows $4.75 across 311 rows,
but every row is tagged Source Type "Song", so Content ID matches and PK's own
Topic-channel plays are indistinguishable. Only the DistroKid setting says.

## How to work it

**Five a day, by hand.** Watch thirty seconds of a recent video first so the
comment is specific — "the Baseco follow-up was rough to watch" beats "great
video". Generic outreach reads as spam and gets deleted.

Comment publicly rather than emailing where possible: other viewers see it, and
it costs the creator nothing to reply.

**Log every one.** Who, when, whether they replied, whether they used a track.
Twenty messages with two replies is a signal worth having; twenty messages
unrecorded is nothing.

## What success looks like

One creator with 20,000 subscribers using a track reaches more people than a
month of PK's own posting at current numbers. Bayanihan is the proof: it did not
need PK to post at all.

Expect most to ignore it. The maths still works — this costs nothing but time,
and a single yes can be worth more than everything else combined.
