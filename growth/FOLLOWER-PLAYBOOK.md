# PK follower playbook — TikTok & Instagram

Written Aug 2026 against real numbers: 0 followers on both, 251 tracks, daily
automated posting already running, YouTube average-percentage-viewed 53%,
Spotify 113 monthly listeners, and one genuine viral event (Bayanihan, 212k
plays) that nobody has tried to repeat.

---

## The diagnosis nobody wants

You have posted daily, on brand, on schedule, for weeks. You have zero followers.

That is not a distribution problem, a quality problem or an algorithm problem.
**Nothing in the content gives anyone a reason to follow.**

A PK video is a complete experience. Beautiful still, lyric, done. The viewer
feels something and scrolls on — satisfied. Satisfaction is the enemy here.
Following is what people do when a video leaves them wanting the *next* one, and
right now there is no next one, because every post is interchangeable with the
250 behind it.

Everything below follows from that.

---

## The two platforms are not the same problem

Treating them identically is why neither is moving.

**TikTok does not gate reach on followers.** The For You page will serve a
zero-follower account to thousands of people if the watch-through is there. On
TikTok, followers are a *consequence* of reach, never a cause. So on TikTok you
optimise the video, and followers arrive on their own.

**Instagram does gate reach on followers**, particularly for a young account.
A daily-posting account at zero reads as automated, and Reels distribution
throttles accordingly. On Instagram you have to manufacture the first few hundred
followers by hand, because the algorithm will not give them to you.

**Therefore:** TikTok is a *content* problem. Instagram is a *manual labour*
problem. Do not apply TikTok tactics to Instagram or you will wait forever.

## The five levers, ranked by leverage

### 1. Give people a reason to come back — the series frame

The single highest-value change, and it costs nothing.

Right now: "here is a track about Plato." Complete. Nothing pulls.

Instead, number them and make them a project:

> **#47 of turning every idea in philosophy into a song.**

Same video, same lyric, same visuals. But now the viewer knows there are 46
behind it and more coming, and that they are watching someone attempt something
with a shape. People follow *projects*. They do not follow *posts*.

The catalogue depth that has been a liability — 251 tracks nobody can navigate —
becomes the entire pitch.

**Shipped Aug 25 2026, live from Aug 29.** The line in production is:

> Track 15 of 251 — turning every idea in philosophy into a song.

Written "Track 15" rather than "#15" on purpose: a leading `#15` renders as a
hashtag on Instagram and TikTok, which looks broken and dilutes the tag set.

Two things changed in that caption at once, and it is worth being honest about
why. The hook line — the first thing anyone reads — was a seven-word slice of a
Whisper segment, and most of them landed mid-clause: *"decide or does 👑"*,
*"and strife. Purpose is carved 👑"*. Shipping a series line on top of a broken
first line would have tested nothing, so the hooks were rewritten to complete
lyric lines in the same pass.

That means this is **not a clean A/B**, and at ~40 views a video it could never
have been one. Read it directionally: profile visits per video, Aug 29 – Sep 7
against the two weeks before. See `growth/SERIES-FRAME-TEST.md`.

### 2. Get other people to use the audio

**This is how Bayanihan did 212,000 plays.** Not your posts. Other people's
Facebook Reels using the licensed audio from the library.

That happened by accident and nobody has tried to make it happen again. It is
the highest-ceiling lever available and it is completely unexploited.

What makes a track get adopted as other people's background audio:
- A clean instrumental stretch someone can talk over
- An emotionally unambiguous mood (grief, resolve, awe) that fits a montage
- A hook line people want *attached to their own footage*

On both TikTok and Instagram, every use of your audio is a backlink to your
profile. One creator with 50k followers using a PK track does more than a month
of your own posting.

Concretely: pick the three tracks with the strongest instrumental beds, and cut
a **15-second loopable clip** of each with no vocal over the top. That is the
format people actually reach for.

### 3. Reply to literally everyone

At zero followers, every comment is a person, and a reply from the artist is
memorable in a way it stops being at 10k.

This also feeds the algorithm — comment replies count as engagement on both
platforms, and a thread of replies lifts the post.

The threshold for this to stop being worth it is a long way above where you are.

### 4. Manual following — Instagram only

Already set up: a daily target post arrives at 08:00, you follow 20–25 people
**from the comments**, not the page itself. Commenters follow back at several
times the rate of the accounts they comment under.

Order of priority each day:
1. **Anyone who liked or commented on your posts** — warmest, free, and they've
   already shown intent. Follow within 48 hours or the reciprocity window closes.
2. The daily target post's comment section.

Never automate this. Instagram bans API-driven following and would restrict the
account. Spread follows over hours, not minutes. Unfollow non-reciprocators after
~10 days.

### 5. Fix the first second

Retention data says people watch 15 seconds and leave, regardless of video
length. The 15-second cuts (Aug 24–28) test whether that is a length problem or a
hook problem.

If the 15s versions land near 80% viewed, length was the issue. If they land near
55%, the *opening* is, and the fix is a hook in the first second rather than a
slow atmospheric build.

Already fixed: the mini-movie renderer no longer opens on black. That defect
alone cost The Cave roughly 10–25 points of retention against the older format.

## What to actually do each day

**Automated (already running):** the daily video across five platforms, Reddit
link post, health checks.

**Yours, ~10 minutes:**
- Follow back everyone who liked or commented, plus 20–25 from the daily target
- Reply to every comment
- One genuine comment on someone else's post in the niche

**Yours, weekly:**
- One Substack essay (the drafts are written; you publish)
- Check whether anyone used your audio — search your track names in TikTok's
  sound library

## What to measure, and when

Ignore follower count for the first month. It is noisy and demoralising. Track:

| Metric | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Average % viewed | YouTube/TikTok analytics | The only number that drives reach |
| Profile visits per video | IG/TikTok insights | Measures "reason to follow", the real bottleneck |
| Saves and shares | IG insights | Worth far more than likes for distribution |
| Audio uses by others | TikTok sound page | The Bayanihan lever, currently zero |

**Realistic:** 25 follows/day at a 15–25% follow-back rate is 4–6 followers a
day — roughly 120–180 in the first month on Instagram. TikTok is lumpier: nothing
for weeks, then a video catches and brings several hundred at once.

The point of the first month is not the number. It is getting off zero so the
account stops reading as abandoned, which is what currently suppresses everything
else.

---

## What not to do

**Do not buy followers.** It poisons your engagement rate permanently — the
algorithm measures engagement *as a proportion of followers*, so bought followers
who never watch make every future post perform worse. This is not a moral point,
it is arithmetic.

**Do not chase trending sounds.** It works, and it would destroy the thing that
makes PK worth following. The Wanderer and the teal are the asset. Consistency is
the moat.

**Do not post more than once a day.** At this size, more posts means the same
audience split across more content, and the algorithm reads it as spam.

**Do not automate following, commenting or DMs.** Account restriction costs more
than the followers are worth.
