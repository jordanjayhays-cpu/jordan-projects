# Who to follow — the targeting system

The daily routine sends a target post. This is the reasoning behind it, so you can
self-serve on days the email is thin, and so you can tell a good target from a bad
one in about five seconds.

**The principle:** you are not looking for people who *like philosophy*. You are
looking for people who **demonstrate effort** around it. Effort is the signal that
predicts a follow-back, because someone who writes a two-line comment about Marcus
Aurelius is a person who engages, and a person who engages will look at your profile.

Passive likers scroll. Commenters convert.

---

## The five tiers, in the order you should exhaust them

### Tier 1 — People who already touched PK
**Follow-back rate: very high. Cost: zero. Do these first, always.**

- Anyone who liked one of your posts
- Anyone who commented on one
- Anyone who used your audio in their own Reel or TikTok
- Anyone who followed you (follow back within 48 hours)

They have already shown intent on your content specifically. This is the warmest
list you will ever have and it costs nothing to work.

**Timing matters.** Follow within 48 hours. After a week they have forgotten your
post and a follow from a stranger is just a follow from a stranger.

On most days Tier 1 will not fill your quota. That is what Tiers 2–4 are for.

### Tier 2 — Commenters on adjacent creators' posts
**Follow-back rate: high. This is the main engine.**

Never follow the big account. Follow **the people commenting under it**. A 40k
Stoicism page will not follow back a small artist; the person who left a real
comment on it very well might, and they have proven they care about the subject.

Where to find them — open a recent post from any of these and mine the comments:

| Niche | What to search |
|---|---|
| Stoicism / Marcus Aurelius | `#stoicism` `#dailystoic` `#marcusaurelius` |
| Philosophy generally | `#philosophy` `#philosophyofmind` `#existentialism` |
| Memento mori / mortality | `#mementomori` `#memento_mori` |
| Filipino values | `#kapwa` `#bayanihan` `#filipinovalues` |
| Reading / books | `#bookstagram` `#philosophybooks` `#currentlyreading` |
| Journaling / reflection | `#journaling` `#morningpages` |

Sort by **Recent**, not Top. Top posts have thousands of comments that are mostly
bots and giveaway hunters. Recent posts have twenty real ones.

### Tier 3 — Peer artists at your size
**Follow-back rate: highest of any tier. Also the most useful long term.**

Other small artists making thoughtful, non-mainstream music — lo-fi, ambient,
spoken word, instrumental hip-hop, dark folk. **500 to 5,000 followers.**

They follow back at very high rates because they understand exactly where you are.
They also share, collaborate, and put you on playlists — which none of the other
tiers do.

Find them: `#lofihiphop` `#spokenword` `#ambientmusic` `#independentartist`
`#unsignedartist` `#instrumentalhiphop` — then check they actually make music
rather than just tagging it.

### Tier 4 — Recent posters under your hashtags
**Follow-back rate: moderate. Use to fill the quota.**

Not people who *browse* the hashtag — people who **posted under it in the last few
days**. Recency proves the account is alive and the person is currently thinking
about the subject.

### Tier 5 — Territory targeting, once the basics work
**For revenue rather than reach.**

Your Spotify listeners are Germany, US, Switzerland, Poland, UK. Your Facebook
audience is 91% Philippines and earns a hundredth of a cent per play. If you are
optimising for money rather than numbers, weight Tiers 2–4 toward European and
American accounts. If you are optimising for reach, Filipino accounts convert
faster because the Bayanihan audience already exists there.

Both are valid. Just know which one you are doing on a given day.

## The five-second check before you follow

Open the profile. You are looking for **three green flags**:

1. **They have posts of their own** — any number above zero
2. **Following-to-follower ratio under about 3:1** — 7,000 following / 200 followers is a farmer
3. **Their comments elsewhere vary** — the same emoji on fifty accounts is a bot

If all three pass, follow. If you are unsure, skip — there is an endless supply and
a bad follow costs you ratio.

---

## Who NOT to follow

- **Anyone with "follow for follow" or "F4F" in the bio** — they unfollow in three days and cost you ratio
- **Giveaway comment sections** — pure bots, zero intent
- **Huge accounts** — they will not follow back, ever, and it skews your ratio
- **Empty accounts** — no posts, no profile picture
- **Engagement pods** — you can spot them by identical comment phrasing across many posts
- **Anyone whose content you would be embarrassed to appear next to** — following is public

## Daily allocation

**Instagram, 20–25 follows, spread across the day — never in one burst.**

1. **All of Tier 1** — every liker, commenter and new follower. Usually 0–5 people.
2. **Fill to ~15 from Tier 2** — the day's target post comment section.
3. **~5 from Tier 3** — peer artists. Do these every day; they compound.
4. **Top up from Tier 4** if you are short.

**TikTok, 10–15 follows.** Lower priority — the For You page serves zero-follower
accounts, so following buys you much less there. Tier 1 and Tier 3 only; skip the
rest.

**Unfollow non-reciprocators after ~10 days.** Keeps the ratio from looking
desperate. Instagram shows your following count to everyone who visits.

---

## Why this works

At 25 follows a day into a well-chosen list, expect **15–25% follow-back** — call
it 4–6 real followers a day, 120–180 in the first month.

Following the same 25 people a day chosen at random would get you closer to 5%,
and the ones who did follow back would never listen to anything.

The tiers are ordered by **evidence of intent**. Tier 1 has proven it on your
content. Tier 2 has proven it on the subject. Tier 3 has proven it by doing the
same thing you are doing. Everything below that is guessing, which is why it is
last.
