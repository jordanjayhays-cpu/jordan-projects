# Placewell International: where the money is and which sales motions convert (international recruitment of Filipino workers, as of 2026-11-24)

Scope note: research run 24 Nov 2026 for a BD director at a DMW-licensed Philippine manpower agency targeting EU, Middle East, Australia and Japan employers. Every number is dated. Items older than 2023 are flagged OLD. UNSURE marks figures from a single secondary source or from a search snippet I could not open. Several primary PDFs (DMW compendium tables, Japan ISA tables) were visible only as snippets; where I relied on a secondary write-up of them I say so.

Headline for the next 90 days (details and sources below):
1. The Gulf, which supplied ~52% of 2025 land-based deployment, is partly closed: DMW Advisory No. 10 s. 2026 suspends deployment to Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE "until further notice" after the Feb 2026 US/Israel–Iran war; Jan–Aug 2026 land-based deployment is down 24.68% year on year. Pipeline that assumed Gulf volume needs replacing.
2. The two destinations where Filipino workers are already the #1 or #2 new-permit nationality in Europe are Czechia (29.4% of all 2025 first work permits, #1) and Poland (29,576 permits in 2025, #2). Croatia issued >16,000 permits to Filipinos in 2025 (#4). Yet DMW recorded only 1,113 (Czechia), 964 (Croatia) and 1,493 (Hungary) deployments in 2025, i.e. most Filipinos entering these markets are not going through DMW-licensed PH agencies. That is both the competitive problem and the opening.
3. Japan is the only large destination with a published, rising, multi-year cap: 1,231,900 SSW + Ikusei Shuro intake through FY2028 (cabinet decision 23 Jan 2026), Ikusei Shuro replacing TITP from 1 Apr 2027. Filipinos are ~9.7% of SSW holders. Food service is already capped and closed (13 Apr 2026). There are 3,755 supervising organizations (kumiai) in Japan; they choose sending organizations by track record and Japanese-speaking staff, and sending organizations win them mainly by direct phone calls and Japan liaison presence. The 87-kumiai call list is the right motion for this market.
4. Zero replies from cold email to European employers is not an anomaly: the largest 2025 dataset (Belkins, 7.53M emails) shows 0.45% replies and ~1 booked meeting per ~6,000 emails. Phone outperforms email per contact in Europe (UK: 2.84% positive outcomes per dial vs 0.55% US), and Gong data shows email reply rates roughly double (1.81% → 3.44%) when a call accompanies the email. No staffing-specific benchmark exists; treat all figures as general B2B.

## Key Question 1: Demand map 2026 — which countries and sectors have the largest documented, legally fillable shortages for Filipino workers

### Takeaway
By volume, the Gulf and East/Southeast Asia (UAE, Saudi, Singapore, Hong Kong, Qatar, Taiwan, Kuwait, Japan) are still where deployment happens (2.15M land-based in 2025), but the Gulf is suspended in 2026. Of the markets Placewell targets, Japan has the largest published and growing intake caps; in Europe, Czechia, Poland, Croatia and Italy have the most documented demand that Filipinos legally fill, while the UK (care) and Hungary (guest-worker route) closed in 2025–2026 and Canada is cutting.

### Inferences
- Gulf exposure is the single biggest revenue risk for any PH agency in 2026: Middle East was ~52% of 2025 land-based volume and is now under a DMW suspension of unknown duration. Any 90-day plan should assume zero new Gulf deployments and redeploy BD effort to Asia and Europe.
- The gap between host-country permit counts and DMW deployment counts in Czechia (5,217 permits vs 1,113 DMW deployments), Croatia (>16,000 vs 964) and Poland (29,576 vs not reported) implies that most Filipinos entering these markets are hired via non-DMW channels (third-country hiring from other EU/Gulf states, or unlicensed intermediaries). For a licensed agency this means (a) employers there already know and want Filipino workers, so the "why Filipinos" sell is done, and (b) the pitch must be compliance and reliability versus the grey channel, not awareness.
- Japan is the only Placewell target with a government-published, rising demand ceiling (1.23M through FY2028) and a formal intermediary layer (3,755 kumiai) whose job is to source workers. It is also the only market where the intermediary is legally required (TITP/Ikusei Shuro) and has a known monthly fee stream, so it is the most "sellable" structured B2B market on the list.
- Italy's quota structure routes agriculture (47,000) through farmers' associations and gives preferential quotas to countries with migration agreements; the Philippines is not visibly among the named priority countries. Italy demand is real but channelled via Italian associations and click-day timing, so BD there is calendar-driven (pre-fill windows Oct–Dec, click days Jan–Feb).
- UK care and Hungary's guest-worker route are closed; Canada is shrinking. These should be deprioritised for new-hire volume in the next 90 days.

## Key Question 2: Economics — fees per placement by destination, worker-paid vs employer-paid rules, margins, payment timing

### Takeaway
Philippine law caps worker-paid placement fees at one month's basic salary and bans them entirely for household service workers, seafarers and destinations that prohibit worker fees (UAE, Japan, Poland among them), so for Placewell's targets revenue must come from the employer side. Japan is the only market with a documented recurring fee stream (JPY 20,000–40,000 per worker per month), but it accrues to the Japanese kumiai or registered support organization, not to the Philippine sending agency; the PH agency's own per-worker fee from kumiai or EU employers is not published anywhere I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Where a placement fee is allowed, the ceiling is one month of basic salary in the DMW-approved contract, collectable only after the worker signs that contract; employer/principal shoulders visa, work/residence permits, round-trip airfare, DMW processing fee and OWWA membership — [Respicio & Co., UAE rules](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/are-overseas-recruitment-agencies-allowed-to-charge-placement-fees-dmwpoea-rules-for-uae-deployment); [Respicio, excessive fees](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/where-to-report-excessive-ofw-placement-fees-in-the-philippines)
- Legal basis: RA 8042 as amended by RA 10022; exempt categories: household service workers, seafarers, and workers under bilateral agreements where the host employer covers recruitment costs; DMW regulates under RA 11641 (Feb 2022) — [EDI-Staffbuilders guide, 4 May 2026](https://edistaffbuilders.com/featured/no-placement-fee-recruitment-philippines-guide/)
- UAE: zero worker-paid fee even for high-salary roles — [Respicio & Co.](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/are-overseas-recruitment-agencies-allowed-to-charge-placement-fees-dmwpoea-rules-for-uae-deployment-1)
- Japan: "NO PLACEMENT FEE shall be charged to the worker (Article 6 of the MOC)"; for TITP, Japanese supervising organizations cover costs and any fee collection is deemed illegal; only documented actual costs (OWWA, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, visa, medical, language training if agency-provided) may be passed on with receipts — [Respicio, Japan placement fee limits](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/poea-placement-fee-limits-japan-ofw)
- Poland prohibits employment agencies from charging placement fees; yet Filipino workers reported paying >$11,000 to a PH agency for factory jobs near Warsaw, and PHP 162,000 plus a €1,500 "assurance fee" in another case; the PH agency Balmün was shut down for illegal recruitment to Polish factories — [Al Jazeera, 27 Mar 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2024/3/27/trapped-abandoned-filipino-workers-lured-to-poland-by-shadowy-agents); [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/philippines-recruitment-agency-balm%C3%BCn-philippines-shut-down-for-illegally-recruiting-workers-to-polish-factories-incl-charging-extortionate-fees/)
- Japan TITP supervision fee: "about 40,000 yen per trainee per month" on average, sometimes lower per head for larger cohorts; no statutory upper limit — [JAC Magazine](https://jac-skill.or.jp/en/columns/point/fees-to-registered-support-organizations.php)
- Japanese accepting companies pay the supervising organization roughly JPY 30,000–40,000 per worker per month depending on worker type; trainees pay nothing — [Migrantimes, 18 Dec 2025](https://migrantimes.com/skill-corridor/18-12-2025/behind-the-scenes-of-japans-trainee-corridor-how-supervising-and-sending-organizations-align)
- SSW registered support organization fee: market range JPY 20,000–30,000 per worker per month (ISA requires disclosure, sets no rate); worked example JPY 25,000/month = JPY 300,000/yr = JPY 1.5M per worker over 5 years; direct-hire Gijinkoku carries no monthly fee, only a one-time placement fee (amount not given) — [Kakehashi-X, 6 Jul 2026](https://kakehashi-x.com/en/blogs/direct-hire-vs-agency-which-way-of-hiring-foreign-workers-in-japan-actually-costs-less)
- MWO Tokyo does not charge for accrediting supervising/implementing organizations; processing 10–15 working days; documents include MOJ/MHLW licences, registry certificate, technical training plan, notarized recruitment agreement in DMW template, employment contract on OTIT Form 1-14 — [MWO Tokyo, 17 Mar 2025](https://mwo-tokyo.dmw.gov.ph/index.php/2025/03/17/technical-intern-training-program-titp/)
- Philippine Embassy Warsaw lists only document verification fees (CHF 10 per document, CHF 30 per individual worker) for Polish employers — [Philippine Embassy Warsaw guidelines (PDF)](https://www.warsawpe.dfa.gov.ph/images/announcements/GUIDELINES-Poland.pdf)
- Czechia employer-side cost signals: salary floor ≥1.22× minimum wage (CZK 22,400/month from 1 Jan 2026) for Qualified Employee Programme hires — [Werklist](https://werklist.com/reports/czechia)
- Croatia employer costs: ~16.5% employer health contribution on top of gross wage (sources disagree on whether this is health or pension); minimum gross wage €1,050 from 1 Jan 2026 — [Gloroots](https://www.gloroots.com/hire-employees/hire-employees-in-croatia); [RemotePeople](https://remotepeople.com/countries/croatia/hire-employees/minimum-wage/)
- Generic staffing margins (not international-specific): SIA-attributed temp staffing gross margin ~21% industry-wide, firm range 14–41% — [LevelCFO benchmarks, 2026](https://levelcfo.com/benchmarks/staffing/) (UNSURE: SIA report not reached); Robert Half 2025 audited gross margin 39.0% contract talent, 99.8% permanent placement — via [LevelCFO](https://levelcfo.com/benchmarks/staffing/)
- Generic permanent/international placement fee ranges: 10–35% of first-year salary or flat $5,000–$18,000 per placement — [Nearshore Business Solutions, 2026](https://nearshorebusinesssolutions.com/news/international-recruitment-agencies/) (vendor estimate); contingency 15–25% of first-year salary — [Pin.com](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-agency-commission-structures/) (vendor)
- Worker-fee abuse remains widespread across the sector despite standards: "many migrant workers still pay high recruitment fees and begin their journeys in debt" — [Migration Policy Institute](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/research/promoting-fair-ethical-recruitment)
- Bottom-up Gulf wage context: a PIDS-reposted investigation describes placement fees and illegal loans preying on OFWs — [PIDS repost](https://www.pids.gov.ph/details/news/in-the-news/bloodsuckers-preying-on-aspiring-ofws-make-a-killing-on-placement-fees-illegal-loans)

### Inferences
- For every one of Placewell's four target regions, the compliant revenue model is employer-pays. The one-month-salary worker fee is legally available only in a shrinking set of destinations and is reputationally toxic with the large EU/Japanese employers that have ethical-recruitment policies (see Q6). Pricing conversations should therefore be framed as an employer service fee, not a "placement fee".
- In Japan the recurring money (JPY 300,000–480,000 per worker per year) sits with the kumiai/RSO. A PH sending organization earns a one-off fee per trainee from the kumiai and bears pre-departure training cost. The lever for a PH agency is volume and repeat orders from a few kumiai, not fee size; hence the value of a concentrated kumiai call list.
- Because no public schedule exists for what kumiai pay sending organizations or what EU employers pay PH agencies per blue-collar worker, Placewell's own historical invoices are the only benchmark; the BD director should extract average revenue and cash timing per placement by destination from Placewell's own books before setting targets.
- Payment timing is structurally slow in Europe: Czechia (30-day labour-office test + up to 60–90-day ministry decision) and Italy (click day → 30-day nulla osta → consular visa, up to 12 months under the trial) mean 3–6+ months from signed order to arrival, so fee-on-arrival models create cash gaps; a deposit or milestone structure is the practical response (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No published figure for the fee a Japanese kumiai pays a Philippine sending organization per trainee, nor for pre-departure Japanese-language training cost borne by the sending agency.
- No published euro figure for what Polish, Croatian or Czech employers pay a licensed recruitment agency per Filipino blue-collar worker; only EOR pricing ($599+/month) surfaced, which is a different product.
- No official DMW list of "no-fee" destination countries was retrieved (secondary sources say DMW maintains one).
- No independent data on actual gross margin per placement for PH land-based agencies by destination; the SIA report itself was not accessible.

## Key Question 3: Sales motion evidence — what converts when selling staffing services to employers (2024–2026 data)

### Takeaway
2025 cold-email data shows reply rates of 0.45% (Belkins, strict definition) to 3.43% (Instantly, all replies) and roughly one booked meeting per several thousand emails; phone produces about one meeting per 370 dials overall and performs 1.6–7× better outside the US, with the UK at 2.84% positive outcomes per dial. Combining a call with email roughly doubles email replies (Gong). No staffing-specific or recruitment-agency benchmark exists; all figures are vendor data on general B2B.

### Cited Findings

**Cold email**
- Belkins 2025 study: 7,530,489 cold emails, 34,393 replies, Jan–Dec 2025; average reply rate 0.45% (unique replies ÷ emails sent, excluding auto-replies/bounces); H1 0.50% vs H2 0.40%; founders/owners 0.57%, C-level 0.42%, VP 0.32%; companies with 0–10 employees 0.72% vs 10,000+ 0.22%; construction, financial services, healthcare, legal 0.56–0.60%; by country: Poland 1.43% (46,000 emails), Ireland 0.74%, Denmark 0.73%, Canada 0.63%, US 0.51%, UK 0.48%; Wednesday/Thursday 0.48%; 8 AM–12 PM 0.54%; "over 1,200 appointments" from 7.5M emails; open tracking disabled for deliverability — [Belkins, 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- Instantly 2025 platform data (published 12 Jan 2026): 3.43% average reply rate counting all replies incl. follow-ups; top quartile 5.5%+; top 10% 10.7%+ — [Instantly report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) via [Duckrow comparison](https://duckrow.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rates-2026)
- Saleshandy: 4.5% average reply rate over 30.3M emails (2026 window), top campaigns "2 to 3 meetings for every 100 cold emails" — [Saleshandy](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/) via Duckrow (UNSURE: period stated inconsistently)
- lemlist guidance: 3–5% positive reply rate is "good"; 30–40% of replies converting to meetings is "good" — [lemlist help centre](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/13942005-lemcoach-cold-email-benchmarks-and-metrics-what-good-looks-like)
- Gong (300M+ calls): email reply rate 1.81% with no cold call vs 3.44% when the rep also called the prospect — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls) (undated; UNSURE on year)
- Per-email meeting rates across vendors ~0.3–2%; average ~0.5%, top performers 2–4% on tightly targeted lists — [Frontpipe, 2025](https://www.frontpipe.com/blog/b2b-outbound-benchmarks-2025/) (vendor)

**Cold calling**
- Belkins 2025 calling study: 175,000+ dials; connect rate 9.9% per dial, 24.5% per prospect (≈3 attempts); connect→conversation 58%; conversation→meeting 4.6%; ≈1 meeting per 370 dials; outcomes: hard rejection 31%, warm signal (call-back/follow-up requested) 19%, wrong person/number 13.5%, gatekeeper 13.4%; meeting-booking calls average 3.7 minutes; best day Wednesday (0.95% positive per dial), worst Friday (0.51%); best hours 3–4 PM (1.05–1.22%) and 6 PM (1.47%); meeting rate by month: Feb 8.9%, May 8.5%, Nov 6.5%, Jul 2.7%, Aug 3.0%, Sep 3.2%; UK live rate 14.7% and positive rate 2.84% vs US 9.0%/0.55%; non-US markets 12–16% live rates and 1.6–7× US positive rates; directors produce the highest share of booked meetings; C-level positives were all follow-up requests — [Belkins, 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks)

**LinkedIn and multichannel**
- LinkedIn: connection acceptance 20–40%, positive reply 4–10%, LinkedIn-to-meeting 1–4% — [Driftwood](https://driftwood.sh/cold-outbound-benchmarks); [Buzz.ai](https://buzz.ai/blog/linkedin-automation-cold-outreach-statistics-2026/) (vendor)
- Multichannel lift claims (email + phone +128% response vs email alone; 0.28% vs 0.11% positive reply) appear in vendor posts — [Nousu Collective](https://www.nousucollective.com/blog/cold-calling-vs-email-vs-linkedin-b2b-2026); [Level Up Leads](https://levelupleads.io/blog/b2b-outbound-benchmarks/) (UNSURE; unaudited)

**Speed to lead**
- OLD (2011) but still the only large study: audit of 2,241 US companies; firms that contacted leads within an hour were nearly 7× more likely to qualify them than those that waited even an hour longer, and >60× more likely than those waiting 24h+ — [HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads", Mar 2011](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)
- Workato audit of 114 B2B companies: none called a new inbound lead within 5 minutes; 42% within the hour — [Workato](https://workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study)
- Average B2B lead response time cited as 42 hours — [Kixie](https://www.kixie.com/sales-blog/speed-to-lead-statistics-that-drive-conversions/) (vendor)
- Feb 2026 survey of 500 marketers: very few respond within minutes; recommends <5-minute SLA; no conversion multiplier given — [Neil Patel, Feb 2026](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/response-time-new-leads/)

**Referrals and partnerships**
- Vendor aggregators claim referred B2B leads convert ~26% vs 2–3% for cold outbound, cost $30–50 per lead vs $200+ for outbound, and close 25% faster — [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/); [SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/how-much-b2b-sales-is-on-referral) (UNSURE: figures attributed to Forrester/HubSpot without links; definitions differ)

### Inferences
- Placewell's zero replies from European cold email is statistically expected at low volume: at the Belkins 2025 rate (0.45%), 200 emails yield ~1 reply and ~6,000 emails yield one meeting. Cold email to European employers as currently run is not a pipeline source; it is at best a warm-up touch before a call.
- Per-contact, phone is the higher-yield European channel: Belkins' UK positive rate (2.84% per dial) is 5× its US rate, and non-US markets generally show 1.6–7× US positive rates. For a 87-kumiai list at Belkins' generic funnel (24.5% per-prospect connect × 58% conversation × 4.6% meeting) a single pass would produce under one meeting; but kumiai have a structural need for sending organizations (they are the buyer side of a legally mandated channel), so the generic cold-call funnel is likely a floor. Expect Japanese Golden Week (late Apr–early May), Obon (mid-Aug) and year-end to depress connect rates the way Belkins' July–September trough does.
- Belkins' seniority data (directors book most meetings; C-level mostly asks for follow-up) argues for targeting kumiai operations managers and EU HR/plant managers rather than owners.
- Because 19% of Belkins' calls ended in a requested call-back and the 2011 HBR data shows qualification odds collapse within hours, the cheapest conversion lever available is same-day call-back discipline on every inbound or warm signal, including replies to the Japan call list.
- Seasonality matters for a 90-day plan: Belkins shows Feb (8.9%) and Nov (6.5%) as peak meeting months and Jul–Sep as the trough; a Nov 2026–Feb 2027 push is well timed, and Italy's click days (Jan–Feb 2026 pattern) and Japan's April fiscal-year hiring reinforce it.

### Gaps
- No benchmark for recruitment/staffing agencies selling to employers (Belkins does not report a staffing/HR vertical). All data is generic B2B, mostly software.
- No published conversion data for calls or emails to Japanese cooperatives; the Belkins non-US data is UK/Europe-weighted.
- Referral/partnership conversion figures could not be traced to a primary study; treat as directional only.
- No 2024–2026 replication of the HBR lead-response study was found; the 2011 figure is flagged OLD.

## Key Question 4: Channel partners — how PH agencies actually win EU and Japanese employers

### Takeaway
Japan is won through supervising organizations (kumiai), which sending agencies court by direct phone outreach and Japan liaison offices and which select partners on track record and Japanese-speaking staff; Europe is won through local intermediaries (Foreign Placement Agencies in Poland, licensed Croatian agencies, Italian trade and farmers' associations, Czech programme sponsors) and government-to-government programmes (Germany Triple Win, Czech Qualified Employee Programme, PH–Canada JDI). Direct employer outreach from Manila has the weakest evidence base.

### Cited Findings
- Sending organizations approach kumiai directly, "often making two to three calls a day, sometimes from liaison offices in Japan"; the Nagano cooperative profiled (≈350 trainees) picks partners on experience with Japanese systems, staff Japanese-language ability, understanding of the programme and ethics ("First, we check their track record and the Japanese language ability of their staff"); local staff handle screening and interpreting — [Migrantimes, 18 Dec 2025](https://migrantimes.com/skill-corridor/18-12-2025/behind-the-scenes-of-japans-trainee-corridor-how-supervising-and-sending-organizations-align)
- Japanese kumiai must be registered/accredited with DMW via MWO Tokyo (DO 188-18, MC 18-2018); dual accreditation requires a certificate of no pending welfare cases and proof of prior deployment; MWO verification is free and takes 10–15 working days — [MWO Tokyo, 17 Mar 2025](https://mwo-tokyo.dmw.gov.ph/index.php/2025/03/17/technical-intern-training-program-titp/)
- Universe of Japanese buyers: 3,755 licensed supervising organizations (Mar 2025) — [office-tree.jp citing OTIT](https://office-tree.jp/blog/immigration/kanri-dantai-kyoka-unei-gaibu-kansa/); OTIT publishes the list of approved sending organizations that kumiai consult — [Embassy of Japan in Cambodia (PDF)](https://www.kh.emb-japan.go.jp/files/101009055.pdf)
- Poland: employers must engage an accredited Philippine Recruitment Agency directly or via a Foreign Placement Agency (FPA) — [Philippine Embassy Warsaw (PDF)](https://www.warsawpe.dfa.gov.ph/images/announcements/GUIDELINES-Poland.pdf); "third-country" and "cross-country" hiring that bypasses PH-registered agencies is widespread — [Al Jazeera, 27 Mar 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2024/3/27/trapped-abandoned-filipino-workers-lured-to-poland-by-shadowy-agents)
- Italy: employers file via SPID/CIE "or can work through a trade association or certified professional"; the 47,000 seasonal agriculture quota is routed via farmers' associations — [ILF Law Firm](https://italylawfirms.com/en/italian-decreto-flussi-2026-work-visa-quotas-clickdays/)
- Czechia: Qualified Employee Programme (Ministry of Industry and Trade) with a Manila embassy cap of 11,400 applications/yr from 1 Jul 2026; 10,300 via the programme — [Werklist](https://werklist.com/reports/czechia)
- Croatia: any staffing/EOR provider must hold a Croatian temp-agency licence — [Payoneer, 2026](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/workforce-management/employer-of-record/best-eor-in-croatia/)
- Germany: Triple Win is run by GIZ (language funding) and the Federal Employment Agency (job matching); ~2,500 Filipino nurses by 2025 — [deutschland.de, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.deutschland.de/en/topic/business/skilled-workers-philippines-manila-munich); DMW is also pursuing a Global Skills Partnership route for TESDA NC II caregivers to become nursing assistants in Germany — [SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/manila/dmw-eyes-scholarships-for-pinoy-nurses-in-germany)
- OLD (2022): the private (agency) track to Germany for nurses was about twice the size of Triple Win (4,000 vs 2,000) — [Philippine Embassy Berlin, Jun 2022](https://philippine-embassy.de/2022/06/04/lvp-pr-5-2022-philippine-secretary-of-labor-bello-and-german-federal-health-minister-lauterbach-agree-to-deepen-bilateral-healthcare-cooperation/)
- Australia: IRT's 46 Filipino aged-care arrivals came via the Aged Care Industry Labour Agreement after Philippines-based training and a seven-month training visa — [The Weekly Source, 24 Dec 2025](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/irt-aims-for-112-overseas-staff-this-year-as-46-arrive-from-the-philippines)
- G2G: PH–Canada JDI signed 2 Jul 2026 — [PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1278610); PH–Luxembourg JDI under negotiation Nov 2026 — [Inquirer](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/346278/dmw-ph-luxembourg-eye-labor-pact-to-open-more-jobs-for-filipinos); Taiwan SHPT renewed Nov 2026 — [Filipino Times](https://filipinotimes.net/latest-news/2026/11/07/orkers-3-hours-ago-%C2%B7-the-department-of-migrant-workers-dmw-welcomed-the-signing-of-the-memorandum-of-understanding-mou-between-the-manila-economic-and-cultural-office-meco-and-the-taipei/)
- Hungary: the closed route (5 Jun 2026) was specifically the fast-track guest-worker scheme "usually run through certified manpower agencies"; direct employment residence permits remain open — [Tribune, 21 Jun 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/06/21/envoy-hungary-offers-bigger-opportunity)

### Inferences
- In Japan the buyer is the kumiai, not the end employer; the two things the one documented kumiai said it screens for (track record, Japanese-speaking staff) are exactly what a cold call must demonstrate in the first minute. A call list of 87 is 2.3% of the 3,755 universe; segmenting by sector (construction, nursing care, agriculture, manufacturing, which have open caps) and by whether the kumiai is already DMW-accredited (faster first deployment) should come before dialling.
- In Poland and Croatia the practical partner is the local licensed agency/FPA, which already holds the employer relationship and the permit process; a PH agency selling "compliant DMW-documented supply" to 10–20 FPAs is a shorter path than cold-emailing thousands of factories.
- In Italy, BD is calendar- and association-driven (pre-fill Oct–Dec, click days Jan–Feb), so the right counterpart is an Italian trade association or consulting firm that files click-day applications in bulk, not individual farms or hotels.
- Czechia's programme caps (11,400 Manila applications/yr) make it a quota-race market: employers who want Filipinos need a partner who can file complete applications quickly, which is a sellable service.

### Gaps
- No case study or trade-press account quantifying how any PH agency won EU employers (e.g., share of orders from FPAs vs direct vs G2G).
- No data on how many of the 3,755 kumiai are DMW-accredited or already have a Philippine sending partner.
- No evidence found on recruitment-fair effectiveness for employer acquisition.

## Key Question 5: Pricing and packaging — retainer vs per-placement vs exclusivity; what employers pay for international recruitment

### Takeaway
Industry reports show the global agency-work market placing 61 million people in 2024 on revenues down 4.2%, with Europe weak but stabilising at end-2025; generic fee norms are 15–25% of first-year salary for permanent placements and ~21% gross margin for temp staffing. There is no published evidence specific to retainer vs per-placement pricing for international blue-collar recruitment; Japan's structure (monthly kumiai/RSO fees) is the only documented recurring-revenue model, and it sits with the Japanese intermediary.

### Cited Findings
- Private employment agencies placed 61 million people in jobs in 2024, nearly one million more than 2023, while industry revenues fell 4.2%; hiring caution persisted in Europe and North America — [WEC Industry Impact Report 2026 (PDF)](https://wecglobal.org/uploads/2026/03/Industry-Impact-Report-2026.pdf); [SIA summary](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/private-employment-agencies-placed-61-million-in-jobs-in-2024)
- WEC Economic Report 2025 (2023 data): agency work activity declined globally in 2023; early 2024 "challenged" in Europe and North America, "buoyant" in Asia and South America — [WEC Economic Report 2025](https://wecglobal.org/publication-post/economic-report-2025/)
- WEC Q1 2026 Labour Market Intelligence: "Europe: Activity stabilised at the end of 2025, though growth remains weak and varies significantly by country" — [WEC](https://wecglobal.org/?p=10031) (UNSURE on exact page)
- Temp staffing gross margin ~21% (firm range 14–41%, SIA-attributed); Robert Half 2025: 39.0% GM contract talent, 99.8% permanent placement — [LevelCFO](https://levelcfo.com/benchmarks/staffing/) (UNSURE on SIA citation)
- Contingency permanent fees typically 15–25% of first-year salary — [Pin.com](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-agency-commission-structures/); international agencies 10–35% or $5,000–$18,000 flat — [Nearshore Business Solutions](https://nearshorebusinesssolutions.com/news/international-recruitment-agencies/) (both vendor)
- Japan recurring fees: TITP supervision ~JPY 30,000–40,000/worker/month; SSW support JPY 20,000–30,000/worker/month (example: JPY 1.5M per worker over 5 years) — [JAC](https://jac-skill.or.jp/en/columns/point/fees-to-registered-support-organizations.php); [Kakehashi-X, Jul 2026](https://kakehashi-x.com/en/blogs/direct-hire-vs-agency-which-way-of-hiring-foreign-workers-in-japan-actually-costs-less)
- Process durations that drive payment timing: Czechia 30-day labour-office test + ministry decision within 60 (90) days — [Werklist](https://werklist.com/reports/czechia); Italy nulla osta within 30 days of click day, worker may take up to 12 months to obtain the visa under the trial policy, visa must be used within 6 months — [ILF](https://italylawfirms.com/en/italian-decreto-flussi-2026-work-visa-quotas-clickdays/); Poland fast-track decisions "in as little as four weeks" — [VisasUpdate](https://www.visasupdate.com/post/poland-work-permits-2025-record-251525-non-eu)
- Employer-side wage floors that anchor any salary-percentage fee: Czechia ≥1.22× CZK 22,400 ≈ CZK 27,300/month (2026); Croatia minimum €1,050 gross/month (2026) — [Werklist](https://werklist.com/reports/czechia); [RemotePeople](https://remotepeople.com/countries/croatia/hire-employees/minimum-wage/)

### Inferences
- A 15–25% first-year-salary fee applied to a Croatian or Czech blue-collar wage near the minimum (≈€12,600–€13,500/yr gross in Croatia at €1,050/month; ≈CZK 328,000/yr in Czechia) implies a per-worker fee on the order of €1,900–€3,400 if European permanent-placement norms were applied. This is an arithmetic illustration from cited floors, not an observed market price (UNSURE).
- Given 3–6 month lead times and the employer-pays constraint, the packaging evidence points toward milestone billing (signing deposit, documentation/visa milestone, arrival balance) rather than pure fee-on-arrival, and toward volume commitments (cohort pricing) rather than exclusivity, because employers in Poland/Croatia/Czechia already multi-source nationalities.
- For Japan, a PH agency's revenue is one-off and small relative to the kumiai's recurring stream; the defensible "package" is reliability and compliance (zero worker fees, documented pre-departure Japanese training) that protects the kumiai's own five-year revenue per worker.

### Gaps
- No report (SIA, APSCo, WEC) found that quantifies retainer vs per-placement vs exclusivity outcomes for international recruitment; APSCo material did not surface.
- No published employer-paid price points for Filipino blue-collar placements in any EU country.
- No data on what Gulf employers pay PH agencies per worker (Musaned/Saudi domestic-worker fee schedules not retrieved).

## Key Question 6: Risk and compliance that affect sales — ethical recruitment standards and 2025–2026 regulatory changes

### Takeaway
Employer Pays Principle/IRIS adoption is spreading among large buyers (e.g., ALDI requires it of partners), and Poland's documented fee-abuse cases involving PH agencies make zero-worker-fee compliance a precondition for credibility with EU employers, but there is no quantitative evidence that certification wins contracts. The 2025–2026 regulatory calendar closed the UK care route, Hungary's guest-worker route, Japan's food-service SSW intake and the Gulf (DMW suspension), while opening Czechia (higher cap), Italy (new three-year decree), Japan (1.23M cap, Ikusei Shuro 2027) and partially Canada (Nov 2026 reforms).

### Inferences
- Because the grey channel into Poland/Croatia/Czechia has a documented abuse record, a licensed PH agency's strongest differentiator with mid-size EU employers is auditability: DMW-verified contracts, zero worker fees with receipts, and a named accountable entity. This should lead the pitch, since employers' legal exposure (Poland prohibits agency fees; EPP buyers audit suppliers) is the pain the grey channel creates.
- Large EU and Japanese buyers with EPP/IRIS policies (hospitality groups, retailers' supply chains) are more likely to require employer-pays documentation than to pay a premium for certification; treat IRIS alignment as a qualifier, not a price lever, until evidence appears.
- The regulatory map favours a 90-day focus on Japan (kumiai), Czechia (quota race from Jul 2026), Croatia/Poland (via licensed local agencies), and Italy's 2027 cycle (pre-fill likely Oct–Dec 2026 if the 2026 pattern repeats), while pausing Gulf, UK care and Hungary guest-worker pipelines.
