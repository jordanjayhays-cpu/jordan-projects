# Amigo Sales: outsourced BD / export-representation agency — market, pricing, conversion and fastest path to a first paying client (as of 2026-11-24)

Scope note: research for a one-person outsourced business-development agency based in Madrid (represents small/family firms entering new markets, appoints named local "BD representatives" whose LinkedIn profiles run outreach via HeyReach, runs email + LinkedIn campaigns, hands qualified interest to the client; zero paying clients to date). Every number is dated; anything with data older than 2023 is flagged **[PRE-2023]**. Vendor-published benchmarks are flagged as vendor data. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Key Question 1 — Market and pricing: what outsourced SDR / lead-gen agencies charge, and what the evidence says about commission-only vs hybrid

### Takeaway
In 2026 the published market for outsourced SDR work clusters at roughly $3,000–$14,000/month retainers (low tier $3,000–$5,000 for single-channel or offshore work), $150–$1,500 per booked meeting depending on qualification level, and hybrids of ~$3,000–$8,000 base plus $100–$300 per qualified appointment; Belkins-tier agencies quote $10,000–$15,000+/month. No independent dataset measures commission-only failure rates — only vendor and recruiter opinion exists — but every source agrees pure commission is rare in long-cycle B2B and that retainer+commission hybrids are the established norm.

### Cited Findings

**Retainer ranges (2026, vendor-published)**
- Market range for outsourced SDR programs $3,000–$14,000/month: low tier $3,000–$5,000 (single-channel or offshore), mid $4,000–$8,000 (multi-channel), high $9,000–$14,000 (dedicated team). Leadium's own cold-calling-only program $3,500/month; multi-channel (phone, email, LinkedIn) $4,000–$5,000/month. Freelance appointment setters $2,000–$4,000/month without data or tooling. Article dated 2026-06-15. — [Leadium, Outsourced SDR Cost](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- SalesRoads dedicated team priced from $9,950 per four-week period; SalesHive ~$4,500–$12,000/month (third-party reviews); per-SDR staffing model $4,500–$9,000/month (2026). — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- Another 2026 guide puts retainers at $2,500–$10,000+/month and per-appointment fees at $150–$800+; a third gives ~$4,000–$18,000/month or ~$75–$750 per meeting. — [Intandemly](https://intandemly.com/blogs/outsourced-sdr-cost); [SalesPipe](https://salespipe.co/blog/outsourced-sdr-pricing-models-costs-hidden-fees); [OutboundSalesPro](https://outboundsalespro.com/outsourced-sdr-pricing-2025/)
- Hybrid model example cited: $3,000–$8,000/month base plus $100–$300 per qualified appointment (Revnew, 2026, secondary). — [SalesPipe](https://salespipe.co/blog/outsourced-sdr-pricing-models-costs-hidden-fees)
- Belkins (secondary summaries, conflicting): one summary gives $10,000–$15,000/month for 1–2 dedicated reps, $35,000+ for full teams, pay-per-appointment $1,000–$2,000 per qualified meeting; another gives $2,000–$25,000/month. Treat as UNSURE and verify with a live quote. — [SalesOne compare](https://salesone.now/compare/outsourced-sdr-companies); [Intandemly](https://intandemly.com/blogs/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- CIENCE (as of Oct 2026, secondary): $2,000/month campaign team + $499/month graph8 platform (75,000 credits) + per-SDR fee from $1,750 offshore, $2,500 Europe, $3,500 US; one US SDR therefore ≈ $6,000/month. — [SalesOne compare](https://salesone.now/compare/outsourced-sdr-companies)
- Martal: two higher tiers are flat fee + sales commission after a four-month pilot; no published price found. — [SalesOne compare](https://salesone.now/compare/outsourced-sdr-companies); [Martal comparison page](https://martal.ca/outsourced-sdr-companies-lb/)
- Punch B2B: 90-day rolling contract from $8k/month, first qualified meetings targeted within 30 days (vendor page, 2026). — [Punch](https://www.punchb2b.com/en-us/b2b-sales-development)

**Per-meeting pricing (2026)**
- Loosely qualified appointments $150–$300; ICP-matched meetings $300–$500; verified executive meetings $800–$1,500. Anything below $150/appointment is "almost always junk." — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- Worked cost-per-meeting examples: $4,500 program producing 8 meetings ≈ $562/meeting; 12 meetings ≈ $375/meeting; $3,000 retainer producing 4 meetings = $750/meeting. — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost); [SalesPipe](https://salespipe.co/blog/outsourced-sdr-pricing-models-costs-hidden-fees)

**Contract terms (2026)**
- Leadium and SalesHive month-to-month; quarterly and annual contracts described as the outsourced-sales norm, often auto-renewing; 12-month lock-ins flagged as a red flag; setup/data/tooling fees can lift a $5,000 quote to ~$8,000. — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- Three months is "the common floor" because month one goes to list calibration and messaging (Martal, a competitor, 2026). — [Martal](https://martal.ca/outsourced-sdr-companies-lb/)

**In-house comparison (the agency's pricing anchor)**
- Fully loaded in-house US SDR $140,000–$160,000/productive year ($11,700–$13,300/month); median SDR OTE $85,000 (Leadium, 2026). — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- Bridge Group 2025 survey (351 B2B companies, 78% North America, 83% SaaS): median SDR OTE $80,000 (~$55k base / $25k variable), median quota 10 held meetings/month, 60% of SDRs at quota (lowest in series), 3.0-month ramp, 1.9-year tenure, $3.78M raw pipeline per SDR, median annual attrition 40% (2024 data). — [CIENCE summary of Bridge Group 2025](https://www.cience.com/programmable-revenue/issue-15/sdr-metrics-quota-ramp-tenure/); [Bridge Group](https://blog.bridgegroupinc.com/participate-2025-sdr-research)

**Commission-only: evidence and failure**
- No measured failure-rate statistic found. The only figure is an anecdotal recruiter claim that "something like 99%" of commission-only reps fail to make a livable wage (opinion, undated). — [Navigar Recruiting](https://www.navigarecruiting.com/where-do-you-stand-on-sales-positions-that-offer-no-base-and-are-100-commission/)
- Sources agree on mechanisms: income volatility and high attrition; reps focus on easiest/fastest deals; "most failures come from bad hiring"; commission-only fits independent contractors with defined territories and consistent deal flow. — [Everstage](https://www.everstage.com/sales-compensation/commission-only-sales-compensation-plan); [Everstage commission vs salary](https://www.everstage.com/sales-commission/commission-vs-salary); [SalesDraft](https://salesdraft.io/blog/hire-commission-only-sales-reps-without-headaches)
- "Pure commission exists, but it is rarely the standard in B2B industries where the sales process is long and technical"; established firms move toward hybrids combining retainer + commission (IUCAB blog). — [IUCAB](https://iucab.com/blog/what-is-a-typical-sales-commission-structure-explained/)
- Retainers are usually paid once a rep has proven sales and wants to dedicate more time to the principal. — [SalesCookie](https://blog.salescookie.com/2019/07/16/sales-commission-structures-for-independent-reps/) **[PRE-2023, 2019]**
- "Once your independent reps realize that your products are hard to sell, they may leave"; independent reps command higher commission rates than in-house staff; most work on straight commission of 10–20% of deal value (recruiter guides, 2026). — [SalesDraft where to find agents](https://salesdraft.io/blog/where-to-find-independent-sales-agents); [CommissionPeople](https://commissionpeople.com/independent-sales-rep-guide-hire-top-commission-agents-commissionpeople/)
- CommissionCrowd (self-reported marketing): 33,815 agents and 2,502 companies on live stats (LinkedIn page says 21,000+); claims commission-only reps earn 15–40% per sale plus residuals vs 5–10% one-time for salaried reps; 75% of its agents have 10–30+ years self-employed; 60%+ join with existing networks. No independent verification and no failure-rate data. — [CommissionCrowd](https://www.commissioncrowd.com/); [CommissionCrowd blog](https://blog.commissioncrowd.com/posts/how-to-find-commission-only-salespeople); [CommissionCrowd LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/commission-only-sales-jobs)

### Inferences
- A solo Madrid operator with no brand should price below the agency tier but above freelancer tier: the published freelancer band ($2,000–$4,000/month) and the low-tier agency band ($3,000–$5,000/month) overlap at ~€2,500–€4,000/month; per-meeting at €300–€500 for ICP-matched meetings is defensible against the Leadium bands.
- The in-house anchor is weaker for Amigo's prospects (Mexican, Indian, Turkish SMEs) than for US SaaS; the more relevant anchor is the cost of a local hire in the target market or an export-house commission (see KQ2).
- Pure commission is consistently described as a structure that attracts reps who leave quickly and that principals use when they will not fund proof-of-concept; for Amigo as the agent, every source implies the agent should insist on a base/retainer.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or independent dataset on commission-only deal failure rates; CommissionCrowd, Time to Hire and RepHunter publish no rate or failure statistics that I could find.
- Martal, Cleverly, Pearl Lemon and smaller European agency prices not retrieved from primary pages (only Leadium's own page was fetched directly); Clutch/G2 price bands not pulled.
- No published data on what European (non-US) outsourced SDR agencies charge specifically.

## Key Question 2 — Export representation: how commission agents / export reps are paid, EU Directive 86/653 and Spanish Ley 12/1992, agents vs distributors

### Takeaway
Export agents are typically quoted at 5–15% of sale value (secondary sources, 2026) and manufacturers' reps at 5–7% (OEM), 5–9% (distributor) and 7–14% (end-user) in the only MANA survey data, which dates from 1999–2005 **[PRE-2023]** and MANA itself says has not been updated. Because Jordan operates in Spain, any agency contract he signs as agent falls under Ley 12/1992 (transposing Directive 86/653): he gains a non-waivable right to a goodwill indemnity (indemnización por clientela) capped at one year's average remuneration, which the CJEU's Ingmar ruling extends even to non-EU principals who choose their own law — a real negotiating asset with Mexican, Indian and Turkish principals.

### Cited Findings

**Commission levels**
- Export agent commissions "generally range from 5% to 15% of the export sale value" (2026 guide). — [Pazago](https://blog.pazago.com/post/export-agents)
- Import/export agent fees 3–10% of order value depending on services (2026 guide). — [GoodCan Trading](https://goodcantrading.com/import-export-agent-fees-what-they-charge/)
- MANA commission survey (402 members; surveys 1999, 2002, 2005): OEM 5–7% (avg 6%), distributor 5–9% (avg 7%), end-user low 7% / avg 11% / high 14%. MANA: "Given there is no other commission rate data available since 2005, please use the results accordingly"; rates "appear to be stable over time"; aim is "a reasonable range," not an industry standard. — [MANA commission survey](https://members.manaonline.org/commission-survey) **[PRE-2023 data, page re-posted 2024-11-20]**
- MANA: a beginner agent may make $15,000–$17,000/yr; experienced agent closer to $52,000 (undated). — [MANA personal qualifications](https://members.manaonline.org/news/the-manufacturers%E2%80%99-agent--personal-qualifications) **[undated, treat as PRE-2023]**
- Manufacturing reps "between 7% and 15%" (Salesmate, undated vendor blog); "7 to 15 percent" **[PRE-2023, 2010]** and 5% starting commission **[PRE-2023, 2011]** (Chron). — [Salesmate](https://www.salesmate.io/blog/manufacturers-sales-rep-commission-rates/); [Chron](https://smallbusiness.chron.com/average-manufacturing-commission-sales-10415.html)
- IUCAB: pure commission is rarely standard in long-cycle technical B2B; established firms combine retainer + commission. — [IUCAB](https://iucab.com/blog/what-is-a-typical-sales-commission-structure-explained/)

**Agents vs distributors (government guidance)**
- Distributors buy and resell; agents take no title, are "generally paid a commission on the sales they generate," take orders while the exporter delivers, invoices and collects. — [EDC Canada guide](https://www.edc.ca/content/dam/edc/en/non-premium/ungated/guide/finding-training-managing.pdf)
- US framework (legacy export.gov): a sales representative "usually works for a commission, assumes no risk or responsibility, and is under contract for a definite period"; "agent" may imply authority to bind the company, so the contract must state whether the agent can commit the principal; commission agents generally work on exclusive territories and won't carry competing lines; keep distributors non-exclusive until they prove performance. — [legacy.export.gov Direct Exporting](https://legacy.export.gov/article2?id=Direct-Exporting) **[legacy pages, 2008–2019, PRE-2023]**
- UK government: when to use an agent or distributor. — [great.gov.uk](https://great.gov.uk/advice/define-route-to-market/use-an-agent-or-distributor); Dutch KVK: distributor or commercial agent dilemma. — [KVK](https://www.kvk.nl/en/international/distributor-or-agent-a-dilemma/)

**Legal framework (Spain / EU)**
- Spain transposed Directive 86/653 via Ley 12/1992, de 27 de mayo, sobre Contrato de Agencia (consolidated text). — [BOE Ley 12/1992](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1992-12347); [Directive 86/653 EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1986/653/oj/eng)
- Art. 28 indemnización por clientela: agent must have brought new clients or materially increased business, with continued substantial benefit to the principal; cap = average annual remuneration over the last five years (or the contract period if shorter); right survives agent's death. Art. 29: separate, cumulative damages where early termination of an indefinite contract prevents amortising the agent's costs. Art. 30 exclusions: principal terminates for agent's breach; agent terminates (unless for principal's cause or age/illness); agent assigns with consent. Art. 31: one-year limitation from termination. — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1992-12347); [Rödl & Partner 2025](https://www.roedl.es/es/articulos/blog2025/indemnizacion-clientela-contrato-agencia); [Legia guide 2026](https://legia.es/guias/contrato-de-agencia-mercantil)
- Directive art. 19: parties may not derogate from arts. 17–18 (indemnity/compensation) to the agent's detriment before the contract expires. — [legislation.gov.uk art. 17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1986/653/article/17); [art. 18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1986/653/article/18)
- CJEU Ingmar C-381/98 (2000): arts. 17–18 apply where the agent carries on activity in a Member State even if the principal is in a non-member country and the contract chooses that country's law. — [BAILII Ingmar](https://www.bailii.org/eu/cases/EUECJ/2000/C38198.html) **[PRE-2023 case law, still binding]**
- Spanish case law reportedly extends goodwill indemnity by analogy to distributors/concessionaires (law-firm blog; not verified against judgments). — [Cecamagan](https://www.cecamagan.com/blog/indemnizacion-clientela-asegurar-cobro-al-terminar-relacion-agencia-distribucion)
- English High Court 2008 applied Ley 12/1992 to a Spanish-based agent's compensation claim under the Directive. — [EWHC 2008/1502](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2008/1502) **[PRE-2023]**

### Inferences
- If Amigo acts as a true commercial agent (negotiating sales on behalf of the principal, continuously, for commission), Ley 12/1992 applies by force of law regardless of what a Mexican or Indian principal writes into the contract; this gives Jordan downside protection on termination that a pure "lead-gen agency" contract does not. Conversely, if Amigo only generates and hands over leads (no negotiation of sales), the agency law probably does not apply — the structure should be chosen deliberately (counsel needed).
- Benchmark commission for a representative who both opens the market and negotiates: 5–10% for industrial/OEM goods, up to 10–15% for end-user or low-volume exports; for IT services, commission on first-year contract value is the common analogue (UNSURE — no primary source found for IT-services agent rates).

### Gaps
- No post-2005 MANA commission data exists (MANA states this). No IUCAB statistics on agent economics retrieved.
- No current trade.gov page on commission practice located (only legacy pages). ITC (intracen.org) guidance on agents vs distributors not retrieved.
- No source on typical agent commission for textiles, packaging, fresh produce, gym-equipment maintenance or water infrastructure specifically.

## Key Question 3 — Outbound conversion benchmarks 2024–2026: cold email, LinkedIn, personalization, deliverability, multichannel; sends per qualified meeting

### Takeaway
The honest 2025-data benchmarks are: cold email reply rate 0.45% of sends (Belkins, 7.5M emails, auto-replies excluded) to 3.43% (Instantly, platform-wide, different counting), with Belkins' own data implying roughly one booked appointment per ~6,000 emails; LinkedIn connection acceptance 19–28% and message reply ~10% (Expandi, 13.2M requests, May 2025–Apr 2026), with Belkins' client data showing 1.3% of connected prospects booking a meeting — about one meeting per ~75 connections or ~400 invites. Personalised connection notes lower acceptance (25.3% vs 27.6%) but raise replies 55% (8.2% vs 5.3%); AI-written messages add only ~0.3 points.

### Cited Findings

**Cold email (2025 data, published 2026)**
- Belkins: 7,530,489 cold emails sent for clients in 2025 → 34,393 unique replies = 0.45% reply rate (auto-replies and bounces excluded); >1,200 appointments booked (≈ 1 appointment per ~6,275 emails, my arithmetic). H1 2025 0.50% vs H2 0.40%. Founders/owners 0.57%, C-level 0.42%, VP 0.32%. Companies 0–10 employees 0.72%, 11–50 0.49%, 10,000+ 0.22%. Morning sends (8–12) 0.54%. Food & Beverage 3.47% (top); Construction/Financial/Healthcare/Legal 0.56–0.60%; Banking and Insurance lowest. Poland 1.43%, Ireland 0.74%, Denmark 0.73%, Canada 0.63%, US 0.51%, UK 0.48%. Updated 2026-06-26. — [Belkins cold email response rates](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- Belkins methodology note: "a 5% rate against openers and a 0.45% rate against total sends can describe the same campaign." — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates); [SomethingInc analysis of the 18x disagreement](https://somethinginc.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmark-contradiction/)
- Instantly 2026 Benchmark Report (data 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-18, "billions" of interactions, published 2026-01-12): average reply rate 3.43%; top quartile ≥5.5%; top 10% ≥10.7%; step 1 produces 58% of replies, follow-ups 42%; best sequence 4–7 touches 3–4 days apart; best emails under 80 words; keep bounce rate <2%; stable domain health yields 15–20% more replies; new domains start at 5–10 emails/day and ramp over 4–6 weeks. — [Instantly report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)

- Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (in force since Feb 2024): spam-complaint ceiling 0.3% (Google recommends <0.10%), SPF/DKIM/DMARC, one-click unsubscribe; bulk status at ~5,000 messages/day to Gmail and sticky once crossed. **Sends per qualified meeting (2026, derived from the above)**
- Email: Belkins' 7.5M emails / >1,200 appointments ≈ 6,000+ emails per booked appointment at agency scale (0.45% reply, ~3.5% of replies become appointments). — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- LinkedIn: Belkins' 1.3% of connected prospects booked → ~75 connections or ~400 invites per meeting; at HeyReach median rates (20.75% accept, 18.1% reply-to-acceptance) 400 invites ≈ 83 connections ≈ 15 replies, consistent with ~1–2 meetings if ~7% of replies convert (Belkins' ratio). — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study.md); [HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmark-report-2026)
- Bridge Group 2025: full-time in-house SDR median quota 10 held meetings/month, 60% attainment. — [CIENCE/Bridge Group](https://www.cience.com/programmable-revenue/issue-15/sdr-metrics-quota-ramp-tenure/)

### Inferences
- For one representative's LinkedIn profile at Expandi's 100–150 requests/week starting cap: ~600 invites/month → ~120–170 connections → ~1–2 booked meetings/month on Belkins' client ratios. Promising 10+ meetings/month from one borrowed profile is not supported by any benchmark; 3–6 meetings/month per client needs 3–5 sending profiles or a parallel email channel.
- Email at a solo operator's volume (say 2,000–4,000 sends/month across 2–3 warmed domains) yields roughly 0–1 appointments/month on Belkins' rates, 1–3 at Instantly's top-quartile rates — email alone will not carry a per-meeting promise; it is a supporting channel.
- The seniority/size data (founders 0.57% vs VP 0.32%; 0–10 employees 0.72% vs enterprise 0.22%) favour Amigo's natural market: owner-led SMEs reply 2–3x more than enterprise buyers.
- Country data (Poland 1.43%, Netherlands 10.5% LinkedIn reply) suggests Northern/Eastern European targets for Amigo's clients convert better than US/UK; no Spain or LatAm figure published.

- No vendor publishes a true "sends per qualified meeting" figure; all above are derived.

## Key Question 4 — The "borrowed profile" model: LinkedIn terms, ban risk, misrepresentation, GDPR and Spain's LSSI

### Takeaway
Running HeyReach from a named representative's LinkedIn account breaches LinkedIn's User Agreement on two counts (no account sharing — s.2.2 and s.8.2(1); no bots or unauthorised automation — s.8.2(13)), so the account owner, not Amigo, bears the restriction risk; no vendor publishes a verified restriction rate (the widely-quoted "23% within 90 days" has no traceable methodology). In Spain, LSSI art. 21 bans unsolicited commercial email to companies as well as individuals unless there is a prior contractual relationship, and the AEPD treats this as overriding GDPR legitimate interest — fines run up to €150,000 for serious breaches — so email outreach from Spain must be built around the narrow role-based-address/legitimate-interest reading plus opt-out, or around LinkedIn messaging, which the sources argue (without authority) falls outside art. 21.

### Cited Findings

**LinkedIn User Agreement (current text, fetched 2026-11-24)**
- s.2.2: members agree to "not share or transfer your account or any part of it" and "You will not share your account with anyone else." s.2.1: "Creating an account with false information is a violation of our terms." s.8.2(1): don't "Create a false identity on LinkedIn, misrepresent your identity" or "use or attempt to use another's account (such as sharing log-in credentials or copying cookies)." s.8.2(2): no software/scripts to scrape or copy the Services. s.8.2(13): don't "Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services." — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- LinkedIn help centre: it "does not permit third-party software, including browser plug-ins and extensions, that automates activity" and users "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down"; restriction usually precedes removal; cloud tools are not treated differently from browser tools (secondary summaries, 2026). — [Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/why-linkedin-automation-tools-get-your-account-banned-and-what-to-do-instead); [ConnectSafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/does-linkedin-allow-automation-policy-guide-2026); [LinkedNav](https://www.linkednav.com/blog/will-linkedin-ban-me-for-using-automation)
- The "23% restriction rate within 90 days" figure circulating in 2026 is attributed to "testing across 50 accounts" with no named source or methodology — UNSURE / treat as unverified. — [ConnectSafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/does-linkedin-allow-automation-policy-guide-2026)
- Ghostwriting is not mentioned in the User Agreement; what LinkedIn regulates is who controls the account, whether the profile is a real person, and whether software acts without permission; personal profiles have no native delegation feature, and shared passwords/bots sit in the highest-risk tier; the account owner stays responsible for everything done through the account (vendor guide, 2026). — [MagicPost](https://magicpost.in/blog/is-linkedin-ghostwriting-allowed)
- GDPR treats public LinkedIn profile data as personal data, which covers any contact lists stored (Expandi, 2026). — [Expandi scraping legality](https://expandi.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal/)
- Expandi safety guidance: up to 300 connection requests/week per account; start at 100–150/week. — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/)

**Spain: LSSI, LOPDGDD, AEPD**
- LSSI (Ley 34/2002) art. 21 prohibits advertising/promotional email that the recipient did not previously request or expressly authorise; the recipient can be a natural or legal person; art. 21.2 exception: own similar products/services to an existing customer whose data was lawfully obtained, with free and simple objection at collection and in every message. — [Imisofts, Cold Email Laws in Spain 2026](https://imisofts.com/blog/cold-email-laws-spain/); [En busca del fuego](https://enbuscadelfuego.com/cold-email-legal-espana/)
- The AEPD "has held for years" that LSSI art. 21 overrides the GDPR legitimate-interest basis for sending; the sending act is governed by art. 21 "que no distingue entre B2B y B2C", while processing of contact data is governed by LOPDGDD art. 19 (role-based contact data of employees/sole traders presumed lawful under legitimate interest for B2B contact). — [Rafa Gandia](https://rafagandia.com/blog/cold-email-rgpd-espana/); [Imisofts](https://imisofts.com/blog/cold-email-laws-spain/)
- Fines: minor up to €30,000; serious €30,001–€150,000 for mass sending or insistent/systematic sending to one recipient in breach of art. 21; GDPR ceiling €20M or 4% turnover. — [Imisofts](https://imisofts.com/blog/cold-email-laws-spain/); [Overloop ES](https://overloop.com/es/blog/b2b-cold-email-espana-rgpd-aepd)
- AEPD sanctions cited (vendor compilation, not primary): 2022 telecoms €70,000 (bought list); 2023 Madrid real-estate €30,000 (SMS, legitimate interest rejected); 2023 Vodafone €30,000 (consent revoked); 2024 SME €2,000 (broken unsubscribe link, LSSI art. 22.2). — [Overloop ES](https://overloop.com/es/blog/b2b-cold-email-espana-rgpd-aepd)
- Overloop's practitioner reading (no AEPD citation given): generic addresses (info@, ventas@) are not personal data and need only opt-out; named role-based addresses can be processed under legitimate interest with opt-out; sole traders need consent unless the professional role is proven; personal webmail always needs consent; LinkedIn connection invitations are "not commercial" and "no entra en LSSI art. 21" (unsupported). Compliance checklist: opt-out in every email, process unsubscribes fast, sequence ≤4–5 emails, documented LIA per campaign kept 5 years, lawful data source (no scraping), DPAs with tools, respond to rights requests within 30 days. — [Overloop ES](https://overloop.com/es/blog/b2b-cold-email-espana-rgpd-aepd)
- AEPD closed a Banco Sabadell case because the emails lacked commercial character — illustrating that art. 21 bites only on "comunicaciones comerciales." — [theDPO.eu](https://thedpo.eu/en/decisions/aad8c1d3-5ec0-4983-8820-7dbf5346faa4)
- Cross-country 2026 overview of cold-email legality in 10 European countries (vendor). — [ITSalesaaS](https://itsalesaas.com/blog/is-cold-email-legal-in-europe)

### Inferences
- Structure that reduces (not eliminates) the risk: (a) the representative keeps sole credential control and runs HeyReach themselves under a written mandate, so Amigo never holds the login — this removes the s.2.2/8.2(1) sharing breach but not the s.8.2(13) automation breach; (b) the representative's profile and messages must be truthful about the role ("BD representative for X in Y") so no s.8.2(1) misrepresentation arises; (c) the representative-agency contract should allocate restriction risk and oblige a 100–150 requests/week cap; (d) manual or semi-manual sending via Sales Navigator (LinkedIn's own product) is the only fully compliant route, at lower volume.
- For email from a Spanish entity, the safest executable approach is: company/role addresses only (never personal webmail), a B2B offer relevant to the role, opt-out in every message, a short written LIA per campaign, and no scraped personal emails — and treat email as a secondary channel. If Amigo's client (e.g., the Indian or Mexican firm) is the sender and Amigo only advises, the LSSI exposure shifts, but the AEPD can still pursue the sender if it targets Spanish recipients.
- Spain's LSSI exposure is a reason to position Amigo's own outreach engine toward LinkedIn and warm introductions rather than high-volume email, and to prefer target markets (e.g., UK, Netherlands, Nordics, US) with explicit B2B soft-opt-in or opt-out regimes — see the ITSalesaaS country table.

### Gaps

- No published restriction/ban rate from HeyReach, Expandi or Dripify; HeyReach's own safety limits page not retrieved.
- No legal analysis or case law found on misrepresentation liability for outreach sent "as" a named representative; needs Spanish counsel.
- Whether LinkedIn DMs with a commercial offer count as "comunicación comercial por medios electrónicos equivalentes" under LSSI art. 21 is asserted both ways by vendors and unresolved here — UNSURE.

## Key Question 5 — Client selection: which client types pay outsourced BD agencies and stay, which churn; retention and time-to-first-meeting benchmarks

### Takeaway
The only billing-record dataset found (FirstPageSage, 418 US agencies, ~19,700 client relationships, Mar 2025–Sep 2026) puts lead-generation agencies at 89.1% monthly client retention, 25% of clients still active at 12 months, and a 9.2-month average client lifetime — near the bottom of all agency types — with the churn peak in months 3–6 and sub-$10M-revenue clients the most likely to leave on "budget cuts." Vendor consensus (not independent data) is that outsourced SDR work only pays back when the client has paying customers (product-market fit), a data-defined ICP, ACV above roughly $15,000, and a sales process that can close booked meetings; first meetings typically land in 30–45 days.

### Cited Findings
- FirstPageSage (published 2026-10-30; 418 US agencies, ~19,700 client relationships, anonymised billing records, excludes one-off projects <$5,000 and clients lost to acquisition/bankruptcy): Lead Generation agencies 89.1% monthly CRR, 25.0% retained after 12 months, 9.2-month average lifetime; Marketing Strategy & Consulting 90.3% / 29.4% / 10.3 months; Full-Service Digital 95.2% / 55.4% / 20.8 months; Advertising 96.4% / 64.4% / 27.8 months. Clients of every type most likely to leave in months 3–6; clients under $10M revenue most likely to cite budget cuts, often within six months; enterprise retention higher due to onboarding switching costs; agencies with >200 staff keep clients >2x longer than those with ≤10; "results falling short of expectations" caused nearly 1 in 3 departures; only 12.6% of departing clients moved to a competitor. — [FirstPageSage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/average-customer-retention-rate-for-marketing-agencies)
- Retainer-based agencies keep clients 2.7x longer than project-based (40.4 vs 14.9 months); retainer agencies ~18% annual churn vs ~42% project-based (Focus Digital, Jan–Apr 2026 data; title says "2027"). — [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/); [Disro 2026](https://disro.com/articles/agency-client-retention-churn-statistics)
- Rule of thumb for small retainer agencies: ≤3% monthly logo churn healthy, 4–6% common. — [Hermes calculator](https://www.buildwithhermes.com/tools/client-churn-cost-calculator)
- Vendor readiness criteria for outsourced SDR (2026): product-market fit confirmed by paying customers, ICP defined from real data, ACV above $15,000, a sales process that can close booked meetings; startups fail by starting before PMF, buying the cheapest agency, or expecting results in 30 days. "Do not outsource SDR if your ICP is unproven, your product is pre-revenue, or your ACV is below $15,000 AUD." — [Orrjo](https://orrjo.com/outsourced-sdr-for-startups); [Uplift Sales AU](https://upliftsales.com.au/blog/outsourced-sdr-for-b2b-tech-startups)
- Payback heuristic: one closed deal per quarter should recoup the program; otherwise ACV is too small or the program is underperforming. — [Martal](https://martal.ca/why-outsource-sdrs-and-sales-executives-instead-of-hiring-in-house/)
- Why outsourced SDR programs fail (vendor survey-style): no meetings, or meetings with the wrong people/companies; poorly defined lead qualification. — [DemandZEN](https://demandzen.com/why-outsourced-sdr-programs-fail/); [Delta-v](https://deltav-global.com/the-truth-about-sdr-outsourcing/)
- Time to first meeting: Belkins "first appointments within 30 days" (Martal's comparison) or "30–45 days" (SalesOne); CIENCE launch <14 days; Punch targets first qualified meetings within 30 days on a 90-day contract. — [Martal](https://martal.ca/outsourced-sdr-companies-lb/); [SalesOne](https://salesone.now/compare/outsourced-sdr-companies); [Punch](https://www.punchb2b.com/en-us/b2b-sales-development)
- HeyReach: LinkedIn campaigns under 30 days consistently underperform; acceptance roughly doubles between week 1 and 180+ days (correlational). — [HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmark-report-2026)
- Bridge Group 2025: 3.0-month SDR ramp. — [CIENCE/Bridge Group](https://www.cience.com/programmable-revenue/issue-15/sdr-metrics-quota-ramp-tenure/)
- One lead-gen vendor self-reports 94% annual retention and 12-day average time to first qualified lead (marketing claim, unverified). — [LeadGenJay](https://leadgenjay.com/blog/exclusive-leads-agency-the-2026-playbook-that-actually-works)

### Inferences
- Amigo's current prospect list (pre-revenue-in-market exporters, SMEs likely under $10M revenue) matches the highest-churn profile in the FirstPageSage data; the mitigations the data supports are: price on a retainer (not project), set a 90-day minimum so the month 3–6 churn window is contracted through, and set expectations in writing that month one produces lists and messaging, not meetings.
- Of the named prospects, the water-infrastructure company (OEPI) and the Indian IT firm plausibly have the highest ACV and longest cycles — the profile that sustains retainers; the Mexican gym-maintenance firm and Turkish produce exporter look like lower-ticket, transactional sales where a commission-on-shipments agent model fits better than an SDR retainer (UNSURE — no ACV data on the prospects).
- Expected first-meeting timing to put in a proposal: 30–45 days for first meetings, 90 days before judging the program — consistent across agencies and the Bridge Group ramp.

- No independent study correlates agency client churn with ACV bands or pre-revenue status; the $15k ACV threshold is a vendor heuristic repeated across blogs.
- No European or Spanish agency retention dataset found; FirstPageSage is US-only.

## Key Question 6 — Fastest path to the first paid client: evidence-ranked options and real early-stage examples

### Takeaway
No credible founder write-up of an outbound agency's first three clients was found; what exists is vendor pricing behaviour and generic playbooks. The patterns that are actually in the market in 2026 are: paid 30-day pilots at $500–$1,500, free pilots that bill only after three qualified opportunities, 90-day rolling contracts with a 30-day first-meeting target, and flat-fee-plus-commission after a pilot (Martal). Combined with the retention and conversion data above, the evidence-weighted order is: fixed-fee paid pilot via warm network first; performance pilot with a minimum second; pure commission last.

### Cited Findings
- Generic how-to (2026): start with own network (former colleagues, LinkedIn connections) because warm introductions "convert 5–10x better than cold outreach" (no source given); offer a 30-day pilot at $1,000–$1,500 with a clear deliverable; "most pilots convert to full retainers" (unsupported). — [Sales-Mind](https://sales-mind.ai/en/blog/post/how-to-start-lead-generation-agency-white-label)
- Live pilot structures: a LinkedIn agency charges a $500 paid 30-day pilot before moving to monthly; another runs a free 30-day pilot and charges only after booking three qualified opportunities (vendor pages, 2026). — [Jennavi roundup](https://jennavi.co/insights/best-linkedin-lead-generation-agencies-for-b2b-founders.html); [Tactera pilot](https://tacteradigital.com/pilot/)
- Punch: 90-day rolling contract from $8k/month; first qualified meetings targeted inside 30 days, and it keeps working at no extra cost if that slips. — [Punch](https://www.punchb2b.com/en-us/b2b-sales-development)
- Martal: higher tiers are flat fee + commission after a four-month pilot. — [SalesOne](https://salesone.now/compare/outsourced-sdr-companies)
- Leadium: anything beyond 90 days is a concession the vendor must earn; 12-month lock-ins are a red flag; below $150/appointment is "almost always junk." — [Leadium](https://www.leadium.com/blog/outsourced-sdr-cost)
- Retainer vs project: retainer clients last 2.7x longer (40.4 vs 14.9 months). — [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-marketing-agency-churn/)
- Commission-only: rarely standard in long-cycle B2B; hybrids are the norm; reps leave when products prove hard to sell. — [IUCAB](https://iucab.com/blog/what-is-a-typical-sales-commission-structure-explained/); [SalesDraft](https://salesdraft.io/blog/where-to-find-independent-sales-agents)
- Belkins conversion ratios that constrain any "first 20 meetings" promise: ~1 LinkedIn meeting per ~400 invites; ~1 email appointment per ~6,000 sends. — [Belkins LinkedIn](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study.md); [Belkins email](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- Agency growth survey (2026): client acquisition, pricing and retention statistics across agencies (secondary compilation). — [Shno agency growth statistics](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/agency-growth-statistics)
- Founder anecdotes found were course-sellers' marketing (first client after ~1 month of daily effort) — unverifiable. — [Gumroad example](https://eduardmea.gumroad.com/l/leadgenmastery)

### Inferences (ranked, with the evidence that supports each)
1. **Fixed-fee paid pilot (30–45 days, €1,000–€2,500) sold to warm contacts** — supported by market presence of $500–$1,500 pilots, the 30–45-day first-meeting norm, and FirstPageSage's finding that expectation misses drive a third of churn (a pilot sets the expectation). Zero-budget executable: the deliverable is ICP, list, messaging and the first live sequences.
2. **Performance pilot with a minimum (e.g., €800 base + €300–€500 per ICP-matched meeting, 90 days)** — matches the Revnew-style hybrid band and Leadium's per-meeting tiers; protects Amigo against the 1-in-400 LinkedIn math while giving the client skin in the game.
3. **Productised "first N meetings" package** — only credible at N ≤ 5 over 90 days per sending profile; a "first 20 meetings" promise would require ~8,000 invites or 4–5 profiles plus email, which the benchmarks say a solo operator cannot deliver in 90 days.
4. **Retainer + success fee (commission on closed revenue)** — the structure the market converges on after a pilot (Martal) and the one Ley 12/1992 protects if Amigo negotiates sales; best as the month-4 upgrade, not the opener.
5. **Reseller/referral deals or pure commission** — lowest evidence of working early; every source describes commission-only as the structure that fails for the agent when the product is unproven in the market — which is exactly Amigo's prospects' situation.
- Real "first three clients" examples: none verifiable found; flag to the report writer as a gap rather than invent.

### Gaps
- No verified founder account of an outbound/BD agency's first three paying clients (Indie Hackers / First Round style) retrieved.
- No data on pilot-to-retainer conversion rates for lead-gen agencies.
- No data on referral/reseller arrangements between export agents and SME exporters.

## Key Question 7 — Positioning: does specialising by geography or vertical outperform generalists?

### Takeaway
The best available survey evidence (Promethean Research / The Bureau, 2025 and 2026 editions) says specialists "once again outpaced their generalist counterparts" on revenue growth (84–86% of agencies now self-identify as specialists), while the 2026 edition found service generalists beat service specialists on profitability; cross-industry benchmarks (insurance brokers, financial advisers) show the same growth pattern. The exact size of the gap for outbound agencies is not published, and no study separates geographic from vertical specialisation.

### Cited Findings
- Promethean Research / The Bureau, State of Digital Services 2025: 84% identified as specialists; "once again, the specialists outpaced their generalist counterparts when it came to revenue growth"; overall agency growth 4.6% in 2024; faster growth attributed to the ability to position as niche experts, which eases the sales process; five-year average digital agency growth 12%. — [Promethean 2025 report PDF](https://prometheanresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-Promethean-Research-Digital-Agency-Industry-Report-V1.02.pdf); [PRWeb release](https://www.prweb.com/releases/the-bureau-and-promethean-research-unveil-the-state-of-digital-services-2025-industry-report-302400664.html)
- Promethean 2026 edition: 86% identify as specialists; focused firms again outpaced generalists on revenue growth, but service generalists outperformed service specialists on profitability. — [Promethean 2026 announcement](https://prometheanresearch.kit.com/posts/new-research-2026-state-of-digital-services-report); [Promethean on risks of specialising](https://prometheanresearch.kit.com/posts/the-real-risks-to-specializing-a-digital-agency)
- MarshBerry insurance-agency benchmarking (via Sonant): top performers with defined verticals grow 8–15% organically vs 3–5% for generalists. — [Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/insurance-agency-niche-specialization)
- Kitces 2024 adviser marketing survey (via Capital Turbine): ~58% client growth for niche firms vs 26% non-niche. — [Capital Turbine](https://capitalturbine.com/blog/why-generic-advisor-marketing-loses-to-niche-specialists)
- Weaker/unsourced: retention 78% vs 54%, project profitability 43% vs 28%, YoY growth 32% vs 17% (no dataset identified). — [Businessing Magazine](https://businessingmag.com/23917/strategy/niche-specialization/); niche agencies 40–80% growth vs 15–25% (no source). — [Phantom Leads](https://phantomleads.ai/blog/niche-down-or-stay-general)
- Outreach-side evidence that geography matters: cold-email reply rates by recipient country ranged 0.48% (UK) to 1.43% (Poland); LinkedIn reply 6.0% (US) to 10.5% (Netherlands) in 2025. — [Belkins email](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates); [Belkins LinkedIn](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study.md)
- Outreach-side evidence that sender industry matters: Staffing senders 36.5% acceptance / 18.9% message reply vs Apparel 19.9% / 7.1% (Expandi, May 2025–Apr 2026). — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/)

### Inferences
- The data support specialising for growth and sales ease, with a profitability caveat; for a zero-client solo agency the "eases the sales process" mechanism is what matters most in the next 90 days.
- A geography-plus-corridor niche ("Spanish-speaking market entry for Indian/Turkish/Mexican SMEs" or "Spain/LatAm market entry for one vertical") is more defensible than Amigo's current spread across gym maintenance, IT, textiles, packaging, produce and water infrastructure; the Expandi industry table shows outreach performance itself varies 2–3x by sector, so a mixed book also makes benchmarks and messaging harder to tune.

### Gaps
- No study isolates geographic vs vertical specialisation for sales/BD agencies; Promethean's data are for digital/marketing agencies and the exact growth gap is in a paid report.
- No survey of export-representation or market-entry consultancies on positioning.
- No published outreach benchmark for Spanish, LatAm or Philippine recipients to test a geographic niche quantitatively.
