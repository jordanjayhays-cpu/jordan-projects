## The math that decides this

**Assumption, not a fact from the call: 12-hour shifts, roughly 15 shifts per physician per month**
(standard 7-on/7-off hospitalist rotation). **Confirm this with Veevy before using these numbers —
everything below scales off it.**

Take one hospital client running **3 physicians** continuously:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Shifts per month | 3 × 15 = **45** |
| Hours per month | 45 × 12 = **540** |
| THN's spread | $265–$280 minus ~$200 = **$65–$80/hour** |
| **THN gross margin** | **$35,100 – $43,200 per month** |
| **Jordan at $25/shift** | 45 × $25 = **$1,125 per month** |
| **Jordan's share of the margin** | **2.6% – 3.2%** |
| Jordan's total, capped at 12 months | **$13,500** |
| THN's margin over 3 years on that one client | **$1.26M – $1.56M** |

**You originate a seven-figure annuity and keep 1% of it.**

For comparison, an originating salesperson in healthcare staffing typically holds **10–20% of gross
margin**, and often for the life of the account. *(Industry norm, from general practice — **not**
sourced from a specific survey. Do not quote a figure at Veevy you cannot back.)*

### The 12 months is the real problem

Incompass is six hospitals and ~$2M. Hospital staffing relationships run for years. A 12-month cap
means the moment the account becomes genuinely valuable, you stop being paid for it.

**Duration is worth more than rate.** At $25/shift for the life of the account, that same client is
$13,500 a year, every year — around **$40,500 over three years** instead of $13,500. Tripling the
rate but keeping the 12-month cap only gets you to $40,500 once, and then zero.

Argue duration. Concede on rate if you must.

## Three structures — pick one

### SAFE — flat rate, longer clock
- **$25/shift, unchanged**, but **36 months** instead of 12, and it survives contract renewals.
- **+$30/shift** when you source the physician too, as offered.
- Sourcing costs — domains, Instantly seats, list data — are **THN's expense**, not deducted.
- *Tradeoff:* easiest yes. Leaves money on the table if THN raises hospital rates, because you are
  fixed in dollars while their margin grows.

### BOLD — percentage of gross margin, life of account  ← **recommended**
- **12% of gross margin** on every client you originate, **for the life of the account.**
- Gross margin defined in the contract as *billed rate minus physician cost.* Nothing else deducted.
- At $65–$80/hr spread that is **$7.80–$9.60 per hour**, or **$94–$115 per shift** — roughly four
  times their offer, and it scales automatically when they raise rates.
- Monthly payment, in arrears, with a margin statement per client.
- *Tradeoff:* a real negotiation. They will push back on "life of account" and on seeing their
  margin. Expect to land near 8–10% and 36–60 months.

### WEIRD — you take the risk, and the upside
- **No commission for the first 90 days.** Nothing.
- From day 91: **18% of gross margin, life of account**, plus a **$10K bonus** on the second client.
- *Tradeoff:* you fund three months of your own outreach. In exchange you are a partner, not a
  finder, and 18% becomes arguable because you carried the risk. Only take this if you believe your
  Placewell and Instantly machine genuinely converts.

## On the retainer — your position, stated for the record

You have said the deal is **commission, paid monthly** — not a small retainer that just covers the
machine. That is the right instinct here: a retainer caps you, and THN has margin to share.

**But do not absorb their costs.** Domains at ~$15 each and Instantly seats were discussed as
roughly $100–$300 to set up. That is THN's line item, not yours. Put it in writing: **you supply the
selling, they supply the tooling budget.**

## Terms to put in writing, whichever structure wins

1. **Definition of gross margin** — billed rate minus physician cost. No allocation of overhead,
   recruiting, insurance or admin.
2. **Attribution** — a client is yours if you made first contact, with a **24-month tail**. Stops
   the "we were already talking to them" argument twelve months from now.
3. **Payment** — monthly, within 15 days of THN invoicing the hospital, with a per-client statement.
4. **What happens on non-payment by the hospital** — do you eat it or do they? Decide now, not later.
5. **Exclusivity** — if they want you exclusive to healthcare staffing, that is worth a floor.
   If not, you keep every other Amigo Sales client.
6. **Craig Hospital** — that warm intro is an asset you are bringing. Name it in the agreement so it
   counts as originated by you, whatever happens with the rest.

---

## Top 3 ways this goes wrong

1. **You sign the 12-month cap.** Most likely failure, and it is invisible for a year. Fix: duration
   is the one term you do not concede.
2. **"Gross margin" gets defined loosely** and overhead is allocated against it until your percentage
   means nothing. Fix: the definition in point 1 goes in the contract, in those words.
3. **You send outreach before terms are signed**, build the pipeline, and negotiate from behind.
   Fix: terms first. Your leverage is highest now, while they have 48 physicians on the bench and
   one client.
