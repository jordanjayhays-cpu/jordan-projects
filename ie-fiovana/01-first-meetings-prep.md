## Working hypotheses (to test, not to present as fact)

1. **Procurement/negotiation work is the easiest to productise.** It is repeatable (tenders, benchmarks, contract renegotiation) and ROI is measurable (% saved). BidBox Pro may be the wedge.
2. **The products are mostly accelerators sold inside consulting deals, not standalone SaaS.** If so, the realistic hybrid is "productised services + IP-enabled delivery", not a software company.
3. **Seniority is the moat and the bottleneck.** Growth is capped by partner hours. Products only help if they let one senior serve more clients without diluting quality.
4. **Channel partners (CGI, VASS, Evolutio) could be a distribution route for products.**
5. **AI is the timing argument:** mid-market clients want AI readiness and governance now, and big consultancies are slow and expensive for them.

## Meeting 1: Rafael (Fiovana) — agenda (60 min)

1. **Intros (5 min).** Each teammate: name, background, one line on what they bring to this project.
2. **Rafael's view of the challenge (15 min).** Let him talk. Do not pitch hypotheses yet.
3. **Clarifying questions (25 min).** See list below; pick the top 10.

### Questions for Rafael (ranked)

**Ambition and definition of success**
1. In 3 years, what does "hybrid consulting + digital products" look like? Revenue split you would be happy with?
2. What would make this project a success for you? What decision will you take with our output?
3. What have you already tried on the product side, and what did not work?

**Current business**
4. Rough revenue, headcount and growth over the last 3 years? (Ranges are fine.)
5. Revenue by pillar and by client. Top 3 clients' share?
6. How do you win work today: network, referrals, partners, tenders?

**Products**
7. Of the 15 products on the site, which have paying clients? Which are prototypes or concepts?
8. How are they priced: licence, subscription, bundled into projects?
9. Who builds and maintains them (Pedro's team? outsourced?) and what does that cost?
10. Which product would you bet the company on, and why?

**Constraints and red lines**
11. What must never change? (Seniority, no juniors, personal relationship, independence from vendors?)
12. Investment appetite: bootstrap only, or open to external funding / partners?
13. Hiring plans and the hardest role to fill.

**Market**
14. Who do you lose deals to? Big 4, mid-tier (NTT Data, Minsait), freelancers, in-house?
15. Who do you see as the model to copy (a firm that went consulting to product well)?

**Access**
16. Can we interview 3 to 5 clients and 1 to 2 partners (CGI, VASS)?
17. Can we meet Pedro (DataLens) and Adrián?

## Premortem: top 3 ways the first meetings go wrong

1. **We pitch solutions before understanding the problem.** Rafael is a consultant and professor; he will notice. Fix: hypotheses stay in our notes; meeting 1 is 70% listening.
2. **We leave without a scope and data access.** Fix: last 10 minutes are reserved for scope, data list, next date. Send a written recap within 24h that he confirms.
3. **Five people talk over each other / nobody owns follow-up.** Fix: one facilitator, one note-taker, questions pre-assigned to each person, one sender for all emails.

Also: Rafael is both client and jury member. Treat confidentiality seriously (ask about an NDA), and don't promise deliverables the syllabus doesn't require.
