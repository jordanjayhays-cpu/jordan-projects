# Small-operator venture lens: starting a factoring / invoice-finance operation in the Philippines

Research date: 4 November 2026. All PHP/USD figures are dated inline. Anything marked **ASSUMPTION** is the researcher's modelling input, not a sourced fact. **UNSURE** flags claims that could not be verified from a primary or reliable secondary source.

Scope note: venture economics are Philippines-only. Where non-Philippine benchmarks (US staffing factors, Indonesian P2P, IFA survey) are used as unit-economics inputs they are labelled as such.

## KQ1. Niche candidates and factorability in the Philippines

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence of large, slow-paying, creditworthy debtors is in (a) supermarket/FMCG supply (60–120 day terms are documented) and (e)/(h) government and PhilHealth payers (documented multi-month delays, but with political and legal-enforcement risk). Staffing/manpower subcontractors (b) are a classic factoring niche abroad and have a hard legal payroll obligation in PH that creates demand, but no Philippine evidence of a dedicated payroll-funding product was found. Construction (c) and logistics (g) have documented payment pain but weaker invoice verifiability (pay-when-paid clauses, retentions, disputes).

### Cited Findings

**(a) Supermarket / FMCG suppliers**
- Philippine commentary on retail supplier pressures (Feb 2026): "Most significant were extended payment terms, sometimes stretching from 60 to 120 days", with smaller firms "financing production, logistics and payroll long before receiving payment" — [Newswav / Philippine retail systems thinking](https://newswav.com/article/why-philippine-retail-now-requires-systems-thinking-A2602_DYaOXA)
- The Philippines has no equivalent of Australia's public payment-times reporting, "allowing '60–90 days after the end of month' (and often much longer) to become normalized" — [Newswav / The hidden cost of getting paid late](https://newswav.com/article/the-hidden-cost-of-getting-paid-late-A2602_FCa1xp)
- SMEs on 60–90-day receivable cycles reportedly "spend 30 to 40 percent more on short-term financing" than businesses with structured working capital (opinion column, 2026) — [Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/the-hidden-cost-of-getting-paid-late-A2602_FCa1xp)
- Puregold states it works with "more than 1500 suppliers and trade partners" (2022) — [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2022/12/02/puregolds-panalo-stories-over-25-years)
- Third-party aggregator balances (June 2024): Puregold accounts payable ≈ ₱9.2B; Robinsons Retail Holdings ≈ ₱16.4B — [AlphaSpread (Puregold)](https://alphaspread.com/security/xphs/pgold/financials/balance-sheet/accounts-payable). Payables-days were not disclosed in results found.

**(b) Manpower / staffing / BPO subcontractors**
- Under Philippine labour law the agency is the direct employer and must pay wages on time regardless of client delays; "Philippine courts and the DOLE do not recognize cash-flow shortages as a valid defense" — [Respicio: Is cash flow a valid reason for delayed salary](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/is-cash-flow-a-valid-reason-for-delayed-salary-in-the-philippines); [Respicio: Can an agency withhold salaries if client hasn't paid](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-an-agency-withhold-salaries-if-client-hasnt-paid)
- DOLE D.O. 174 (2017) requires legitimate contractors/subcontractors to have "substantial capital" of ₱5 million (paid-up capital for corporations; net worth for sole proprietors) and DOLE registration with a ₱100,000 fee valid two years — [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/nation/164366-legal-contractualization-still-allowed-new-dole-order); [Emerhub](https://emerhub.com/glossaries/dole-department-order-no-174-d-o-174/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/philippine-labor-contracting-and-subcontracting/)
- US benchmark (not PH): staffing factors advance 85–93% and charge roughly 1.0–2.5% per 30 days (≈12–30% APR); one worked example: $10,000 invoice, 90% advance, 2.19% per 30 days — [Beancount staffing guide, Aug 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/12/staffing-agency-bookkeeping-payroll-funding-factoring-guide); [Advance Partners example quote](https://advance-partners.foleon.com/cost-of-factoring/wp/example-of-a-factoring-quote); [United Capital Source](https://www.unitedcapitalsource.com/blog/factoring-for-staffing-agencies/)
- Philippine-specific payroll-funding / staffing-factoring products: none found in search results (see Gaps).

**(c) Construction subcontractors**
- A DPWH-related subcontract ties subcontractor payment to the contractor's receipt: payment due "within seven (7) days after the check issued by DPWH to CONTRACTOR has already been made good" (pay-when-paid) — [Supreme Court e-library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/4/40415)
- Public-works contracts commonly withhold 10% retention, released three months after final inspection and acceptance; commentary says retention is usually 5–10% — [Respicio: subcontractor remedies](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/legal-remedies-for-subcontractors-facing-non-payment-by-general-contractors)
- Disputes route to CIAC arbitration where the contract has an arbitration clause; late progress-billing payment is a recurring litigated issue (Hanjin v. Dynamic Planners, 2008) — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/construction-contract-payment-dispute-in-the-philippines)

**(e) Government suppliers / contractors**
- Infrastructure spending fell 25% in July 2025 "on delayed DPWH fund release" to contractors — [Manila Bulletin, Sept 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/09/25/infra-spending-fell-25-in-july-on-delayed-dpwh-fund-release)
- DBM attributed a 2026 slowdown to payment claims "undergoing stringent validation procedures" after the flood-control corruption scandal; ₱13.8B of DPWH checks remained uncashed as of end-April 2026 — [Philstar, June 2026](https://www.philstar.com/bansa/2026/06/22/2536824/infrastructure-expenses-decline-anew-april)
- DPWH withheld payments to contractors tied to "ghost" flood-control projects — [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/958152/dpwh-payment-withhold-payment-contractors-flood-control-projects/story)
- DPWH Cordillera workers unpaid for two months amid probe (Oct 2025) — [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/10/30/dpwh-cordillera-workers-unpaid-for-two-months-amid-probe)
- The President ordered prompt payment of "good" contractors (Feb 2026) — [Lacson](https://pinglacson.net/2026/02/03/lacson-presidents-orders-to-pay-performing-contractors-monitor-projects-to-help-avert-infra-crisis/)

**(f) Exporters / USD receivables**
- Acudeen partnered with DS-Concept (Tradewind) in 2018 to offer export factoring to PhilExport members — [Tradewind Finance](https://www.tradewindfinance.com/blog/2018/03/29/acudeen-partners-with-ds-concept-to-support-philexport-members/)
- No current Philippine-based USD-receivables factoring product for freelancers/IT firms was found (Gap).

**(g) Logistics / trucking**
- Portcalls opinion piece: Philippine logistics SMEs face "clients demand[ing] extended credit terms", late/erroneous invoicing delaying payment, and overdue invoices "noticed too late"; no day-counts given — [Portcalls](https://portcalls.com/delayed-payments-blame-poor-financial-management-in-logistics)

**(h) Medical suppliers / PhilHealth claims**
- PhilHealth average claims processing time fell to 23.4 days in 2024 from 41 days in 2018, per its fund-management SVP (Jan 2025) — [ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/news/business/2025/1/10/private-hospitals-appeal-for-pending-philhealth-payments-1749)
- PhilHealth owed government hospitals ₱14.8B in unpaid claims (Sept 2024) — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/metro/2024/09/06/2383279/philhealth-owes-govt-hospitals-p148-billion-unpaid-claims)
- PhilHealth paid ₱195.21B in claims Jan–Aug 2025 — [SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/manila/philhealth-settles-p195b-benefit-claims); hospitals complained of claims difficulty (Mar 2025) — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/nation/2025/03/19/2429394/hospitals-hit-difficulty-processing-philhealth-claims)
- Hospital claims filing window extended to 120 days (Dec 2025) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/12/17/marcos-extends-hospital-claims-filing-to-120-days-amid-philhealth-payment-issues)
- PhilHealth cites incomplete documents / missing forms / late eClaims as causes of delay — [Malaya, Sept 2025](https://malaya.com.ph/news/duterte-to-philhealth-simplify-payment-process)

**Existing PH lenders' sector exclusions (relevant to which niches are underserved)**
- First Circle excludes "financing, investment, gambling and gaming, multilevel marketing, firearms, travel and tours, mining, and car dealerships" and requires ₱1M annual revenue (B2B) / ₱25M (B2C, >1 yr) — [First Circle loan guide, updated 3 Nov 2026](https://www.firstcircle.ph/blog/first-circle-loan-guide)

### Inferences
- Supermarket/FMCG supply is the most "textbook factorable" PH niche: large, listed, solvent debtors (SM, Puregold, Robinsons), documented 60–120 day terms, high invoice regularity. The constraint is debtor cooperation (acknowledgement of assignment, see KQ5) and the fact that First Circle and banks already target revenue ≥₱1M B2B sellers; the gap is likely sub-₱1M-revenue and provincial suppliers.
- Staffing/manpower subcontracting has the sharpest cash-flow mismatch in law (wages must be paid; cash-flow is no defence) and a ₱5M DOLE capital floor that screens out the weakest sellers. Debtors (BPOs, hotels, retailers, developers) are usually creditworthy. Absence of any PH payroll-funding product in results suggests an open niche, but also suggests either low awareness or hidden difficulty (e.g., principals' solidary liability for wages reduces their willingness to pay a third-party assignee). Flag as hypothesis to test with Placewell's network.
- Government/PhilHealth receivables are large and slow but are politically volatile (payments withheld after the 2025 flood-control scandal; validation delays) and sovereign payers generally cannot be bound by assignment notices in practice — suitable only with recourse and seasoned sellers.
- Construction pay-when-paid and retention structures make invoices contingent; only progress billings already certified by the owner are reasonably factorable.
- PhilHealth processing-time claims (23–25 days) conflict with hospital complaints and ₱14.8B arrears; treat the debtor as "slow and disputed" rather than "slow and certain".

### Gaps
- No disclosed supplier payment terms (days payable) for SM Retail, Puregold, Robinsons Retail or 7-Eleven PH were found; requires reading SEC Form 17-A trade-payables notes and computing DPO.
- No Philippine evidence of staffing/payroll factoring products, pricing, or volumes.
- No data on agri aggregator-to-processor payment terms (d) was found.
- No PH-specific data on freelancer/IT USD-receivable financing (f), or on trucking payment terms (g) beyond qualitative commentary.

## KQ2. Unit economics: fees, advance rates, tenors, cost of funds, opex, losses, illustrative P&L at ₱5m / ₱20m / ₱50m books

### Takeaway
Published PH SME-credit pricing clusters at roughly 0.8–3% per month plus 1–4% processing fees, and marketplace invoice discounts of 2–5% per invoice; a small factor pricing at 2.5–3.5% per 30 days on an ~45-day tenor can gross 30–42% p.a. on funds employed, but at a ₱5m book fixed opex eats nearly all of it; the model only clears a meaningful return at ₱20–50m with leverage. All P&L numbers below are illustrative and assumption-labelled.

### Inferences
- At ₱5m the venture is a sweat-equity side business; the economics become a real business only at ₱20m+ with pricing at the upper end (≥3%/30 days) and credit losses held near 3%. This matches First Circle's path (seed-funded, immediately raising debt) and Investree PH's failure once the capital partner withdrew.
- The ₱1.99% processing fee + 0.79%/mo First Circle headline implies an all-in cost of roughly 3–4% for a 45-day draw at the small-SME tier; a new factor pricing at 2.5–3.5% per 30 days is competitive only if it is faster, provincial, or serves sellers First Circle rejects.
- Cost of funds for a solo operator realistically sits at 12–18% (private/family-office money) because there is no evidence of banks lending to unlicensed or newly licensed micro-factors; bank lines (policy rate ~5% + spread) are plausible only after a track record.

## KQ3. Minimum viable structure: unlicensed purchaser, broker/originator models, licensing capital and timeline, moratorium, buying a licensed shell

### Takeaway
Factoring and discounting receivables as a business falls squarely inside the Financing Company Act definition, so a recurring operation needs an SEC Certificate of Authority (₱10M paid-up in Metro Manila; a 2026 SEC circular reportedly raises this to ₱15M for new/expanding FCs — conflicting sources). The 2021–2026 moratorium covered online lending platforms, not FC/LC registration as such, and was lifted 1 Aug 2026. The lowest-capital legal entry is to originate for a licensed lender: First Circle pays ₱10k–₱45k per funded referral and advertises ₱30k–₱325k per client for "Agents".

### Cited Findings

**Licensing scope and capital**
- Factoring/invoice discounting is "typically captured under the regulated business of a financing company, which includes extending credit by discounting or factoring commercial papers or accounts receivable" under RA 8556; financing companies may be 100% foreign-owned since RA 10881 (2016); must be stock corporations; paid-up capital ₱10M / ₱5M / ₱2.5M by location; SEC may raise minimums; five directors required for entities covered by the Revised Code of Corporate Governance (SEC-OGC 24-37) — [NDV Law, June 2026](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)
- A financing company must be organised as a corporation and then "secure the proper authority from the SEC before engaging in financing activities" (Certificate of Authority) — [BusinessTips.ph](https://businesstips.ph/how-to-register-a-financing-company-in-the-philippines)
- Branches in higher-capital locations may require matching that location's minimum (SEC MC 18-2004) — [NDV Law capitalization article](https://ndvlaw.com/sec-capitalization-demands-for-foreign-owned-credit-businesses-and-lending-and-financing-companies-in-the-philippines/)

**2026 moratorium lift and new capital rules (conflicting figures)**
- SEC MC No. 20, Series of 2026 (dated 7 July 2026, effective 1 Aug 2026) lifts the moratorium on new online lending platforms (in place since 5 Nov 2021 under MC 10-2021) and sets prudential/disclosure/market-conduct rules for FCs and LCs — [Conventus Law, 10 July 2026](https://conventuslaw.com/report/philippines-sec-lifts-five-year-moratorium-on-online-lending-platforms-and-issues-new-regulatory-framework/); [Philstar, 12 June 2026](https://www.philstar.com/metro/2026/06/12/2534515/sec-issues-rules-lifting-ban-new-lending-apps)
- Fintechnews.ph on MC 20: FCs need ₱15M paid-up without an OLP, rising to ₱100M for five OLPs; LCs start at ₱5M; existing companies "only need to comply if they expand operations, with one year to do so", or may scale down; max five OLPs per company; single Certificate of Authority per company; OLP operators must register with the Credit Information Corporation — [Fintechnews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/72397/lending/sec-online-lending-platforms-moratorium-lifted/)
- The March 2026 draft proposed ₱20M for FCs without OLPs and ₱10M for LCs — [Crowdfund Insider, Mar 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/266764-philippines-sec-proposes-lifting-moratorium-on-new-online-lending-platforms/); the ₱15M/₱5M figures appear in later coverage — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/sec-lifts-five-year-moratorium-on-online-lending-platforms-and-issues-new-regulatory-framework/1815602). **UNSURE which figure is in the final circular text; verify against SEC MC 20 s.2026 directly.**
- Enforcement during the moratorium: SEC suspended Jia Financing 60 days in July 2025 for launching a website while MC 10-2021 was in force — [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=535710)

## KQ4. Software and operations: factoring software, BIR e-invoicing verification, InstaPay/PESONet, PDCs and BP 22, how PH lenders verify invoices

### Takeaway
Off-the-shelf factoring platforms start at roughly $299/month (Factor Avenue) and are quote-based for FactorFox; BIR's e-invoicing (EIS) becomes mandatory for covered taxpayers only on 31 Dec 2026 and excludes micro taxpayers, so it will not yet give a small factor a universal invoice-verification rail; InstaPay for Business (₱500k per transaction since July 2026) and PESONet (bank-set limits, often ₱250k+) cover disbursement/collection; post-dated cheques backed by BP 22 remain the dominant PH collection-enforcement device, with the criminal penalty now generally a fine.

**Post-dated cheques and BP 22**
- Because BP 22 carries imprisonment, "it is now a common practice for creditors to require their debtors to issue post-dated checks to cover amortizations" (2007) — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/business/2007/01/23/381336/bp-22-defanged)
- BP 22 applies even if the PDC was given as "security" rather than payment; BP 22 cases are bailable; lenders may also file civil collection, small claims, or proceed against collateral — [Respicio: bouncing checks and restructuring](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/liabilities-for-bouncing-checks-and-loan-restructuring-options-in-the-philippines); [Respicio: collecting through PDCs](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/collecting-payment-through-post-dated-checks-in-the-philippines)
- Supreme Court Administrative Circulars 12-2000 and 13-2001 direct fines rather than imprisonment as the rule for BP 22 — [Inquirer: BP 22 after 17 years](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=428258)
- Liability attaches even for guarantee cheques (Llamado v. CA); a stop-payment order on insufficiency grounds does not bar prosecution — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/collecting-payment-through-post-dated-checks-in-the-philippines)
- Small claims: up to ₱1,000,000 via summary procedure; a properly served small-claims case "may be decided within one to three months"; IPA study found mean small-claims duration ≈63 days — [Respicio: how long does a court case take](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/how-long-does-a-court-case-take-in-the-philippines); [IPA study](https://poverty-action.org/study/impacts-judicial-reform-small-claim-procedures-court-congestion-philippines)

**How PH lenders verify**
- First Circle requires government ID, latest AFS or ITR, last 3 months' bank statements, "trading relationships or proof of ongoing/upcoming projects: unpaid purchase orders, invoices, contracts, or official receipts of B2B transactions", corporate documents; underwriting includes "net income, bank revenue, and credit bureau checks" — [First Circle loan guide](https://www.firstcircle.ph/blog/first-circle-loan-guide)
- Acudeen onboarded >400 SMEs in the month after its Dec 2017 launch with RCBC and UnionBank as bank partners; later moved its receivables platform onto blockchain — [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/fintech/fintech-startup-takes-receivables-platform-to-blockchain/)
- Investree PH launched "AI-based credit assessment" and had funded >600 applications — [PIDS](https://pids.gov.ph/details/news/in-the-news/investree-launches-new-ai-based-credit-assessment-solution-for-resilient-smes-seeking-financial-sust)

### Inferences
- First Circle's product is effectively revenue-based SME credit with invoices as supporting evidence, not true factoring with debtor notification/verification; this leaves "debtor-verified" factoring (calling the buyer's AP desk, obtaining acknowledgement) as a differentiator a small operator can execute manually.
- For a ₱5–20m book, a spreadsheet/Airtable ledger plus ₱200k/yr software is sufficient; the real operating cost is field verification and collections labour, not software.
- A PDC from the seller (recourse) remains the cheapest enforcement tool; BP 22 gives leverage through criminal complaint even though prison is rare. Combining PDCs with assignment notices to large debtors is the practical PH stack.

- No pricing found for HPD LendScape, Codix, WinFactor, Finvoice, Lendflow; no open-source factoring ledger identified.
- No evidence on whether BIR EIS data can be queried by third-party financiers for invoice authentication.

## KQ5. Philippines-specific risk catalogue and mitigants

### Takeaway
The documented PH risks are: slow ordinary-court enforcement (1.5–4 years), active SEC conduct enforcement on collections (fines ₱50k–₱1M; licence revocations), sovereign-payer volatility (DPWH payments withheld in 2025–26), platform/partner capital withdrawal (Investree PH), and fraud types that are global but have no PH-specific case law found (double financing, fake invoices). Mitigants in use: PDCs/BP 22, small-claims for ≤₱1M, 30%-of-net-worth exposure cap, debtor concentration limits, with-recourse structures, and notice-of-assignment documentation.

### Cited Findings
- Fraud typologies (global): double financing = "selling the same invoice to multiple factors"; TransCare sold the same invoices to multiple lenders; BHS Electronics submitted falsified invoices to Commerzbank; such fraud "could go unnoticed by a lender for many weeks" — [KlearStack glossary](https://klearstack.com/glossary/invoice-double-financing-fraud); [SFNet top 5 frauds](https://sfnet.com/detail-pages/article-detail/top-5-invoice-finance-frauds); [IFA magazine](https://magazine.factoring.org/magazine-articles/the-hidden-risks-of-fraud-in-factoring-and-invoice-discounting)
- PH: altered digital invoices can be prosecuted as computer-related fraud; one set of acts can trigger multiple offences — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/computer-related-fraud-suing-vendors-who-manipulate-digital-supply-chain-invoices-in-the-philippines/)
- Indonesian parent Investree's licence was revoked amid a fraud probe; PH unit wound down — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/10/231500-indonesia-revokes-license-of-p2p-lending-platform-investree/)
- Concentration: SEC-OGC Opinion 24-26 — total credit to any single borrower ≤30% of net worth; NDV recommends client limits, debtor concentration monitoring, and net-worth planning for anchor programmes — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)
- Government payment risk: DPWH withheld contractor payments after the flood-control scandal; validation delays persisted into April 2026 — [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/958152/dpwh-payment-withhold-payment-contractors-flood-control-projects/story); [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/bansa/2026/06/22/2536824/infrastructure-expenses-decline-anew-april)
- Legal enforcement: ordinary civil collection 1.5–4 years at trial; small claims 1–3 months — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/how-long-does-a-court-case-take-in-the-philippines)
- Collections conduct: SEC MC 18-2019 prohibits threats, obscenities, contacting non-guarantor contacts; fines ₱50k (FC first offence) / ₱100k (second) up to ₱1M; applies to third-party collectors with the FC remaining responsible — [Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/prohibition-on-unfair-debt-collection-practices-of-financing-companies-lending-companies/); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=249677)
- Enforcement examples: FCash Global Lending CA revoked (2020); Surity Cash fined ₱1M; Global Dominion fined ₱50k under MC 18 and the Financial Consumer Protection Act (Feb 2026) — [SEC press release](https://www.sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/2020PressRelease_SEC-revokes-FCash-Global-Lendings-license.pdf); [Fintechnews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/68781/lending/sec-fines-surity-cash-1-million-unfair-debt-collection-practices/); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/corporate/2026/02/11/729705/sec-penalizes-global-dominion-over-debt-collection-practices/?amp)
- Debtor non-acknowledgement / pay-when-paid: DPWH subcontract pays subcontractor only after DPWH's cheque to the main contractor clears — [SC e-library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/4/40415)
- PDC/BP 22 as mitigant: see KQ4 citations (Philstar 2007; Respicio commentaries)
- FX: BSP policy rate 5.00% (Aug 2026); inflation expected above the 4% ceiling in 2026–27 — [ADB](https://asianbondsonline.adb.org/philippines/data.php); [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=595920). BSP FX rules for purchasing USD-denominated receivables: **not researched — UNSURE.**

### Inferences
- Because ordinary litigation is multi-year, PH factors effectively rely on (i) seller recourse via PDCs with BP 22 leverage, (ii) small claims for sub-₱1M tickets, and (iii) debtor cooperation (direct payment to the factor). A small operator should size tickets so that each seller's recourse exposure is enforceable in small claims (≤₱1M) or cover it with PDCs.
- Seller diversion (debtor pays seller, seller keeps cash) is the dominant operational loss channel where debtors will not pay a third party; mitigate with assigned collection accounts or escrow at a partner bank, and only finance sellers whose debtors accept notice of assignment.
- Collections conduct is a reputational and licensing risk even for B2B; MC 18 compliance should be designed in from day one, particularly if third-party collectors are used.

### Gaps
- No Philippine court decision or SEC action specifically on invoice double-financing or fake-invoice fraud against a financier was found.
- No data on frequency of debtor refusal to honour assignments among large PH retailers.
- BSP foreign-exchange rules for NBFIs holding USD receivables not researched.

### Inferences
- A realistic Jan–Jun 2027 plan for a capital-light founder, modelled on these precedents: (1) months 1–2: map 20–30 staffing/FMCG-supplier prospects via Placewell, register as a First Circle referral partner, obtain PH counsel opinion on unlicensed receivables purchase; (2) months 2–4: fund 5–10 hand-picked invoices (≤₱300k each, PDC-backed, debtor-acknowledged) from personal/friends capital while tracking days-to-pay and dilutions; (3) months 4–6: decide between (a) licensed FC with ₱10–15M paid-up and private debt, (b) acquiring a compliant FC via share purchase, or (c) remaining an originator. ASSUMPTION-based plan; no PH source prescribes it.
- The precedent companies needed ~US$1–2.5M equity before any debt and still took 18–24 months to secure credit facilities; a solo founder should not expect bank funding in year one.
