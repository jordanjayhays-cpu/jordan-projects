# Developing-market factoring benchmarks: how factoring and invoice finance scaled (or failed) in markets comparable to the Philippines

Research date: 4 November 2026. All figures are dated inline. Where a figure comes from trade press relaying an association or regulator, that is stated. Items that could not be sourced are listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## Key Question 1: Which comparator markets to keep or drop, and why

### Takeaway
Seven markets are kept as comparators for a small Philippine factoring venture: Mexico, India, Indonesia, Peru, Chile, Colombia and Vietnam, with Turkey retained only as a "regulated independent-factor" reference and Egypt/Africa as a light reference. Brazil, Thailand, Malaysia, Kenya and Nigeria are dropped as primary comparators either because the structural analogy is weak or because no reliable, dated national factoring data could be found.

### Cited Findings
- **Keep: Mexico.** Mid-income, SME-heavy, bank-dominated, with the canonical state-backed reverse-factoring program (NAFIN Cadenas Productivas) and a wave of invoice-finance fintechs. FCI puts Mexican factoring at €31,410 million in 2024 with penetration of ~2.5% of GDP, and only 3% of companies have ever used factoring (ENAFIN 2024) — i.e. a low-penetration market like the Philippines — [Vocero, citing FCI/AMEFAC/ENAFIN](https://www.vocero.com.mx/?p=416760)
- **Keep: India.** Low-to-mid income, informality, weak enforcement, and the most relevant regulator-built platform model (TReDS); four platforms financed about ₹2.38 lakh crore cumulatively by end-May 2025 — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/industry/rxil-msme-invoice-financing-2lakh-crore-fy25)
- **Keep: Indonesia.** Closest ASEAN analogue in income, informality and archipelago geography; it has the region's clearest fintech invoice-finance failure (Investree, licence revoked 21 Oct 2024) — [OJK press release PDF](https://ojk.go.id/en/berita-dan-kegiatan/siaran-pers/Documents/Pages/OJK-Revokes-Business-License-PT-Investree-Radhika-Jaya/SP%20158%20OJK%20REVOKES%20BUSINESS%20LICENSE%20PT%20INVESTREE%20RADHIKA%20JAYA.pdf); [Jakarta Post, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/10/22/ojk-shuts-down-troubled-p2p-lender-investree-amid-alleged-fraud)
- **Keep: Peru.** Mid-income, high informality, and the best-documented example of an invoice registry (CAVALI "factura negociable") turning factoring into a MIPYME product: S/43,069 million negotiated in 2024, +13% — [Forbes Perú, 7 Feb 2025](https://forbes.pe/economia-y-finanzas/2025-02-07/el-volumen-de-facturas-negociables-en-peru-crecio-un-13-en-2024-y-supero-los-s-43-000-millones); [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-desembolsos-factoring-superaron-los-s-43000-millones-2024-1027470.aspx)
- **Keep: Chile.** Higher income than the Philippines, but it is the world's benchmark for how e-invoicing plus a legal "invoice as enforceable title" rule drives factoring penetration (~16% of GDP) — [Vocero](https://www.vocero.com.mx/?p=416760); market >US$50,000 million annually with >40% of SMEs using factoring — [ACHEF/EFA submission to CMF, March 2025 (PDF)](https://cmfchile.cl/portal/principal/613/articles-97244_doc_pdf.pdf)
- **Keep: Colombia.** Mid-income, 2–3% of GDP factoring penetration, and a live experiment in a tax-authority-run invoice registry (RADIAN) — [Vocero](https://www.vocero.com.mx/?p=416760); endorsed invoices on RADIAN grew from COP 11.23 trillion (2022) to COP 46.7 trillion (2025) — [Portafolio, citing DIAN](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/el-factoring-gana-terreno-en-colombia-las-facturas-endosadas-en-radian-suman-37-1-billones-a-julio-de-504996)
- **Keep (light): Vietnam.** Regional, lower-middle income, bank-only factoring under SBV Circular 20/2024 effective 1 July 2024; outstanding factoring only ~VND 16,000 billion against ~VND 17 million billion of total system credit (Aug 2025) — [VNBA / IFC article, 13 Aug 2025](https://vnba.org.vn/en/promoting-factoring-services-in-vietnam-18611.htm). Kept mainly as a "what happens without non-bank factors" counterfactual.
- **Keep (reference only): Turkey.** Upper-middle income with a large, separately licensed independent-factoring sector (49 companies under Law 6361, BDDK-supervised) — [Fiba Faktoring industry page](https://www.fibafaktoring.gricreative.com/en/about-factoring/factoring-industry-in-turkey); factoring = 2.77% of GDP in 2022 — [BCR WFY'23](https://bcrpub.com/news/wfy23-turkish-factoring-sector-accounted-277-cent-turkeys-gdp-2022/). Useful as the model for a non-bank factoring licence, less so for income/legal analogy (civil-law, EU-aligned, hyperinflation economics).
- **Keep (reference only): Egypt / Africa.** FRA-regulated non-bank factoring growing fast from a small base (EGP 12.449 bn in Q1 2024, +16% y/y — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/north-africa/egypt-fra-discloses-non-banking-finance-volume-for-q1-2024-ujvauyt9); EGP 102 bn in first 10 months of 2025, +82.4% — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=842726)). Africa-wide volume €50 bn in 2024 from €21.6 bn in 2017 with ~200 factoring companies — [Nairametrics, citing Afreximbank, Dec 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/12/10/africa-must-scale-factoring-volumes-to-e240-billion-to-boost-smes-afreximbank/). Kept only as evidence that a dedicated non-bank factoring licence plus DFI push can grow a market from near zero.
- **Drop: Brazil.** Its market runs on the "duplicata" (a civil-law negotiable instrument unique to Brazil) and deep FIDC capital-market vehicles; ANFAC estimated factorings and securitisers move >R$150 billion a year (2023 interview) — [Economic News Brasil, Oct 2023](https://economicnewsbrasil.com.br/2023/10/21/presidente-da-anfac-destaca-papel-no-fomento-comercial-brasileiro/). Neither the instrument nor the fund structure exists in the Philippines, and no reliable 2024 ANFAC series was found.
- **Drop: Thailand and Malaysia.** Only fintech-marketing-level data found (e.g. CapBay–OSMEP MoU, March 2025 — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2025/03/09/capbay-partners-with-thailands-osmep-to-expand-sme-financing-solutions)); no national factoring volumes, recourse mix or loss data surfaced. Malaysia's P2P disclosures are useful for fintech unit economics (see KQ3) but not as a market comparator.
- **Drop: Kenya and Nigeria.** No country-level factoring volumes found beyond Afreximbank's continent-wide figures; the Afreximbank push is referenced under Egypt/Africa only.

### Inferences
- The most useful comparators for a *small independent* Philippine factor are Peru and Chile (how a registry/legal title turns invoices into a bankable asset for non-bank factors), Indonesia (how fintech invoice finance fails), and Mexico/India (how anchor-buyer programs open SME access). Turkey and Egypt are the regulatory templates for a non-bank licence.

### Gaps
- No FCI country-by-country table for 2024 or 2025 was retrievable in open sources; FCI's own statistics pages and the EUF tables were not accessible, so per-country FCI penetration figures for Peru, Colombia, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and the Philippines are missing.

## Key Question 2: Market profiles for each kept market (size, growth, model, recourse mix, regulation trigger, players, loss experience)

### Takeaway
Across the kept markets, the growth unlock was never the factoring law alone; it was the combination of (a) a legal rule making the invoice an enforceable, transferable title, (b) a central invoice/e-invoice registry that prevents double financing, and (c) a channel (anchor buyer platform or non-bank licence) that lets non-banks finance SME receivables. Markets without (a)+(b) — Vietnam, and historically Indonesia's fintechs — stayed bank-dominated or produced fraud-driven failures.

### Inferences
- The two mid-income markets where non-bank factors own a large share of *count* (Peru: 71% of registered financiers are factoring companies; Chile: EFA non-bank segment of ~US$1 bn placements vs a ~US$55 bn total) are both markets where an invoice registry or legal-title rule exists. In both, banks still take most *volume* (Peru: 10% of entities = 73% of volume), so a small independent should expect to be a niche, small-ticket player even in a well-plumbed market.
- The Chilean non-bank sector's 10.18% weighted arrears with 59% under 30 days (2025) suggests that late payment, not write-off, is the normal operating condition for small factors serving SMEs; pricing and liquidity buffers must assume it.
- Peru's 27% growth in sub-S/10,000 invoices and Colombia's 1.77 million endorsed invoices (2025) show registries pull ticket sizes down toward micro-suppliers, which is exactly the segment a small Philippine factor would target.
- Vietnam shows that a bank-only factoring circular, without an invoice registry or non-bank licence, leaves factoring at ~0.1% of credit even in a fast-growing economy.

### Gaps
- Recourse vs non-recourse shares: no dated, sourced split was found for any kept market. FCI publishes this for members but the tables were not accessible. The Chilean EFA report, Peru CAVALI data and TReDS reports do not report it (TReDS is structurally non-recourse to the MSME since the financier takes buyer risk, but no source stating a share was found).
- Number of factors in Chile is unknown by the industry's own admission (no registry, CMF submission 2025).
- Loss/fraud experience: no sourced write-off rates for Peru, Colombia, Mexico, Turkey or Egypt; the only quantified loss metrics are Chile's EFA arrears and Indonesia's TWP90.
- TReDS average discount rates: only an upper bound (<18%) and the bidding mechanism are sourced; no platform-published average was found.
- The Klapper (2005) full text (NAFIN buyer/SME counts, pricing, loss experience) returned HTTP 403; only the abstract is cited.
- The KoinWorks article body returned HTTP 429; only the headline (~US$37 m) is cited and the mechanism is unverified.

## Key Question 3: Fintech lessons — who succeeded, who failed, why, and what unit economics they reported

### Takeaway
The invoice-finance fintechs that survived (Funding Societies, Drip Capital, Xepelin) kept cohort defaults in the low single digits, diversified into other SME products, and financed themselves with bank/DFI warehouse lines rather than retail P2P money; the ones that failed (Investree, Acudeen's disappearance from the record, KoinWorks' parent) failed on governance and capital, not on demand.

### Cited Findings
- **Funding Societies / Modalku (success case):** US$3 bn cumulative disbursed across >5 million transactions to almost 100,000 SMEs by April 2023 (implied ~US$600 per transaction, my arithmetic); platform cohort default rate below 2%; markets Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam; US$50 m credit line from HSBC — [FF News, 18 Apr 2023](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/funding-societies-crosses-us3-billion-in-sme-lending)
- Funding Societies Malaysia default rate 1.04% per its website, below a 3–5% long-term target; invoice-financing notes are classed as default at 60 days late (90 for term notes); one note issuer's stated probability of default was as high as 15.47% — [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/article/cover-story-how-p2p-financing-platforms-manage-defaults)
- Modalku reported it had pushed default to 0.8% after three years in Indonesia (2019; dated, included only as historical) — [Kontan, 2019](https://keuangan.kontan.co.id/news/tiga-tahun-beroperasi-di-indonesia-modalku-tekan-default-08)
- **Drip Capital (success case):** Sept 2024 raise of US$113 m: US$23 m equity (GMO Payment Gateway, SMBC) and US$90 m debt led by IFC and East West Bank — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/09/drip-capital-secures-113-million-funding-expand-trade-finance); earlier US$135 m warehouse debt including a US$100 m Barclays facility and US$35 m East West Bank increase — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/drip-capital-secures-113mm-in-new-funding-to-accelerate-growth/); first committed warehouse was US$40 m from East-West Bancorp (2021) — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=173620); Mexico products include export factoring, domestic factoring, supplier financing and invoice discounting — [Moge.ai profile](https://moge.ai/product/drip-capital)
- **Xepelin (success case):** US$30 m Series A (2021, Kaszek/DST), US$111 m Series B (2022, Kaszek/Avenir), US$148 m debt led by Goldman Sachs (2022) — [Expansión MX via VC MX newsletter](https://venturecapitalmx.beehiiv.com/p/expansion-mx-xepelin); US$140 m Goldman facility for Mexico expansion — [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/fintech-xepelin-gets-140-from-goldman-sachs-to-finance-a-mexico-expansion/); US$15.75 m line from BBVA Spark — [LatamList](https://latamlist.com/?p=17152); >70,000 clients, >US$7 bn financed since 2019, >US$300 m raised (Dec 2025) — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/xepelin-raises-300m-for-latin-american-smes)
- **Mundi (Mexico):** offers up to US$10 m non-recourse export factoring; competitors Xepelin, Marco, Factoro — [Mundi](https://mundi.io/en/factoraje-proveedores); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/mundi-1/alternatives-competitors)
- **Investree (failure case):** licence revoked 21 Oct 2024 for failing the Rp 7.5 bn (~US$480k) minimum equity after sanctions; founder banned and under criminal scrutiny; had closed a Series D with JTA (Qatar) only a year earlier (4 Oct 2023) — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/10/22/ojk-shuts-down-troubled-p2p-lender-investree-amid-alleged-fraud); Kapronasia flagged "the end may be near" ahead of revocation — [Kapronasia](https://kapronasia.com/insight/blogs/fintech-research/the-end-may-be-near-for-investree)
- **KoinWorks parent (failure/fraud case):** three top executives arrested over ~US$37 m alleged credit corruption — [Investortrust headline](https://investortrust.id/market/102555/fintech-fraud-scandal-top-koinworks-executives-arrested-over-37-million-credit-corruption)
- **Acudeen (Philippines, status unknown):** bought invoices at a 2% per month discount (2017) — [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/manila-startup-acudeen-wins-500k-at-seedstars-world-2017); financed >₱500 m of invoices for 1,000 SMEs in 2018 and earmarked ₱5 bn for 2019 — [Philstar, Dec 2018](https://www.philstar.com/business/2018/12/10/1875605/acudeen-earmarks-p5-b-sme-financing-2019); had helped >800 MSMEs and transacted >US$8 m over two years per a UnionBank GlobalLinker partnership post — [UnionBank GlobalLinker](https://unionbank.globallinker.com/bizforum/article/acudeen-partners-up-with-unionbank-globallinker-to-help-millions-of-msmes-nationwide/21274); was on track for US$30 m of invoices in a year after US$3 m the prior year — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=138859). No source after ~2019–2020 was found; no report of closure, sale or regulatory action surfaced.
- **Fraud baseline:** MonetaGo's CEO estimates 1–2% of invoice financing globally is subject to fraud; its registry creates a digital fingerprint of invoices to prevent duplicate financing — [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/anti-fraud-blockchain-for-invoice-financing-goes-live); [Financial IT](https://financialit.net/node/33180)

### Inferences
- Survivors report cohort defaults of 1–2% while the Indonesian productive-lending segment runs TWP90 of ~3% industry-wide with many platforms over 5%; the dispersion, not the average, is the lesson. A small factor's book will look like a single P2P platform, not the industry average.
- Investree's Rp 7.5 bn equity shortfall is tiny relative to its Series D; the failure was governance (founder conduct) and loss of funder confidence, which implies that for a small factor the credible-governance signal (independent credit committee, audited book, bank-grade KYC) is worth more than fundraising headlines.
- Acudeen's 2%/month discount (≈24%+ annualised) with ~US$8 m volume over two years suggests Philippine demand existed at high prices but scale did not follow; the venture should assume Acudeen-era pricing is feasible but that ticket size, funder trust and buyer verification — not pricing — are the binding constraints.

### Gaps
- No fintech in the set publishes yields, take rates, average ticket, tenor and loss rates together; Xepelin, Drip and Mundi disclose nothing on profitability or losses in open sources.
- Why Acudeen stopped appearing in the record after ~2020 could not be determined; the Philippine SEC registry would need to be checked.
- Investree's loan book, outstanding to lenders and TWP90 at revocation were behind paywalls (DealStreetAsia) and are not cited.
- No sourced data on Vayana or KredX losses or regulatory action.

## Key Question 4: Funding models for small factors — what worked at small scale

### Takeaway
Across the comparators, the funding ladder for independent factors ran: founder equity and retail/P2P money at the start, then bank warehouse lines, then DFI-led debt (IFC) once the book had a track record; P2P retail funding proved fragile (Indonesia), while bank/DFI warehouse lines were what let Drip, Xepelin and Funding Societies scale.

### Cited Findings
- Drip Capital's progression: US$40 m committed warehouse from East-West Bancorp (2021) — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=173620) → US$100 m Barclays + US$35 m East West (US$135 m total) — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/drip-capital-secures-113mm-in-new-funding-to-accelerate-growth/) → US$90 m debt led by IFC and East West Bank with US$23 m equity (Sept 2024) — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/09/drip-capital-secures-113-million-funding-expand-trade-finance)
- Xepelin's debt stack: US$148 m Goldman-led debt (2022) — [VC MX newsletter](https://venturecapitalmx.beehiiv.com/p/expansion-mx-xepelin); US$140 m Goldman facility for Mexico — [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/fintech-xepelin-gets-140-from-goldman-sachs-to-finance-a-mexico-expansion/); US$15.75 m BBVA Spark line — [LatamList](https://latamlist.com/?p=17152)
- Funding Societies' first global-bank line: US$50 m from HSBC (2023) — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/funding-societies-crosses-us3-billion-in-sme-lending)
- IFC structures: with Partners for Growth, IFC co-invests in tailored debt facilities for growth-stage companies in emerging markets covering working capital and fintech warehouse needs — [IFC press release, May 2022](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2022/ifc-partners-for-growth-join-forces-to-support-growth-stage-companies-in-emerging-markets); in its C2FO supply-chain facility (initially Nigeria) IFC initially funds all invoice purchases itself and expects to syndicate to private banks later — [IFC C2FO case (PDF)](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2025/c2fo-connecting-smes-with-equitable-access-to-working-capital.pdf)
- Mexico: eFactor Network (an independent Mexican factor) consolidated international funding (Sept 2025) — [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/monterrey/2025/09/15/consolida-efactor-network-fondeo-internacional/)
- Peru: regulated financial-system entities (10% of CAVALI financiers) did 73% of 2024 volume, i.e. independent factors with their own/limited funding did 27% across 92 or so firms — [Forbes Perú](https://forbes.pe/economia-y-finanzas/2025-02-07/el-volumen-de-facturas-negociables-en-peru-crecio-un-13-en-2024-y-supero-los-s-43-000-millones)
- Turkey: 49 licensed factoring companies with 365 branches — [Fiba Faktoring](https://www.fibafaktoring.gricreative.com/en/about-factoring/factoring-industry-in-turkey) (funding mix not retrievable; see Gaps)
- Indonesia: retail P2P-funded invoice lending produced the Investree collapse once equity fell below the Rp 7.5 bn minimum — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/10/22/ojk-shuts-down-troubled-p2p-lender-investree-amid-alleged-fraud)
- Chile's non-bank factors (EFA): ~US$1,050 m of placements in 2025 funded as non-bank "empresas de servicios financieros" — [Chócale](https://chocale.cl/2026/05/factoring-no-bancario-crece-casi-14-en-2025-y-supera-los-us-5-000-millones/); Factotal, a mid-sized Chilean non-bank factor, is rated and reports publicly (April 2025 annual report) — [Factotal rating report (PDF)](https://ws-clasificadora.azurewebsites.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Informe-Factotal-Abril-2025-Anual.pdf)

### Inferences
- The first institutional warehouse line in every success case was US$15–50 m and came from a bank (East West, HSBC, BBVA Spark), not a DFI; IFC entered only at the US$90 m+ stage with a multi-year track record. A small Philippine factor should plan for 2–3 years on equity plus a local bank line before DFI money is realistic.
- Peru's structure (many small factoring companies registered at CAVALI, sharing 27% of volume) shows that an invoice registry lets small, self-funded factors operate legally alongside banks; without such a registry, small factors have no cheap way to prove title to funders.
- Retail P2P funding is the cheapest to start but the regulator's equity rules and the fragility of lender confidence (Investree) make it the most dangerous way to fund an invoice book.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on securitisation of factored receivables at small scale in any kept market (Brazil's FIDC is the model but was dropped; no 2024 figures found).
- No sourced funding-mix (bank borrowing vs bonds vs equity) for Turkish factoring companies in 2024.
- No FMO, BII, DEG or ADB facility to an invoice-finance fintech in the kept markets was found in this search.

## Key Question 5: The anchor-buyer (reverse factoring) playbook

### Takeaway
Reverse factoring around large buyers is what opened SME access in Mexico (NAFIN), India (TReDS) and increasingly Chile (confirming); the common ingredients are an electronic platform where the buyer confirms the invoice, pricing keyed to the buyer's credit, multiple financiers bidding, and a regulator or state bank as sponsor. An independent can run one only by owning the buyer relationship and the confirmation workflow.

### Cited Findings
- NAFIN's Cadenas Productivas is a state development bank program based on reverse factoring in which MSME invoices are discounted electronically — [RBI concept paper](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/concept-paper-on-trade-receivables-and-credit-exchange-for-financing-of-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-225)
- Klapper (2005): NAFIN reverse factoring shows how electronic channels and a supportive legal and regulatory environment can cut costs and widen SME services; reverse factoring mitigates borrower opacity where information infrastructure is weak — [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wbk:wbrwps:3593)
- NAFIN scale: average 18,400 companies/year supported 2019–2022, 95% MiPyMEs — [Nafin/Bancomext bulletin (PDF)](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/834883/Bolet_n_Nafin_y_Bancomext_-_D_a_Internacional_MiPymes.pdf); portfolio shrank from MXN 46,045 m (2021) to MXN 30,987 m (June 2024) — [Nafin 2021 (PDF)](https://www.nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/Acerca_de_Nafin/logros_resultados/Nafin_2021_.pdf); [Nafin July 2024 (PDF)](https://www.nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/Acerca_de_Nafin/logros_resultados/Nafin_JUL_2024_V1.4.pdf)
- TReDS mechanics: financiers bid competitively; pricing depends on the buyer's credit profile, tenor and market conditions; buyer or seller may bear the cost — [SMFG India Credit](https://www.smfgindiacredit.com/knowledge-center/trade-receivables-electronic-discounting-system-treds.aspx); [M1xchange explainer](https://www.m1xchange.com/treds/)
- TReDS scale: ₹1.38 lakh crore / 41.6 lakh invoices in FY24 — [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=75105); ~₹2.38 lakh crore cumulative across four platforms by end-May 2025; RXIL alone ₹80,500 crore in FY25 with corporate and government buyers joining — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/industry/rxil-msme-invoice-financing-2lakh-crore-fy25)
- A buyer-onboarding mandate exists in India (a ₹250 crore turnover threshold appears in related coverage) — [Outlook Business related-story link](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/industry/rxil-msme-invoice-financing-2lakh-crore-fy25) (threshold detail not verified in body text; see Gaps)
- Chile: confirming (buyer-led reverse factoring) is "gaining ground" in a US$55.3 bn market (2025) — [BCR WFY'26 teaser](https://bcrpub.com/news/wfy25-chilean-factoring-faced-significant-challenges-in-2024/)
- Colombia: Bancolombia and BBVA market RADIAN-based factoring/confirming for suppliers — [Bancolombia blog](https://blog.bancolombia.com/negocios/radian-colombia/); [BBVA](https://www.bbva.com/es/empresas/factoring-electronico-en-radian-como-convertir-tus-facturas-en-liquidez-en-solo-unas-horas/)
- IFC–C2FO Africa supply-chain finance facility: IFC funds invoice purchases from local suppliers of large buyers, starting in Nigeria — [IFC (PDF)](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2025/c2fo-connecting-smes-with-equitable-access-to-working-capital.pdf)
- Thailand: CapBay signed an MoU with OSMEP (March 2025) to deliver supply-chain finance to SMEs — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2025/03/09/capbay-partners-with-thailands-osmep-to-expand-sme-financing-solutions)

### Inferences
- Every scaled program had a sponsor that could compel or strongly nudge buyer participation (NAFIN as state bank; RBI/MSME ministry for TReDS). An independent Philippine factor cannot compel buyers, so the practical version is "mini-reverse-factoring": sign 2–5 mid-size anchor buyers (distributors, contractors, BPO vendors) who will confirm invoices on the factor's portal in exchange for longer payment terms or supplier stability.
- NAFIN's portfolio decline (-33% 2021→mid-2024) and TReDS' still-small share of Indian MSME credit suggest anchor programs plateau once the "easy" large-corporate supply chains are covered; the independent's opportunity is in the second tier of buyers the platforms do not reach.
- Pricing off the buyer's credit (TReDS <18% ceiling observed; competitive bids) means an independent running an anchor program competes with bank cost of funds; its edge must be speed, small tickets and buyer-specific workflow, not price.

### Gaps
- NAFIN pricing, number of participating banks and buyers, and loss experience (in the Klapper full text) could not be retrieved (HTTP 403).
- The exact TReDS buyer-mandate thresholds (₹500 crore → ₹250 crore turnover) were not confirmed in retrieved article bodies.
- No source describing how a private, non-platform independent factor in any kept market runs a reverse-factoring program and what it earns.

## Key Question 6: Transferable lessons for the Philippines (weak enforcement, limited credit data, high informality, bank-dominated finance)

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a small Philippine factor positioning as a registry-dependent, anchor-buyer-led, bank-line-funded niche player: use the new PPSA registry to perfect and publicise title to receivables, build around a handful of confirmable buyers, keep tickets small and tenors short, price for 1–2% fraud and double-digit arrears, and avoid retail/P2P funding.

### Cited Findings
- Philippines: the PPSA registry was officially established in 2025, letting MSMEs use receivables and other movable assets as collateral; MSMEs are 99.5% of businesses and services were 86% of new corporations registered in 2024; lack of real estate collateral is the main obstacle to bank loans — [Daily Tribune, 21 Mar 2025](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/03/21/factoring-unlocking-msme-growth-in-service-economy)
- Philippine invoice discounting/factoring is regulated as a financing-company activity under the Financing Company Act, with SEC-OGC 2024 opinions on scope and governance (financing companies under the Revised Corporate Governance Code need ≥5 directors) — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)
- A commercial estimate puts the Philippine factoring market at USD 18.35 bn in 2025 — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/philippines-factoring-market) (not an FCI figure; treat as unreliable)
- Factoring is larger where credit information bureaus are developed; creditor rights are not statistically related to factoring volume (cross-country evidence) — [Klapper 2005 abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wbk:wbrwps:3593)
- Chile's success attributed to legal title (Ley 19.983) + e-invoice + tax authority — [ICARE](https://www.icare.cl/contenido-digital/semana-economia-t2-c19/); Peru's registry made >95% of >25,000 factoring users MIPYMEs — [Peru Congress (PDF)](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/comisiones2024/Produccion/files/desafios_e_impacto_ppt_final_dv_16525_18.pdf); Vietnam's bank-only regime left factoring at ~VND 16,000 bn and needs a receivables data centre to stop duplicate financing — [VNBA/IFC](https://vnba.org.vn/en/promoting-factoring-services-in-vietnam-18611.htm)
- Non-bank SME factoring in Chile runs at 10.18% weighted arrears with 59% under 30 days (2025) — [Chócale](https://chocale.cl/2026/05/factoring-no-bancario-crece-casi-14-en-2025-y-supera-los-us-5-000-millones/); Indonesian productive-sector platforms are over-represented above 5% TWP90 — [Kontan](https://keuangan.kontan.co.id/news/strategi-fintech-lending-sektor-produktif-tekan-twp90-agar-tak-membengkak); 1–2% of invoice finance globally is estimated to be fraudulent — [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/anti-fraud-blockchain-for-invoice-financing-goes-live)
- Acudeen priced at 2% per month in the Philippines (2017) and reached ~US$8 m over two years — [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/manila-startup-acudeen-wins-500k-at-seedstars-world-2017); [UnionBank GlobalLinker](https://unionbank.globallinker.com/bizforum/article/acudeen-partners-up-with-unionbank-globallinker-to-help-millions-of-msmes-nationwide/21274)
- Successful fintech factors funded growth with bank warehouse lines (US$40–50 m first lines) then IFC debt — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=173620); [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/funding-societies-crosses-us3-billion-in-sme-lending); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/09/drip-capital-secures-113-million-funding-expand-trade-finance)
- Investree failed on a Rp 7.5 bn equity floor and founder governance, not demand — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/10/22/ojk-shuts-down-troubled-p2p-lender-investree-amid-alleged-fraud)

### Inferences
- **Model fit:** With weak enforcement and thin credit data, the Klapper finding (information, not creditor rights, drives factoring) argues for a model that substitutes the buyer's information and confirmation for the SME's credit history: i.e. anchor-buyer / confirmed-invoice factoring, not open spot factoring of unverified invoices. This is the NAFIN/TReDS logic applied at independent scale.
- **Infrastructure to lean on:** The Philippines now has the PPSA registry (2025) but no Chile/Peru/Colombia-style invoice-as-title rule or mandatory e-invoice registry. A small factor should (a) register every purchased receivable on the PPSA registry to deter double financing, (b) require buyer acknowledgement on each invoice as a contractual substitute for legal "mérito ejecutivo", and (c) treat lobbying for an e-invoice/registry link (via BIR EIS) as a strategic, not charitable, activity.
- **Recourse mix:** Given no reliable buyer credit data and no credit insurance depth, start recourse-to-seller on unconfirmed invoices and move to non-recourse only on confirmed invoices from anchor buyers the factor has underwritten directly — mirroring how TReDS financiers take buyer risk only because the buyer has confirmed on-platform.
- **Pricing and loss budget:** Acudeen's ~2%/month shows the market bore 24%+ annualised; Chilean non-bank arrears of ~10% and a 1–2% fraud baseline imply pricing of 1.5–3%/month with explicit provisions, and tenors of 30–90 days to keep exposure turning.
- **Funding:** Avoid retail P2P; plan founder equity plus a Philippine bank line sized like Drip's first US$40 m warehouse (proportionally smaller), then approach IFC/ADB only after 24+ months of audited cohort loss data.
- **Scale expectation:** Even in Peru's well-plumbed market, ~92 non-bank factoring companies share ~27% of S/43 bn; a Philippine independent should model a niche book, with growth coming from buyer count rather than seller count.

### Gaps
- No FCI or BSP/SEC figure for actual Philippine factoring volume, number of active factoring/financing companies doing receivables finance, or penetration was found; the only market-size number is a commercial estimate (IMARC).
- No Philippine data on credit-insurance availability for domestic receivables, which determines whether non-recourse is feasible at small scale.
- Whether the BIR's e-invoicing system (EIS) can be linked to receivables assignment the way SII (Chile), CAVALI/SUNAT (Peru) and DIAN/RADIAN (Colombia) are, was not researched here and should be covered by the Philippine-regulation workstream.
