# Philippines: Factoring, Invoice Financing and Supply Chain Finance Market and Players (as of November 2026)

Research notes compiled 4 November 2026. Scope: Philippines only. Every number carries the year of the data; anything older than 2023 is flagged "(pre-2023)". Where a company's current status could not be verified, that is stated explicitly.

Method notes for the report writer:
- Sources are mostly Philippine business press (Manila Bulletin, Philstar, BusinessWorld, Inquirer, Tribune), bank/company websites, ADB/IFC disclosures and law-firm commentaries. Several primary sites were unreachable from this environment (dti.gov.ph did not resolve; many inquirer.net, bworldonline.com, philstar.com, e27 and tribune pages returned HTTP 403), so some figures are cited via the search-result snippet of the article URL rather than a full read. These are marked "(snippet)".
- The web-search budget for this session was exhausted before every sub-question could be closed. Open items are listed under "Gaps" in each section.
- No FCI country data, no SEC aggregate for financing-company receivables, and no Philippine late-payment survey with hard numbers were found. The market-size section therefore triangulates.

## Key Question 1 — Demand side: MSME base, credit gap, bank MSME lending, Magna Carta, payment terms and receivables stress

### Takeaway
The Philippines has roughly 1.24 million MSMEs (99.6% of establishments, ~6.35 million jobs, ~67% of employment, 2023 data) but banks lend them only ~4.7% of their loan book (P574.8 billion at end-2025), MSME credit is ~2.1–2.2% of GDP, and MSME NPLs run ~10%. The Magna Carta mandatory allocation expired in June 2018 and is not enforceable; before that, big banks routinely paid fines rather than lend. Hard survey data on B2B payment terms is absent; practitioner commentary consistently describes 30/60/90-day terms from large buyers and government, with modern retail at 60–90 days, and government contractor payments slowing sharply in 2025–2026 after the flood-control scandal.

### Cited Findings

**MSME base (2023 data, latest official)**
- PSA 2023 List of Establishments: 1,246,373 businesses, of which MSMEs were 99.63%; MSME employment 6,351,466 jobs or 66.97% of national employment (2023) — [DTI MSME Statistics page (via search snippet; dti.gov.ph unreachable from this session)](https://dti.gov.ph/businesses/msmes/msmeresources/msme-statistics); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=710803)
- ADB Asia SME Monitor 2024 (released Nov 2024): Philippine MSMEs rose to a record 1,241,766 in 2023; MSME jobs rose from 5.38M (2020) to 6.36M (2023); MSMEs are 99.6% of registered businesses and 66.8% of the workforce; about four in five MSMEs and three-quarters of MSME workers are outside Metro Manila — [Manila Bulletin, 20 Nov 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/11/20/bad-loans-hinder-ph-msme-financing) (Note: the 1,241,766 ADB figure and the 1,246,373 PSA figure differ slightly; both refer to 2023.)
- 2022 comparison (pre-2023): 5,607,748 MSME jobs, 65.10% of employment — [DTI 2022 statistics](https://dti.gov.ph/resources/msme-statistics)

**MSME credit market size and gap**
- ADB Asia SME Monitor 2024: MSME loans were only 4.1% of total bank lending and 2.1% of GDP in 2023; share of bank loans fell from 6.1% pre-pandemic to 4.9% (2020), 4.6% (2021), 4.3% (2022); MSME loans to GDP fell from 3.2% (2019) to 2.2% (2022); loans to 1,521,815 MSMEs rose 2.1% to P503 billion in 2023; MSME NPLs were P51.9 billion in 2023, about a tenth of the sector's borrowings vs 3.2% gross NPL ratio system-wide; the Philippines "continued to have the highest MSME NPL ratio at 10.3 percent, nearly double the 5.5-percent ratio in 2019"; pawnshops had P40 billion outstanding at end-2022 with 98.5% in the provinces — [Manila Bulletin, 20 Nov 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/11/20/bad-loans-hinder-ph-msme-financing)
- ADB quote on why: "lack of collateral and recurring issues over risks and costs make MSME lending a high-risk and low-margin proposition"; "there is no well-functioning online registry for movable collateral" (as of 2024) — [Manila Bulletin, 20 Nov 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/11/20/bad-loans-hinder-ph-msme-financing)
- SME credit penetration 2.2% of GDP in 2023 (IFC citing ADB SME Monitor 2023) — [e27, 25 May 2026](https://e27.co/first-circle-lands-us4-87m-from-cathay-united-to-expand-philippine-sme-loans-20260525/)
- A financing gap for Philippine small businesses of "around P67 billion to P180 billion" is attributed to ADB in press coverage of the Asialink/IFC deal (2025); the original ADB source, year and methodology were not located — [GMA News (snippet)](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/933613/asialink-secures-130-m-loan-from-ifc-to-boost-lending-to-women-led-msmes/story). Treat as unverified.
- IFC has not published its own Philippine MSME gap number. An IFC advisory project (approved Sept 2025, budget $962,317, running to end-March 2027, clients SB Corp and First Digital Finance Corp.) will "quantify the country's lending gap, and explore innovative investment structures" including for MSMEs outside Metro Manila — [Manila Bulletin, 3 Jul 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/07/03/ifc-eyes-wider-msme-credit-access-in-philippines); [IFC disclosure](https://disclosures.ifc.org/project-detail/AS/609779/scaling-up-msme-lending-in-the-philippines)
- IFC "Philippines Supply Chain Finance Market Development" study (pre-2023, reported Dec 2022): estimated "over $20 billion in readily available SCF assets" that banks and non-deposit-taking lenders could finance; the country has a legal foundation for SCF but non-bank lenders and fintechs are "not yet adequately engaged" and "banks have not stepped up" — [PaymentsJournal, 20 Dec 2022](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/supply-chain-finance-in-the-philippines/)
- BSP financial inclusion survey (cited by ADB, Oct 2025): 57% of Filipino borrowers still depend on informal lenders charging up to 20% per week — [Manila Bulletin, 27 Oct 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/10/27/adb-approves-30-million-loan-for-gcashs-fuse-financing-to-boost-msme-lending)

**BSP data on bank MSME lending (end-2025)**
- Total bank loans to MSMEs P574.8 billion at end-December 2025, +5.2% from P546.22 billion; micro & small P238.45B (+9.8%), medium P336.35B (+2.2%); total bank loan portfolio net of exclusions P12.14 trillion, so MSME loans are ~4.7% of the book — [Philstar, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/03/06/2512273/banks-lend-52-more-msmes); [Manila Bulletin, 3 Mar 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/03/03/bank-lending-to-msmes-grows-to-575-billion-in-2025-but-gap-persists); [BusinessWorld, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.bworldonline.com/banking-finance/2026/03/04/733948/phl-banks-loans-to-micro-small-and-medium-businesses-up-5-at-end-2025)
- Breakdown by bank type (end-2025): universal & commercial banks P429.19B (micro/small P152.96B; medium P276.23B); thrift banks P87.98B; rural banks P56.83B; U/KBs hold nearly three-quarters of system MSME exposure — [Philstar, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/03/06/2512273/banks-lend-52-more-msmes)
- Mid-year: MSME lending rose 10.8% to P540.92 billion at end-June 2025 — [Tribune, 17 Sep 2025](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/09/17/msme-loans-up-108-amid-bsp-post-quota-support)
- Q1 2026: medium enterprises received P336.4 billion, 2.8% of banks' total lending book — [Inquirer (snippet), Jun 2026](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=594559)

**Magna Carta for MSMEs (RA 6977 as amended by RA 9501) — status and avoidance**
- The mandatory allocation (8% of loan portfolio to micro and small, 2% to medium) expired on 16 June 2018; BSP says banks remain under "close supervisory monitoring" via quarterly reporting and that it now favours an "enabling ecosystem" over quotas — [Philstar, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/03/06/2512273/banks-lend-52-more-msmes); [Manila Bulletin, 11 Sep 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/09/11/bsp-credit-scoring-system-seen-to-lift-msme-lending-after-falling-short-of-quota-in-q2). Several 2025 press reports still describe the quota as in force; those are wrong on this point.
- Pre-expiry avoidance (pre-2023): big banks "have often opted to pay penalties for noncompliance instead of taking on the risks of lending to small firms" — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=372227); BSP's Guinigundo: "many banks prefer to just pay the fines rather than risk lending" — [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=169181); fine for medium-enterprise shortfall was P100,000 multiplied by the percentage of under-compliance (2013-era) — [Edge Davao](https://edgedavao.net/the-economy/2012/09/bsp-advances-support-to-msme-sector/); BSP levied P859 million in fines on financial institutions 2018–2020 with mandated-credit noncompliance among top violations — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=395368)
- Academic assessment of the mandated-credit program (ADB working paper, pre-2023) — [ADB](https://www.adb.org/node/176822)
- Bills to restore mandatory lending have been filed (HB 8109, 19th Congress; HB 2098, 20th Congress) but none found enacted as of Nov 2026 — [HB 8109](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_19/HB08109.pdf); [HB 2098](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB02098.pdf)
- Pandemic-era alternative: BSP allowed MSME loans to count as alternative reserve compliance (2020–2023 relief; pre-2023 policy) — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=372227)
- Rate pass-through: during the 2022–2024 hiking cycle SME loans passed through ~32% of policy-rate increases vs ~64% for corporate and ~20% for household loans; the gap stems from a "significant credit risk premium embedded in SME ... lending rates"; policy rate peaked at 6.5% in H1 2024 with cuts from Aug 2024 — [AMRO](https://amro-asia.org/understanding-weak-policy-rate-pass-through-in-the-philippines-why-are-bank-lending-rates-so-sticky)

**Typical B2B payment terms and late-payment stress (no hard survey found; commentary only)**
- Philippine SMEs "commonly serve bigger companies, institutions and government projects under 30-, 60- or even 90-day payment terms. The bigger the client, the longer the wait tends to be" (2026 column) — [Newswav / BusinessWorld column, "The problem of delayed payments in SMEs"](https://newswav.com/article/the-problem-of-delayed-payments-in-smes-A2607_Aag3MC)
- Modern-trade retail: suppliers of local products to supermarkets wait "60 to 90 days before receiving the first collection" (2026 column, Cebu) — [SunStar Cebu column](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/toral-why-local-products-quietly-disappear-from-supermarkets)
- Cebu SME financing piece: even fully digitised businesses face cash-flow problems "if the buyer still pays after 60 or 90 days" (2026) — [Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/empowering-cebu-smes-through-flexible-financing-solutions-A2606_ykbOU8)
- A column argues the Philippines has no regulatory limit on payment terms comparable to the UK, so "60–90 days after end of month" has become normalised; recommends large firms default to 30 days and publish average days-to-pay (2026) — [Newswav, "How big business can help SMEs grow"](https://newswav.com/article/how-big-business-can-help-smes-grow-A2601_aGm1BW); [Newswav, "The hidden cost of getting paid late"](https://newswav.com/article/the-hidden-cost-of-getting-paid-late-A2602_FCa1xp)
- Logistics/forwarding: industry piece on delayed payments in Philippine logistics (date not shown) — [PortCalls](https://portcalls.com/delayed-payments-blame-poor-financial-management-in-logistics)
- Government contractors: see Key Question 5 (payments to contractors slowed in 2025–2026; DBP provisioning; ARTA/DBM "Ease of Paying Credit" initiative).
- World Bank Enterprise Survey Philippines 2023 (fieldwork Apr 2023–Feb 2024; 68% response rate) contains loan-application and obstacle variables but no headline payment-days figure was retrievable here — [World Bank Microdata](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/6464)
- Atradius Payment Practices Barometer Asia 2023 covers the region; the Philippine country table was not retrievable — [Daily Guardian](https://www.dailyguardian.com.ph/blog/asian-businesses-bolster-credit-risk-management-amid-optimism-for-future-growth-survey)

### Inferences
- With MSME bank credit at ~4.7% of a P12.1 trillion book and NPLs around 10%, banks have structural reasons to prefer anchor-backed (reverse factoring) structures over direct SME lending; this is consistent with every bank SCF programme found (BPI, RCBC, UnionBank, Security Bank) being anchor-led.
- The absence of a payment-terms survey is itself a finding: a venture would need to generate its own data (e.g., from anchor AP ageing) rather than rely on published benchmarks.
- The IFC's ">$20 billion SCF assets" estimate (2022) versus ~P13 billion of bank SCF volume at RCBC and a few hundred suppliers at BPI (see KQ2) implies the formal SCF market is still at low single-digit percent penetration.

### Gaps
- No post-2023 DTI/PSA MSME count (2024 List of Establishments not located; dti.gov.ph unreachable).
- No primary IFC or ADB document giving a peso-denominated MSME finance gap; the P67–180 billion figure is a secondhand citation.
- No quantitative Philippine late-payment / DSO survey by sector (retail/FMCG, construction, BPO suppliers, agriculture, exporters) was found. Only columns and vendor blogs.
- BSP quarterly MSME exposure reports exist (bsp.gov.ph) but were not fetched directly.

## Key Question 2 — Supply side, banks: who offers factoring / receivables finance / SCF and on what terms

### Takeaway
Four banks have visible, anchor-led SCF programmes: BPI (Digital SCF launched 2024; 12+ anchors, 500+ suppliers; transaction value tripled in 2025; non-recourse supplier finance plus recourse/non-recourse receivables discounting lines), RCBC (SCF ~4% of ~P330 billion 2025 trade flows, growing ~350% y/y), UnionBank (blockchain-based SCF on "The Portal", 58 anchors and P7 billion cumulative loans at last disclosure), and Security Bank (first ADB risk-participation agreement in the Philippines, May 2026). Metrobank lists a Supplier Finance Program (page dated 2019). No BDO, Land Bank or DBP SCF/factoring product was found. No bank publishes SCF pricing.

### Cited Findings

**BPI (Bank of the Philippine Islands)**
- Digital Supply Chain Financing Program launched 2024; corporate clients upload approved invoices, suppliers choose which to fund, funds credited same day; "more than 12 corporate clients and over 500 suppliers across various industries"; transaction values grew "more than threefold" in 2025 — [BPI press release, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.bpi.com.ph/about-bpi/news/bpis-digital-supply-chain-financing-program-triples-transaction-volumes-in-2025); [Context.ph, 5 Aug 2026](https://context.ph/2026/08/05/bpi-digital-supply-chain-financing-triples-in-value-amid-rising-business-adoption/)
- Oct 2026: programme extended to suppliers of logistics firm FAST Group; suppliers select receivables and receive same-day funding — [Context.ph](https://context.ph/?p=80509); [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/bpi-fast-group-boost-supplier-liquidity-with-digital-financing)
- Product structure: Supplier Finance lets anchors' suppliers sell eligible receivables to BPI "on a non-recourse basis" — [BPI Supply Chain Solutions page](https://www.bpi.com.ph/institutional-banking/financing/supply-chain-solutions); Receivables Finance product page — [BPI](https://www.bpi.com.ph/content/bpi/ph/en/institutional-banking/cashflow-management/supply-chain-solutions/receivables-finance.html)
- Mechanism (merger FAQ): SCF facility "grants a Receivables Discounting Line to approved Suppliers to receive financing for their approved invoices", with non-recourse and full-recourse receivables discounting options — [BPI–Robinsons Bank merger FAQ](https://bpi.com.ph/about-bpi/bpi-and-robinsons-bank-merger/faqs/supply-chain-finance)
- Pricing: not published.

**RCBC (Rizal Commercial Banking Corp.)**
- Expects trade flows of P330 billion by end-2025; documentary trade 48%, LCs/SBLCs/guarantees 31%, trade finance ~16%; SCF currently ~4% of total trade volume and projected to grow 342% by year-end; SCF volumes "more than tripled" y/y; suppliers get early payment "at competitive rates" priced off the buyer's credit — [RCBC](https://www.rcbc.com/rcbc-supply-chain-finance-philippine-trade-growth); later report cites 349% SCF volume growth in 2025 — [Context.ph, 16 Nov 2025](https://context.ph/2025/11/16/rcbc-sees-trade-boom-scf-to-triple/)
- RCBC uses a standard "Deed of Assignment – VIP Receivable" form for corporate loans, i.e., receivables assignment is routine collateral practice — [RCBC form (PDF)](https://www.rcbc.com/bank_forms/CorpLoans/DOA%20-%20VIP%20Receivable.pdf)

**Security Bank + ADB**
- 12 May 2026: ADB signed a risk participation agreement (RPA) with Security Bank under its Trade and Supply Chain Finance Program (TSCFP), "the first such agreement forged by the Manila-based multilateral lender with a Philippine bank"; ADB shares credit exposure so Security Bank can "extend financing support to more local SME suppliers"; signed by Steven Beck (ADB) and John Cary Ong (Security Bank EVP, wholesale banking); TSCFP has supported >$74 billion of trade since 2009 via >200 partner banks — [Manila Bulletin, 12 May 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/05/12/adb-security-bank-launch-first-philippine-supply-chain-finance-partnership); [BusinessWorld, 12 May 2026](https://www.bworldonline.com/economy/2026/05/12/749191/adb-security-bank-to-offer-smes-supply-chain-financing/); [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/adb-security-bank-tie-up-to-boost-sme-financing)
- Programme size and pricing not disclosed.

**UnionBank of the Philippines (incl. UBX)**
- SCF platform embedded in corporate portal "The Portal", described as the Philippines' first blockchain-enabled SCF platform (built with IBM); anchor-centric "community" model; API for anchor ERP integration — [SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/business/bank-unveils-phs-1st-blockchain-enabled-supply-chain-financing-platform); [Manila Bulletin, 16 Nov 2022 (pre-2023)](https://mb.com.ph/2022/11/16/unionbank-steps-up-supply-chain-finance-with-the-blockchain); [FintechNews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/55995/fintech/unionbank-enhances-it-supply-chain-financing-solution-for-msmes/)
- Scale at last disclosure (undated article, c. 2023): >200 MSME customers, 58 supply-chain community anchors, >P7 billion in loans and >P12 billion in trade transactions generated to date — [Inquirer (snippet)](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=430705)
- 2022 (pre-2023): >10,000 MSME leads from anchors; expected >P4 billion new business lines — [Philstar, 18 Mar 2022](https://philstar.com/business/2022/03/18/2167969/unionbank-eyes-p4-billion-new-business-lines); named anchors include New Hope Bulacan Agriculture, Pilmico Foods, a major telco — [Manila Times, 5 May 2022](https://www.manilatimes.net/2022/05/05/public-square/unionbank-honors-5-supply-chain-community-anchors/1842353)
- Group context: UnionBank 2025 net income P10 billion (-17%); unsecured consumer loans P150.8 billion, 61% of loan book — [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/aboitizs-unionbank-2025-full-year-income-down-17-to-p10b)
- No 2024–2026 anchor/supplier counts found.

**Metrobank**
- Trade financing page (dated 2019, pre-2023) lists a Supplier Finance Program under which the bank purchases supplier receivables so the buyer can extend terms — [Metrobank](https://www.metrobank.com.ph/articles/business/solutions/trade/financing-services-solutions). No 2025–2026 SCF announcement found.

**BDO Unibank**
- No SCF or factoring product page found. BDO's SME product is a collateralised term loan: "BDO SME Loan" renamed "BDO Multipurpose Loan" from 1 Oct 2024; P3 million–P50 million or 70% of appraised collateral value; 1–10 year terms; real-estate collateral; priced as "P4.75 per P1,000" monthly amortisation rather than a stated rate — [BDO SME Loan page](https://bdo.com.ph/loans/sme-loan)
- BDO H1 2025: loans P3.4 trillion, deposits >P4 trillion — [Manila Bulletin, 28 Jul 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/07/28/bdo-h1-profit-rises-as-loans-hit-34-trillion-deposits-breach-4-trillion)

**Land Bank of the Philippines / DBP**
- No SCF platform or factoring programme found for either. Land Bank's 2026 "SULONG MSME Fair" offers tiered financing and digital services, not SCF — [Pulse.bot (snippet)](https://www.pulse.bot/banking/news/the-philippines-tarlac-msmes-gain-digital-banking-financing-support-a341624b-98a7-4d02-9eb2-cb6f4a7b5d26/)
- DBP: infrastructure lending is 50–60% of its loan book; lends to contractors "secured by progress billings or government receivables" (see KQ5) — [Manila Bulletin, 28 Nov 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/11/28/dbp-braces-for-up-to-2-billion-dent-in-2025-earnings-amid-looming-rise-in-bad-loans)
- 2018 (pre-2023): Cebu City considered a DBP-backed "loan for suppliers" facility to bridge delayed city payments — [PressReader / SunStar Cebu](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/sunstar-cebu/20180212/281586651058301)

**Bank SME loan pricing (for benchmarking; see KQ8)**
- BPI SME term loan listed at "1.30%" for 12 months in a 2024–2025 comparison; UnionBank business loan at "3% per month"; basis (monthly add-on vs annual) not confirmed — [The Business Manual (snippet; site unreachable)](https://thebusinessmanual.onemega.com/list-banks-offer-business-loans-msmes/)

### Inferences
- All active bank SCF is reverse-factoring on anchor credit; none of the banks appears to offer open-market factoring to SMEs without an anchor. The "white space" for a small factor is SMEs whose buyers are mid-sized firms not on any bank anchor programme, and SMEs too small for bank KYC.
- Bank SCF volumes are still modest: if RCBC's SCF is ~4% of ~P330 billion trade flows, that is on the order of P13 billion in 2025 (own calculation from RCBC figures); BPI's 500 suppliers across 12 anchors implies an average of ~40 suppliers per anchor.

### Gaps
- No published SCF pricing, advance rates or tenor limits for any bank.
- No BDO, Land Bank, DBP, PNB, China Bank or EastWest SCF/factoring product confirmed.
- Could not confirm whether Metrobank's 2019 Supplier Finance Program is still marketed.

## Key Question 3 — Supply side, fintechs and non-banks

### Takeaway
First Circle is the only scaled Philippine fintech originating invoice/receivables-type SME credit (P16 billion+ cumulative to 5,000+ businesses since 2016; SEC-licensed financing company; IFC-backed ABS of up to $110 million approved Sept 2026). Acudeen (invoice marketplace) is effectively defunct (domain parked for sale; no press since 2019). Investree Philippines is wound down (dissolution to 30 Sept 2025) and SeedIn suffered a founder-fraud event (Dec 2023) with the SEC audit outcome unreported. The large volume non-bank MSME lenders (Asialink P41.9 billion book, Esquire, Global Dominion, Fuse/GCash, Maya) are term or vehicle/PDC-secured lenders, not factors. No evidence Jenfi operates in the Philippines. Advance.ph could not be found.

### Cited Findings

**First Circle (First Circle Growth Finance Corp.)**
- SEC-registered financing company (Reg. No. CS201605477, CA No. 1108); not BSP-regulated; banking rails via Netbank (a rural bank); products listed: Business Accounts, Invoices, Business Premium Card, Business Credit Line (up to P25 million, non-collateral), Business Credit Plus, Express Business Loan (short-term, funds in 1 day), Future Salary Access, Solar Financing; "zero upfront fee, pay only for what you use, rebates for early repayment"; "5,000+ Filipino businesses", "P16B+ in loans given since 2016"; customer profile: small P1M–P200M revenue, 42 employees avg; medium+ P200M–P3B revenue; investors Accion International, Fasanara Capital, Insignia Ventures Partners, Koru Partners; institutional partners DTI, Quezon City Government, CIC, IFC — [firstcircle.ph](https://www.firstcircle.ph/)
- May 2026: P300 million (US$4.87 million) credit facility from Cathay United Bank (first Taiwanese bank partner); offers unsecured online lines and loans "of up to 20 million pesos"; ~P16 billion (US$260 million) extended to >5,000 businesses since founding; product mix now includes solar financing; relies on wholesale credit facilities — [e27, 25 May 2026](https://e27.co/first-circle-lands-us4-87m-from-cathay-united-to-expand-philippine-sme-loans-20260525/)
- IFC: $7 million equity/debt in 2024 — [Manila Bulletin, 17 Jun 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/6/17/ifc-invests-7-million-in-local-fintech-firm-first-circle); IFC board approved (10 Sept 2026) up to $110 million: up to $30 million IFC own account (senior tranche up to $20 million, mezzanine up to $10 million; first-tranche participation up to $15 million) plus up to $80 million mobilised from local/institutional investors; structure is an off-balance-sheet SPV issuing ABS backed by "a diversified pool of SME receivables that First Circle originated", up to four years with annual roll-over, "could be the first SME-backed securitization of its kind in the Philippines"; status "pending signing" — [Tribune, 20 Sep 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/20/ifc-approves-up-to-110m-for-first-circle); [Manila Bulletin, 13 May 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/05/13/ifc-eyes-landmark-110-million-philippine-sme-securitization-deal); [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=590240)

**Acudeen Technologies (invoice discounting marketplace) — status: effectively defunct, formally unverified**

## Key Question 5 — Public-sector receivables: can receivables from government agencies/LGUs be financed, and is it done?

### Takeaway
Yes, assignment of government-contract receivables to lenders is legal and routine (Civil Code arts. 1624–1635; deeds of assignment upheld by the Supreme Court; RA 11057 PPSA covers receivables), and banks including DBP lend to contractors against progress billings and government receivables. But in 2025–2026 the flood-control scandal slowed government payments, DBP booked up to P2 billion of extra provisions, and bank credit committees became "more cautious about lending against government receivables". ARTA and DBM launched an "Ease of Paying Credit" initiative (July 2026) to speed payments.

### Cited Findings
- Contractors "borrow from banks through working-capital lines and short-term project financing, often secured by progress billings or government receivables"; payments pass through COA review, DBM budget release and Treasury cash programming; "payment releases for infrastructure contractors are encountering heightened scrutiny and slower processing following controversies surrounding anomalous flood-control projects"; "credit committees consequently become more cautious about lending against government receivables"; author proposes BSP encourage "structured supply-chain financing frameworks for verified government receivables"; public infrastructure spending ~5–6% of GDP — [Benel Lagua (ex-DBP EVP), Manila Bulletin/FINEX, 21 May 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/05/21/the-problem-of-financing-govt-receivables); [FINEX](https://finex.org.ph/2026/05/21/the-problem-of-financing-govt-receivables/)
- DBP expects up to P2 billion lower 2025 net income after provisioning for NPLs "caused by delayed payments to contractors involved in public infrastructure projects"; "Even the good contractors are having problems with some of their receivables"; infrastructure lending is 50–60% of DBP's loan book; 90–95% of soured loans already provisioned — [Manila Bulletin, 28 Nov 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/11/28/dbp-braces-for-up-to-2-billion-dent-in-2025-earnings-amid-looming-rise-in-bad-loans)
- ARTA and DBM advanced the "Ease of Paying Credit" initiative (meeting 17 July 2026); DBM to designate a focal person for a Joint Memorandum Circular on faster government payment procedures — [Context.ph, 20 Jul 2026](https://context.ph/2026/07/20/arta-dbm-target-faster-government-payments-for-contractors/)
- Legal mechanics (commentary, AI-assisted, not legal advice): deed of assignment of payment rights binds the agency once officially notified; agency should acknowledge remittance to the lender's account; government retains set-off rights (e.g., BIR arrears, penalties on other projects); performance obligations are not assignable without consent; milestone-based payments mean termination for default extinguishes the receivable; banks check Notice of Award, Notice to Proceed, GAA line item or Certificate of Availability of Funds, and absence of anti-assignment clauses; lenders should register under RA 11057 — [Respicio & Co.](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/using-government-contracts-as-collateral-for-loans-in-the-philippines)
- Case law (all pre-2023): assignments of 10% of collections under a Republic contract (Prudential Bank v. Community Builders, 1988) — [Jur.ph](https://jur.ph/jurisprudence/summary/prudential-bank-trust-co-v-community-builders-co-inc); deeds assigning receivables from National Power Corporation contracts (Asian Construction v. PCIB, 2006) — [Chan Robles](https://chanrobles.com/scdecisions/jurisprudence2006/apr2006/gr_153827_2006.php); assignment of payments from a Bureau of Public Works contract (PNB v. CA, 1991) — [Batasnatin](https://batasnatin.com/laws/gr-33174)
- A House bill would let accredited contractors use National Government certificates of indebtedness as loan collateral (undated; not found enacted) — [HB 3120](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_13/HB03120.pdf)
- Historical magnitude (pre-2023): allegation that unpaid 2018 infrastructure contracts "ballooned to P100 billion" (disputed by Palace) — [Inquirer, 2019](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1086126); [Philstar, 2019](https://www.philstar.com/business/2019/02/18/1894735/business)
- PPSA registry: Land Registration Authority launched the Personal Property Security Registry on 13 Feb 2025 (operational from 3 Feb 2025 per LRA Circular 02-2025); receivables are within scope; transitional eCMR arrangement under Circular 03-2025 (May 2025) — [LRA Circular 02-2025](https://lra.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/02-2025-OPERATIONALIZATION-OF-THE-PERSONAL-PROPERTY-SECURITY-REGISTRY.pdf); [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/land-registration-authority-launches-the-personal-property-security-registry/); [PPSR portal](https://ppsr.lra.gov.ph/?p=60)
- LGU-level: a 2018 Cebu City proposal for a DBP-backed supplier loan facility to bridge delayed city payments (pre-2023) — [SunStar Cebu via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/sunstar-cebu/20180212/281586651058301)

### Inferences
- Government receivables are legally financeable and historically a core bank product for contractors, but the 2025–2026 payment slowdown has turned them into a timing-risk asset. A small factor would face both the set-off/consent frictions and unpredictable collection cycles; the national-agency segment is currently contracting rather than opening.
- LGU receivables are the least documented segment; no operating LGU supplier-finance scheme was found.

### Gaps
- No text of the New Government Procurement Act (RA 12009, 2024) or IRR on assignment/consent rules was retrieved.
- No data on average days-to-pay by national agencies or LGUs.
- No evidence of any non-bank factor financing LGU receivables.

## Key Question 6 — Market size for Philippine factoring / receivables finance

### Takeaway
There is no FCI country statistic for the Philippines and no SEC aggregate of financing-company receivables in the sources found. The only "market size" number is a commercial estimate (IMARC: US$18.35 billion in 2025) with no visible methodology, which should not be used uncritically. Triangulation from disclosed programme volumes suggests formal bank SCF is on the order of a few tens of billions of pesos per year and fintech receivables finance low single-digit billions, against the IFC's 2022 estimate of >$20 billion (≈P1.1 trillion) of financeable SCF assets.

### Cited Findings
- FCI: the search found no Philippines row in FCI statistics; FCI data covers ~390 members in 90 countries — [BCR / FCI coverage](https://bcrpub.com/news/fci-factoring-volumes-experience-further-growth). No Philippine FCI member identified in sources found.
- IMARC: "Philippines factoring market size reached USD 18.35 Billion in 2025" (commercial estimate; methodology not visible) — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/philippines-factoring-market)
- IFC (2022, pre-2023): ">$20 billion in readily available SCF assets" — [PaymentsJournal](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/supply-chain-finance-in-the-philippines/)
- Bank MSME loans outstanding P574.8 billion (end-2025) — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/03/06/2512273/banks-lend-52-more-msmes)
- RCBC: SCF ≈4% of ~P330 billion 2025 trade flows — [RCBC](https://www.rcbc.com/rcbc-supply-chain-finance-philippine-trade-growth)
- UnionBank: >P7 billion cumulative SCF loans, >P12 billion trade transactions (c. 2023) — [Inquirer (snippet)](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=430705)
- BPI: 500+ suppliers, 12+ anchors, value 3x in 2025 (no peso figure) — [BPI](https://www.bpi.com.ph/about-bpi/news/bpis-digital-supply-chain-financing-program-triples-transaction-volumes-in-2025)

- Crowdfunding (incl. invoice notes): P2.24 billion cumulative to end-2024 — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/02/235903-crowdfunding-generates-34m-fresh-funds-for-small-businesses-in-philippines/)

### Inferences
- Own triangulation: RCBC SCF ≈ P13 billion (2025); if BPI and UnionBank are of similar order and Security Bank/Metrobank smaller, bank SCF throughput is plausibly P30–60 billion per year — a few percent of the IFC's >$20 billion asset pool. This is an inference, not a sourced figure.
- The IMARC US$18.35 billion figure is close to the IFC "addressable asset" figure and likely represents potential rather than realised volume.

### Gaps
- No SEC annual aggregate for financing-company receivables (SEC publishes only registry lists in sources found).
- No BSP breakdown of bank loans by "receivables financing" or "factoring".
- FCI annual report country tables not accessed.

## Key Question 7 — Credit information infrastructure: CIC, bureaus, BIR e-invoicing, PhilSys

### Takeaway
The Credit Information Corporation has scaled sharply (40.8 million borrowers and 423.6 million contracts as of end-July 2026; 79.6% hit rate in 2025; P10 per report), is PhilSys-integrated for identity, and 115 of 340 financing-company submitting entities now access it. BSP launched a JICA-built SME scoring system (CRDPh) in Aug 2025. BIR's mandatory e-invoicing (EIS) deadline has been pushed to 31 Dec 2026 for large taxpayers, e-commerce and CAS users, so invoice data is not yet a universal verification rail. No findings on CIBI, TransUnion PH or CRIF roles.

### Cited Findings
- CIC (RA 9510) database: 40.8 million borrowers (+60%) and 423.6 million contracts (+261%) at end-July 2026 vs end-2024; 115 of 340 financing-company SEPs accessing; accessing entities +47%; 1.8 billion records loaded in 2025 vs 669 million in 2024; hit rate 79.6% (2025) vs 75.1% (2024); access volume 37.7 million (2025) with 28.2 million credit reports generated, 63.8 million annualised 2026; direct access needs P30,000 advance for 1,000 reports; Online Dispute Resolution integrated with PhilSys; plans to add utility data and alternative data — [CIC President Ben Baltazar presentation, 13 Aug 2026 (PDF)](https://microfinancecouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Breakout_CIC_Atty.-Ben-Baltazar.pdf)
- CIC generated 10 million credit reports in 2024, double 2023 — [Philstar, 15 Mar 2025](https://www.philstar.com/business/2025/03/15/2428401/cic-generates-10-million-credit-reports-2024/amp/); "CIC credit reports rise to 27M in 2025" (headline; body not verified) — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=727421); fee P10 per inquiry — [CIC](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/press-release-cic-credit-report-fee-financial-institutions-still-p10-inquiry); PhilSys compatibility — [CIC](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/press-release-cic-database-covers-30m-borrowers-be-compatible-philsys-id-key-creditworthiness-and); submitting-entity lists — [CIC SEP list](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/SEPlist)
- CRDPh: BSP launched the Credit Risk Database Philippines Web-based Scoring System in Aug 2025, built with JICA from anonymised data of 33 pioneer financial institutions; outputs probability of default for SMEs; intended to move banks from collateral- to risk-based lending — [Manila Bulletin, 11 Sep 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/09/11/bsp-credit-scoring-system-seen-to-lift-msme-lending-after-falling-short-of-quota-in-q2); [FintechNews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/67883/lending/bsp-jica-launch-new-credit-scoring-platform-for-smes/); [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/09/11/bsp-bets-on-new-credit-scoring-after-msme-loans-fall-short-of-quota-2)
- BIR EIS: RR 26-2025 (Sept 2025) amended RR 8-2022 and set a single deadline of 31 Dec 2026 (from 14 Mar 2026) for covered taxpayers — large taxpayers, e-commerce/online sellers and users of computerised accounting systems; invoices transmitted as JSON via API to EIS in near real time; pilot started with the ~100 largest taxpayers — [Comarch](https://www.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/philippines-e-invoicing-mandate-extended-new-2026-compliance-deadline/); [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/10/25/bir-e-invoicing-philippines-2026-scope-rollout-compliance-and-technical-requirements-explained/); [Ecosio](https://ecosio.com/en/compliance/philippines/e-invoicing/); [Banqup](https://www.banqup.com/resources/blog/philippines-e-invoicing-countdown-to-compliance-under-the-bir-eis). No source addresses third-party (lender) access to EIS invoice data.
- Movable collateral registry (PPSA/RA 11057) live since Feb 2025 (see KQ5) — [LRA](https://lra.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/02-2025-OPERATIONALIZATION-OF-THE-PERSONAL-PROPERTY-SECURITY-REGISTRY.pdf)
- Fraud landscape for digital lending: loan stacking and fake employment verification are highlighted risks (2025) — [Bureau.id](https://www.bureau.id/blog/the-philippines-in-focus-fraud-landscape-for-digital-banking-lending-report)

### Inferences
- A factor can run CIC checks on SME principals cheaply (P10/report) but CIC holds loan-contract data, not trade-payable data, so it does not detect double financing of invoices. Until EIS is universal (post-2026 and only for larger taxpayers), invoice verification will rely on anchor confirmation.
- PPSA registration of receivables assignments is now possible and would be the primary legal defence against double-pledging.

### Gaps
- No findings on CIBI, TransUnion Philippines or CRIF products for SME/trade-credit data.
- No source on whether BIR will expose EIS data to lenders or on a planned Phase 2 for smaller taxpayers.
- No PPSR registration statistics.

## Key Question 8 — Pricing benchmarks: what Philippine SMEs pay for short-term credit

### Takeaway
Published Philippine SME pricing is sparse. Government-subsidised SB Corp lends at 1% per month (≈12% p.a. simple) on PO financing; bank SME term loans are quoted around 1.3% (basis unclear) to 3% per month for unsecured; fintech consumer loans run 4%+ per month add-on (APR up to ~84%); informal lenders charge up to 20% per week. Banks' SME lending rates carry a large credit-risk premium and pass through only ~32% of policy-rate moves. No factor or SCF provider publishes discount rates.

### Cited Findings
- SB Corp PO Financing: 1% per month, 30–360 days, 80% advance (2025) — [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/01/17/sbcorp-unveils-2-new-loan-programs-for-msmes); Business Expansion loan 0% first 12 months then 1%/month diminishing — same source
- UnionBank business loan "3% per month"; BPI SME term loan "1.30%" for 12 months (basis not stated); SBC 1%/month (2024–2025 listing; site unreachable so taken from snippet) — [The Business Manual](https://thebusinessmanual.onemega.com/list-banks-offer-business-loans-msmes/)
- BDO Multipurpose Loan (collateralised): P4.75 per P1,000 monthly amortisation (≈ standard rate for borrowers under 50) — [BDO](https://bdo.com.ph/loans/sme-loan)
- Policy rate peaked 6.5% (H1 2024); SME loans pass through ~32% of hikes; large credit-risk premium in SME rates — [AMRO](https://amro-asia.org/understanding-weak-policy-rate-pass-through-in-the-philippines-why-are-bank-lending-rates-so-sticky)
- Tonik Quick Loan (consumer): "as low as ~4.26%" monthly add-on; APR up to ~84% (2025) — [Moneymax](https://www.moneymax.ph/personal-loan/partners/tonik-quick)
- Maya personal loan (2023): 0.83% monthly add-on, 1.40% effective monthly — [Gadget Pilipinas, Aug 2023](https://www.gadgetpilipinas.net/2023/08/maya-gcash-personal-loans-easier/); GCash GLoan standard rate "2.89%" (period unspecified; snippet) — [GMA](https://gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/834763/gcash-loan-disbursements-hit-p2-5-billion-in-10-months/story)
- Informal lenders: up to 20% per week; 57% of borrowers use them (BSP survey via ADB, 2025) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/10/27/adb-approves-30-million-loan-for-gcashs-fuse-financing-to-boost-msme-lending)
- Regulatory ceiling for small consumer loans from lending/financing companies: 6%/month nominal, 15%/month effective, 5%/month penalty (loans ≤P10,000, ≤4 months) — [BusinessWorld, 2022](https://www.bworldonline.com/banking-finance/2022/03/02/433211/sec-issues-rules-on-interest-rate-cap-for-lending-financing-firms)
- Esquire Financing: rates set case-by-case by credit committee, not published — [esquire.com.ph](https://esquire.com.ph/); First Circle: no rate card; "zero upfront fee ... rebates for early repayment" — [firstcircle.ph](https://www.firstcircle.ph/)
- CIC credit report cost to a lender: P10 per inquiry — [CIC](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/press-release-cic-credit-report-fee-financial-institutions-still-p10-inquiry)

### Inferences
- A practical pricing corridor for unsecured SME working capital in the Philippines is roughly 1%/month (subsidised/state) to 3%/month (bank unsecured) to 4–5%/month (fintech/consumer-style), i.e., ~12% to ~60%+ effective annual. A factor pricing at 1.5–3% per 30 days on invoice value sits inside the bank-unsecured band and well below fintech consumer pricing — but must beat SB Corp's 1%/month on speed and eligibility.

### Gaps
- No bank publishes SCF/supplier-finance discount rates; no fintech publishes invoice-finance fees.
- No BSP series on average SME lending rates was retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- The "1.30%" BPI figure's basis (monthly add-on vs annual) is unresolved.

## Key Question 9 — Known failures and lessons

### Takeaway
Every Philippine receivables/invoice marketplace launched since 2016 has failed or been impaired: Acudeen (blockchain pivot, then silence; domain now for sale), Investree Philippines (wound down 2024–2025 after the Indonesian parent's fraud and licence revocation), SeedIn (founder fraud, payouts suspended, SEC audit). No Philippine case of fake-invoice or double-financing fraud against a lender was found in the press; documented fraud is on the consumer side (loan stacking, fake apps) and in governance (founder misconduct). SEC enforcement against lenders is frequent (56 licences revoked 2025; Digido revoked; Jia suspended then cleared).

### Cited Findings
- Acudeen: no post-2019 coverage; acudeen.com parked for sale (Nov 2026) — [acudeen.com](https://acudeen.com); blockchain/token pivot 2018–2019 — [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/fintech/fintech-startup-takes-receivables-platform-to-blockchain/)
- Investree PH: >P100 million losses (2023), P64 million + P42.8 million impairments, partner refused capital — [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/gotianuns-shut-down-investree-philippines-after-partner-fails-to-provide-capital); parent licence revoked by OJK 21 Oct 2024; CEO Gunadi charged, red-noticed, repatriated — [OJK](https://ojk.go.id/en/berita-dan-kegiatan/siaran-pers/Pages/OJK-Revokes-Business-License-PT-Investree-Radhika-Jaya.aspx); [Indonesia Business Post](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/4883/financial-crimes/former-investree-ceo-placed-on-interpol-red-notice-for-alleged-f)
- SeedIn: founder "anomalous transactions", payouts halted (Dec 2023), SEC special audit (Jan 2024), outcome unreported — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1882089/venture-capital-firm-seedin-suspends-payouts); [Philstar](https://philstar.com/business/2024/01/03/2323001/sec-conducts-audit-seedin)
- SEC regards crowdfunding's "birth pains" as survivable and is still promoting it (July 2024) — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/07/22/2371857/despite-birth-pains-sec-optimistic-crowdfunding)
- Fraud typologies: invoice double-financing described generically (no Philippine case) — [KlearStack glossary](https://klearstack.com/glossary/invoice-double-financing-fraud); manipulated digital supply-chain invoices are prosecutable as computer-related fraud/forgery under RA 10175 — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/computer-related-fraud-suing-vendors-who-manipulate-digital-supply-chain-invoices-in-the-philippines/); loan stacking and fake employment verification flagged in Philippine digital lending (2025) — [Bureau.id](https://bureau.id/resources/blog/the-philippines-in-focus-fraud-landscape-for-digital-banking-lending-report)
- SEC enforcement: revoked 56 lending/financing licences (May–June 2025) — [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/05/31/sec-voids-56-lending-financing-licenses); Digido Finance (UnaCash/UnaPay) licence revoked — [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/sec-revokes-digidos-license-why-it-happened-what-borrowers-need-to-know); CDOs against six financing/lending firms (June 2024) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2024/6/26/sec-orders-6-lending-firms-to-stop-operations); seven unregistered OLPs stopped (Aug 2025) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/08/25/sec-stops-operations-of-7-unregistered-online-lending-platforms); SEC handled 5,415 complaints vs financing/lending companies and OLPs (year unclear) — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=697945); >3,300 complaints vs unregistered entities — [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/961486/sec-over-3-300-complaints-lodged-vs-unregistered-financing-entities/story)
- Government-receivable credit stress 2025–2026 (DBP provisions; contractor delays) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/11/28/dbp-braces-for-up-to-2-billion-dent-in-2025-earnings-amid-looming-rise-in-bad-loans)
- TrueMoney Philippines exited after a decade (payments, not lending) — [FintechNews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/71228/lending/truemoney-philippines-operations-exit/)

### Inferences
- The three marketplace failures share a pattern: dependence on an external funder/partner (Investree SG; Acudeen's bank/Rizal Microbank lines) or a single controlling founder (SeedIn, Acudeen), and no durable balance-sheet funding. The survivor model (First Circle) is DFI-backed balance-sheet lending.
- Absence of a reported double-financing case most likely reflects the tiny size of the invoice-finance market rather than low fraud risk; the PPSR (2025) and anchor confirmation are the available mitigants.

### Gaps
- No public post-mortem or SEC action on Acudeen; formal corporate status not checked against the SEC registry.
- SeedIn audit outcome, investor losses and current status unknown.
- No Philippine court or regulator case on fake invoices or double-financed receivables located; this may exist in jurisprudence under estafa/falsification but was not searched for due to budget limits.
