# Non-recourse factoring feasibility in the Philippines: credit insurance, guarantees, export networks, and emerging-market practice

Research date: 2026-11-04. All figures carry the date of the source. "UNSURE" flags items I could not verify against a primary source. Several target pages (BusinessWorld, Manila Bulletin, The Digital Banker) returned 403/empty on fetch; where I rely on search snippets of those pages I say so.

Bottom line up front: a small Philippine factor cannot realistically offer *insured* non-recourse factoring on domestic SME-to-SME receivables today. The only credible non-recourse paths are (a) buyer-anchored / reverse factoring against a short list of large Philippine corporates and (b) export factoring through an FCI import factor, with the second gated by FCI membership that the Philippines currently appears to lack. Domestic trade credit insurance exists (Atradius via Malayan since March 2024; Coface via local partners since 2006), but it was launched for multinationals and the middle market, and I found no evidence any Philippine insurer has written a whole-turnover policy for a small non-bank factor. Details and sources below.

## Key Question 1: Trade credit insurance availability in the Philippines

### Takeaway
Trade credit insurance (TCI) is sold in the Philippines through fronting partnerships (Atradius–Malayan since March 2024; Coface via local partners since 2006; Allianz Trade lists a Philippine site but I could not identify its local carrier), and it was explicitly launched first for local multinationals, then the middle market. I found no published Philippine premium rates, minimum premiums, turnover thresholds, or any Insurance Commission line-item for "credit insurance"; global indicative pricing is 0.1%–just over 1% of insured turnover.

### Cited Findings
- Atradius names Malayan Insurance Co., Inc. as its Philippines partner; the page (hosted on the Atradius Singapore site, undated, roughly 2024) says Philippine trade covers both the domestic market and international trade, offers trade credit insurance plus collections, but states no thresholds, premiums or limits and gives no Philippine office contact — [Atradius Philippines contact page](https://atradius.sg/contact-us/atradius-philippines)
- The Malayan–Atradius partnership was announced in March 2024 at a Makati press briefing; Atradius's Southeast Asia director said "initially, we started offering it to local multinational corporations", and the partnership is meant to reach the middle market and small businesses. The policy covers the sales contract between the insured seller and its buyer — [Manila Bulletin, 24 Mar 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/3/24/malayan-atradius-tie-up-for-trade-credit-insurance); [Inquirer, Mar 2024](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=453440); [BusinessMirror, 25 Mar 2024](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/03/25/malayan-insurance-partners-with-dutch-insurer-atradius/); [The Digital Banker](https://thedigitalbanker.com/malayan-insurance-partners-with-atradius-to-expand-trade-credit-insurance-in-ph/) (page returned empty on fetch; facts above are from search snippets of the other three)
- Atradius said in 2011 it already offered credit insurance in the Philippines directly or through a partner — [Atradius press release, 27 Sep 2011](https://atradius.us/press/atradius-extends-offering-in-asia.html)
- Coface says it has provided credit insurance in the Philippines since 2006 "with local insurance partners" (Indonesia since 2009) — [Insurance Business Asia](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/breaking-news/coface-appoints-country-manager-for-indonesian-and-philippines-markets-189812.aspx). Coface appointed Darren Chan as Country Manager Malaysia and Head of the Philippines effective 1 April 2026 — [People Matters, 2026](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/appointments/coface-appoints-darren-chan-as-country-manager-for-malaysia-and-head-of-the-philippines-49200). The local partner's name was not found. UNSURE which Philippine non-life insurer fronts for Coface.
- Allianz Trade's "our sites" index lists Philippines among its country sites, but no local carrier or partner is named; in Indonesia Allianz Trade describes its model as "providing reinsurance support and capacity to a number of selected locally licensed insurance companies" — [Allianz Trade sites index](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_BR/our-sites.html); [Allianz Trade Indonesia](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_ID/confirm-your-subscription.html). UNSURE whether Allianz Trade writes Philippine domestic receivables and through whom.
- Global indicative pricing: Atradius says premiums typically range from 0.1% to just over 1.0% of a company's turnover — [Atradius Group credit insurance page](https://group.atradius.com/our-solutions/credit-insurance). Allianz Trade's own factoring comparison uses an example of $5m turnover x 0.25% = $12,500 to x 0.50% = $25,000 for insurance versus about 2% for factoring (vendor-sourced, non-Philippine) — [Allianz Trade (Taiwan site) factoring explainer](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_TW/resources/resources-archive/factoring.html). A broker site cites 0.1%–0.5% of insured revenue — [AccountsReceivableInsurance.net](https://www.accountsreceivableinsurance.net/top-7-trade-credit-insurance-carriers-for-custom-policies/)
- Market structure: Allianz Trade, Atradius and Coface held about 60–65% of global TCI premiums in 2024 (two market-research sources disagree on the exact share) — [Atlas Magazine](https://www.atlas-mag.net/en/articles/credit-insurance-market); [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/trade-credit-insurance-market). ICISA reported global TCI premium volume of $13.89 billion in 2022 (cited in the Malayan–Atradius coverage) — [Manila Bulletin, Mar 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/3/24/malayan-atradius-tie-up-for-trade-credit-insurance)
- Insurance Commission statistics: the IC publishes 2024 non-life performance tables (gross premiums written, net premiums written, premiums earned, as of 31 Dec 2024, unaudited) by company, not by line of business; total non-life net premiums written 2024 were PHP 71,835,444,443 — [IC non-life statistics index](https://www.insurance.gov.ph/non-life-insurance-statistics/2); [IC Net Premiums Written 31 Dec 2024 PDF](https://www.insurance.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Performance-of-Non-Life-Insurance-Companies-Net-Premiums-Written-as-of-31-December-2024.pdf). No "credit insurance" line item appears in these tables.

### Inferences
- Because Atradius and Coface operate via fronting partners and launched with multinationals, a small factor seeking a policy would be an atypical, small-ticket, high-touch client; the economics (minimum premiums are not published, but global practice is that insurers set minimum annual premiums) likely make a whole-turnover policy on a sub-PHP 100m book uneconomic or unavailable. This is an inference; no Philippine minimum premium was found.
- Insurers covering domestic Philippine buyers will underwrite the *buyers*, so a factor's insurable universe will collapse to the same large, well-documented corporates that reverse-factoring programs already target (see Q5).
- The IC's company-level tables cannot tell us how much credit insurance is written in the Philippines; it is almost certainly a rounding error within "casualty/miscellaneous" lines.

### Gaps
- No Philippine premium rates, indemnity percentages (global norm 85–90% UNSURE for PH), minimum premiums, or minimum insured turnover were found for Malayan/Atradius, Coface, or Allianz Trade. Direct quotes from Malayan and the Coface partner are needed.
- No IC data on credit-insurance premiums by line was found; the IC may hold it internally.
- Whether Pioneer, AIG Philippines, Chubb Philippines, Credendo or other local non-life insurers write TCI: not found in this pass.
- Whether any Philippine TCI policy has ever been issued to a non-bank factor (as opposed to a seller): not found.

## Key Question 2: Public guarantees and multilateral backstops (PhilGuarantee, SB Corp, ADB TSCFP, IFC)

### Takeaway
No Philippine public body offers a receivables or non-recourse factoring guarantee product. PhilGuarantee guarantees *bank loans* to MSMEs (standard 60% cover, 65% for women-led MSMEs in a promo running 12 Oct–10 Dec 2026); SB Corp announced a "Receivables Financing" program in January 2025 that I could not confirm launched. ADB's TSCFP signed its first Philippine supply-chain-finance risk participation with Security Bank in May 2026, but ADB's own evaluation (Nov 2025) says the SCF program has struggled to scale and favors low-risk deals; IFC has no identified Philippine GTSF bank.

### Inferences
- ADB/IFC programs are wholesale: they share risk with *banks* on anchor-led programs. A small non-bank factor cannot plug into TSCFP directly; the realistic route is to become a sub-originator or servicer for a bank that holds the ADB risk participation.
- PhilGuarantee cover is for loans, not purchased receivables; a factor structuring its advance as a loan to the seller (with-recourse, receivables as collateral) could in principle sit under a partner bank's PhilGuarantee line, but that is recourse finance, not non-recourse.

- SB Corp "Receivables Financing" program status, terms, and whether non-bank factors can act as conduits: unconfirmed.
- Whether PhilGuarantee has ever guaranteed a financing company's (non-bank) MSME portfolio: not found.

## Key Question 3: Export factoring via FCI two-factor system and Philippine membership

### Takeaway
The FCI two-factor system is the one proven mechanism by which a small emerging-market factor gets true non-recourse buyer-credit cover (the import factor in the buyer's country assumes the credit risk). But the Philippines lost its FCI members after the 1997 crisis, and I could not confirm any current Philippine FCI member in 2025–26; FCI/IFC have been actively courting the Philippines (Manila conference, 2018 FINEX event), and FCI reports new members from the Philippines, but no institution is named.

### Cited Findings
- FCI (4 June 2018): factoring "disappeared" from the Philippines and FCI lost its Philippine members after the 1997 Asian financial crisis; the lack of a sound secured-transactions law hurt factors' recoveries; FCI's Asia Chapter Director presented at a FINEX event on 31 May 2018 to help the Philippines rejoin; the Philippines was an early Asian adopter of international factoring (before China) — [FCI news, 4 Jun 2018](https://fci.nl/en/news/Philippines%20will%20be%20ready%20to%20go%20into%20factoring%20soon?language_content_entity=en)
- IFC/FCI APEC 2020 presentation: the Philippines and Thailand "have passed the law that opened the way for growth"; FCI and IFC ran a factoring promotion conference and an SCF workshop in Manila (November, year per the deck); "two thirds of the new FCI members came from the emerging economies, including Philippines, Bangladesh and Cambodia" — [IFC APEC 2020 FCI deck](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2020/apec-2020-session2-evolving-role-of-factoring-en.pdf). The Philippine member is not named. UNSURE whether that member is still active.
- IFC's 2023/24 Knowledge Guide lists the Philippines among "current reform projects" for factoring/secured-transactions law alongside Azerbaijan, Georgia, Jordan, Ukraine — [IFC Knowledge Guide on Factoring Regulation and Supervision, Oct 2023, p. ~25](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2024/knowledge-guide-on-factoring-regulation-and-supervision-ifc-2024.pdf)
- Two-factor mechanics: the export factor funds and manages the exporter; the import factor evaluates buyer credit, collects, and (under the GRIF) bears the approved credit risk; the parties are linked by an Inter-Factor Agreement and exchange credit-approval requests via edifactoring; typical advance up to 80% — [FCI: How international factoring works](https://fci.nl/en/international-factoring-how-does-it-work?language_content_entity=en); [FCI General Rules for International Factoring, June 2019 version](https://www.garantibbvafactoring.com/assets/uploads/pdf/fci_griff1.pdf); [Credlix explainer](https://www.credlix.com/blogs/fci-membership-and-international-factoring-what-it-means-for-your-export-receivables)
- Two-factor transactions are about 15% of total cross-border factoring; FCI has close to 400 members in 90+ countries — [SME Finance Forum study visit note](https://www.smefinanceforum.org/post/study-visit-to-fci-pioneering-international-factoring-and-knowledge-for-receivables-finance); [FCI Wang Qian interview](https://fci.nl/en/discoverfactoringwangqian?language_content_entity=en)
- Global factoring volume grew 2.7% in 2024 to EUR 3.89 trillion (4,451 companies surveyed); Singapore, India and Taiwan posted double-digit growth; China EUR 713bn (+5%) — [GTR, May 2025](https://www.gtreview.com/news/global/us-factoring-revenues-soar-as-global-turnover-hits-record-highs-fci/); [FCI Annual Review page](https://fci.nl/annual-review). FCI has since released its Annual Review 2026 — [TFG](https://www.tradefinanceglobal.com/wire/fci-unveils-latest-annual-review-of-the-factoring-industry/). The Philippines does not appear in the growth highlights.
- Comparator entry paths: Bangladesh's Eastern Bank and Mercantile Bank joined FCI as (associate) members — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/banking/eastern-bank-joins-fci-member-153748); [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/170854); Vietnam's BIDV launched export factoring under the FCI two-factor system — [FCI news](https://fci.nl/en/news/bidv-launch-export-factoring-under-fci-two-factor-system); Vietnam has eight credit institutions in FCI (four domestic banks, four foreign bank branches) per VNBA, Jan 2026 — [VNBA](https://vnba.org.vn/en/promoting-factoring-services-in-vietnam-18611.htm)

### Inferences
- A small Philippine non-bank factor could in principle apply for FCI associate membership (FCI admits non-bank factors in emerging markets, e.g., Lider Faktoring in Turkey — [FCI news](https://fci.nl/en/news/FCI%20welcomes%20Lider%20Faktoring%20A.S.%20as%20its%20new%20Member%20in%20Turkey)), but import factors extend credit lines to the *export factor* under the Inter-Factor Agreement, so a thinly capitalised operator would get small lines and would need a correspondent bank for FX settlement. UNSURE on current FCI capital/track-record admission criteria.
- Factorable Philippine export receivables: electronics (mostly intra-group/consignment, low factorability), agri-food (coconut, banana, seafood to Japan/US/EU buyers), garments, and BPO/IT-services invoices to US/UK/AU clients. Services receivables are factorable under FCI rules if undisputed and assignable, but I found no source on Philippine BPO receivables factoring specifically. This paragraph is inference, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No confirmed list of Philippine FCI members as of 2025–26 (the FCI member directory was not retrievable). Must check fci.nl member search or ask FCI Asia Chapter directly.
- No data on which Philippine export sectors currently use open-account terms with assignable receivables.

## Key Question 4: How emerging-market factors actually do non-recourse (Turkey, Mexico, India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Kenya/Nigeria)

### Takeaway
Across the comparators, non-recourse at scale exists almost only where the *buyer* is the underwritten party: Mexico's NAFIN Cadenas Productivas and India's TReDS are both non-recourse to the SME seller precisely because large anchors accept the invoices on a platform. Turkey's open-market factoring remained roughly 57–64% with-recourse in the last published splits (2010–2012), and Vietnam did not even permit non-recourse factoring until 1 July 2024. Current (2024–25) recourse/non-recourse splits are not published for any of these markets.

### Cited Findings
- World Bank (Klapper, 2005): in emerging markets recourse is often used because it is hard to assess buyer default risk; "the alternative usually used in emerging markets is for the factor to buy receivables with recourse"; reverse factoring is typically non-recourse because only the anchor buyer's credit is assessed — [World Bank WPS3593](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/844291468321884034/pdf/wps3593.pdf); [World Bank, Dec 2006 (NAFIN case)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/774801468314706544/pdf/389630AF150Klapper0Article01PUBLIC1.pdf)
- IFC Knowledge Guide (Oct 2023): "One of the advantages of reverse factoring is that whether receivables are purchased is determined according to the creditworthiness of the anchor buyer... factoring enables high-risk MSMEs to transfer credit risk to high-quality buyers" (p. 12); India's TReDS "accommodates both traditional factoring as well as reverse factoring. The transactions processed under TReDS are 'without recourse' to small businesses" (Box 2, p. ~18) — [IFC Knowledge Guide, 2023](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2024/knowledge-guide-on-factoring-regulation-and-supervision-ifc-2024.pdf)
- Turkey: BRSA-reported factoring receivables at end-2010 were 57.2% with recourse and 42.8% non-recourse — [ResearchGate: Factoring as a Financing Option in Turkey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251323971_Factoring_as_a_Financing_Option_in_Turkey_A_Comparative_Study); in 2012 "the bulk of transactions, totaling 64%, were with recourse" — [The Secured Lender via InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/FACTORING-TAKES-OFF-IN-TURKEY-%5bSecured-Lender-The%5d-a-402383). Lider Faktoring (FCI member) is "mainly active in with-recourse factoring" and does non-recourse via the supplier-financing method — [FCI news](https://fci.nl/en/news/FCI%20welcomes%20Lider%20Faktoring%20A.S.%20as%20its%20new%20Member%20in%20Turkey). Sector volume: Q1 2024 up ~60% y/y to near TRY 250bn (FKB MFKS data) — [Para Dergi, 6 May 2024](https://www.paradergi.com.tr/finans/2024/05/06/faktoringde-talep-patlamasi-yasaniyor); FKB data show the sector closed 2025 at TRY 1.7 trillion volume, +39.5% — [Ekonomi Gazetesi](https://www.ekonomigazetesi.com/ekonomi/faktoringde-islem-hacmi-ilk-ceyrekte-5346-milyar-liraya-cikti-80363) (UNSURE: figure taken from a search snippet; exact article not fetched). No 2024–25 recourse split published in accessible sources.
- Mexico: NAFIN's Cadenas Productivas is reverse factoring; Monex (participating bank) describes it as factoring "without recourse for the supplier" because once invoices are accepted on the NAFIN platform a commercial dispute is no longer possible — [Monex NAFIN productive chains page](https://monex.com.mx/portal/corporate-banking/20/nafin-productive-chains); NAFIN provides the platform and also funds discounts — [NAFIN Cadenas Productivas PDF](https://www.nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/modulo-de-atencion-virtual/pdf/factoraje/cadenas_productivas.pdf); NAFIN July 2024 results deck shows Cadenas Productivas at MXN 30,987 million, about 7% of 423,379 million placed by NAFIN programs (period UNSURE from snippet) — [NAFIN results, Jul 2024](https://www.nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/Acerca_de_Nafin/logros_resultados/Nafin_JUL_2024_V1.4.pdf). IMF BOPCOM notes Mexico has "no way to separate non-recourse factoring from recourse factoring" in official statistics — [IMF BOPCOM 22/06.1](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2022/pdf/39/22-06a.pdf). IDB Invest (May 2026) found firm-level evidence that reverse factoring raises MSME sales — [IDB Invest study summary](https://www.pulse.bot/finance/news/the-impact-of-reverse-factoring-on-msmes-firm-level-evidence-from-mexico-adeb5c14-fc27-41d9-9463-25ba8eaabe6e/)
- India TReDS: FY24 total across four platforms was 41.6 lakh invoices / INR 1.38 lakh crore — [IMPRI India](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=75105); RXIL alone financed INR 80,500 crore in FY25 for 44,000+ MSMEs, ~35% market share, targeting INR 1.25 lakh crore in FY26 — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/industry/rxil-msme-invoice-financing-2lakh-crore-fy25); M1xchange reported ~INR 78,000 crore throughput in FY25 (+100% y/y) — [M1xchange](https://www.m1xchange.com/?p=13776). All TReDS transactions are without recourse to the MSME seller — [Finnova Advisory](https://finnovaadvisory.com/insights/what-is-reverse-factoring-india); RBI's original TReDS concept paper — [RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/concept-paper-on-trade-receivables-and-credit-exchange-for-financing-of-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-225)
- Vietnam: under the 2017 circular "non-recourse factoring is not allowed" — [IFC APEC 2020 Vietnam SCF deck](https://ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2020/apec-2020-session3-role-non-bank-fis-vietnam-scf-en.pdf); Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN (effective 1 July 2024) now recognises recourse (client = seller) and non-recourse (client = buyer) factoring by commercial banks, finance companies, factoring finance companies and foreign bank branches — [Circular 20/2024 text](https://dulieuphapluat.vn/?p=769627); [LawNet summary](https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/thong-bao-van-ban-moi/cases-where-banks-in-vietnam-shall-reject-providing-factoring-from-july-1-2024-140073.html). FCI put Vietnam factoring at ~USD 1.1bn in 2019 — [IFC APEC deck](https://ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2020/apec-2020-session3-role-non-bank-fis-vietnam-scf-en.pdf); IMARC's USD 16.3bn 2024 estimate uses a different methodology and is not comparable — [IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/vietnam-factoring-market)
- Indonesia: only generic market-research descriptions found; no official recourse split — [Bonafide Research](https://www.bonafideresearch.com/product/6507156126/indonesia-factoring-service-market)
- Kenya/Nigeria: not researched in depth (deprioritised per brief); IFC Knowledge Guide lists Nigeria's factoring law as "Pending" (2023) — [IFC Knowledge Guide](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2024/knowledge-guide-on-factoring-regulation-and-supervision-ifc-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pattern is consistent: open-market SME factoring in developing economies is with-recourse; non-recourse appears where (i) an anchor buyer confirms invoices on a platform (Mexico, India), (ii) an import factor underwrites a foreign buyer (FCI), or (iii) the factor is a bank with its own credit-insurance program. None of the comparators show a small independent factor running insured non-recourse on fragmented domestic SME buyers at scale.
- Credit-loss data for non-recourse books in these markets is not published; the Vietnam ban until mid-2024 and Turkey's recourse majority imply regulators and factors treat buyer risk in domestic SME chains as hard to price.

### Gaps

## Key Question 6: Credit data for buyer underwriting in the Philippines

### Takeaway
A financing company can access CIC credit reports only after submitting its own borrower data for at least six months, and CIC data is thin on corporate trade behaviour; the practical corporate toolkit is SEC filings (AFS/GIS by request through SEC Express, plain-copy fees in the hundreds of pesos as of 2018), PSE disclosures for listed anchors, bureau reports from CIBI/CRIF/TransUnion, and court checks. It is workable for a few dozen large buyers, not for hundreds of SME buyers.

### Cited Findings
- CIC: access to credit reports is limited to the data subject or accredited submitting/accessing entities for legitimate purposes — [CIC: About your credit report](https://cic.gov.ph/about-your-cic-credit-report/). Submitting entities may register as accessing entities "upon continuous submission of at least six months' worth of actual basic credit data of their borrowers" — [CIC press release](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/node/399). Registration runs via the Covered Entity Portal with a MOA, Accessing Entity Information Sheet and board resolution — [CIC 106th accessing entity release](https://creditinfo.gov.ph/press-release-cic-enlists-106th-accessing-entity-enjoins-lenders-use-database-assessing-borrowers)
- CIC-accredited Special Accessing Entities (credit bureaus): CIBI Information, CRIF Philippines, Island Credit Solutions, Juanscore, TransUnion Information Solutions Philippines — [Wikipedia: Credit bureaus in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_bureaus_in_the_Philippines); [CIC 2023-02 revised SAE rules PDF](https://www.creditinfo.gov.ph/sites/default/files/CIC%20Circular%20No.%202023-02_Revised%20Standards%20and%20Rules%20for%20Accreditation%20of%20Special%20Accessing%20Entities%20to%20the%20Credit%20Information%20System%20%28ONAR%20stamped%20received%29%20August%202023_0.pdf); JuanScore accredited March 2024 — [BusinessWorld via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/business-world/20240311/281702619682092)
- TransUnion's CIC Stand-Alone Credit Report is available only to accessing entities that completed CIC accreditation and nominated TransUnion as SAE — [TransUnion PH](https://www.transunion.ph/product/cic-standalone-credit-report)
- CIC does not itself provide credit scores; scoring is a value-added service of SAEs — [CIC press release](https://cic.gov.ph/press-release-cic-mulls-direct-to-consumer-credit-reports-via-accessing-financial-institutions/)
- SEC Express: plain or authenticated copies of AFS, GIS and other filings can be requested online with bank/counter/card payment and courier delivery — [SEC: Request for a Copy of AFS, GIS](https://www.sec.gov.ph/?p=32398); 2020 notice on the web interface and call centre — [Grant Thornton alert, 2020](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/accounting-alerts/2020/SEC-Notice-on-the-Opening-of-SEC-Express-System-and-SEC-Express-Nationwide-Submission/); 2018 Citizen's Charter sample fees: Articles of Incorporation PHP 293.94 plain / 397.44 authenticated; combined Articles and By-Laws PHP 397.44 / 604.44 — [SEC 2018 Citizen's Charter PDF](https://appointment.sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/2018CitizensCharter_SECExpressSystemRequestforCopies.pdf). ## Key Question 7: Fraud and dilution risk in non-recourse; invoice verification; e-invoicing (BIR EIS)

### Takeaway
Non-recourse factors everywhere carve out dilution (disputes, returns, set-off, credit notes) and fraud from the credit cover: the factor bears only *insolvency/protracted default* of an approved buyer, and the seller remains liable for everything else. The core controls are buyer acknowledgment of the assignment and of each invoice, PO-to-invoice matching, and payment into a controlled account. Philippine e-invoicing (BIR EIS) becomes mandatory for Large Taxpayers and e-commerce sellers on 31 Dec 2026, which will eventually give factors a BIR-validated invoice record for anchor-buyer invoices, but not for most SME counterparties.

### Cited Findings
- Non-recourse cover is generally limited to buyer insolvency/credit default, not disputes; factors typically buy credit insurance on the debtors and build the premium into the facility — [SMB Compass, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.smbcompass.com/recourse-vs-non-recourse-factoring-2/); [Spark Finance glossary](https://www.sparkfinance.co.uk/glossary/non-recourse-factoring); [Expertsure UK](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/business-finance/recourse-and-non-recourse-factoring/)
- Fraud typology seen by factors: fictitious invoices for goods never delivered, duplicate financing of the same invoice with several factors, and client–debtor collusion on fabricated paperwork; red flags include sudden invoice-volume spikes, new debtors with no history, duplicate invoice numbers, invoices dated before delivery — [eCapital: identifying fraudulent invoices](https://ecapital.com/blog/how-factoring-companies-identify-fraudulent-invoices/); [Invensis](https://www.invensis.net/blog/factoring-companies-invoice-fraud-losses); [IFA Commercial Factor magazine](https://magazine.factoring.org/magazine-articles/the-hidden-risks-of-fraud-in-factoring-and-invoice-discounting)
- Verification practice: match each invoice to a real PO from a verified customer; check invoice numbers not previously factored; a verification clerk calls the debtor to confirm the amount owed on a specific invoice; confirm payments originate from the debtor's actual bank account — [eCapital invoice verification](https://ecapital.com/financial-term/invoice-verification-process); [IFA: Defusing the Bomb](https://magazine.factoring.org/magazine-articles/2xb77zpzjjazm5d2b54qwltxlzhlav); [ResolvePay](https://resolvepay.com/blog/verification-fraud-factoring). An industry article claims successful verification reduces average non-payment by a factor of three and speeds payment by three days (unverified claim) — [IFA magazine](https://magazine.factoring.org/magazine-articles/the-430-million-emailnbspnbsp)
- Platform-based mitigant: on NAFIN's platform, once invoices are accepted by the anchor "a commercial dispute is no longer possible", which is what allows non-recourse — [Monex](https://monex.com.mx/portal/corporate-banking/20/nafin-productive-chains)
- Under the FCI GRIF the import factor's credit cover excludes disputed receivables; the dispute must be resolved before cover reattaches — [FCI GRIF June 2019](https://www.garantibbvafactoring.com/assets/uploads/pdf/fci_griff1.pdf)
- BIR EIS: under RR 11-2025 as amended by RR 26-2025, the first phase of mandatory e-invoicing effective 31 Dec 2026 covers taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service (generally >PHP 1bn gross sales), large taxpayers under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act, e-commerce sellers (excluding micro), and CAS/CBA users; the BIR's September 2026 issuance (reported as RMC 98-2026) kept the deadline — [Grant Thornton PH, 2026](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2026/bir-issues-guidelines-on-electronic-invoicing-retains-31-dec-2026-deadline/); [BusinessWorld "Let's Talk Tax", 18 May 2026](https://www.bworldonline.com/economy/2026/05/18/750488/the-shift-to-e-invoicing-and-electronic-sales-reporting/); [Daily Tribune, 23 Sep 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/23/bir-sets-31-december-e-invoicing-deadline); [PwC PH 2026](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2026/e-invoicing-when-compliance-outpaces-readiness.html). Only mandated/notified taxpayers can register; the BIR issues a Permit to Transmit; electronic sales reporting to BIR is deferred pending separate guidelines; the 2022 pilot was suspended by late 2023 — [ClearTax PH](https://www.cleartax.com/ph/en/philippines-e-invoicing); [Comarch](https://www.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/philippines-e-invoicing-mandate-extended-new-2026-compliance-deadline/)

### Inferences
- For a Philippine non-recourse program, the practical structure is: (1) notified assignment with the buyer's written acknowledgment (enforceable under the Civil Code and PPSA; UNSURE on PPSA registry mechanics, not researched here); (2) buyer-side invoice approval before purchase (the NAFIN/TReDS model), which converts dilution risk into buyer credit risk; (3) seller warranties and a recourse carve-back for disputes, returns, set-off, fraud and credit notes, i.e., "non-recourse" = insolvency-only; (4) payment to a factor-controlled account. Without buyer approval, the factor should treat the deal as recourse regardless of label.
- BIR EIS helps only where the *seller* is a mandated e-invoicer (large taxpayers), which is the opposite of the SME-seller profile; its value to a factor in 2027+ is in anchor-buyer self-billing or reverse-factoring where the anchor's EIS records can validate invoices. UNSURE whether EIS data will be queryable by third parties at all.

### Gaps
- No Philippine-specific case data on factoring fraud losses or dilution rates.
- No source on third-party access to BIR EIS invoice data.
- Philippine Personal Property Security Act (RA 11057) registry status and how notice-filing of receivables assignments works in practice: not researched in this pass (the IFC guide lists the Philippines as a reform project as of 2023).

## Key Question 8: Economics — non-recourse premium over recourse, and viability at small ticket sizes

### Takeaway
Vendor and broker sources (no regulator data) put the non-recourse surcharge at roughly 25–50 bp of invoice value in competitive markets and up to 0.3–1.5% where credit insurance is layered in; standalone TCI costs 0.1%–1% of insured turnover. At a small Philippine factor's likely scale (tens of millions of pesos), insurer minimum premiums and per-buyer limit fees would dominate, so insured non-recourse is unlikely to be viable below a concentrated, anchor-approved book.

### Cited Findings
- Non-recourse "credit guarantee fee" premium of 25 to 50 basis points over recourse factoring — [eCapital glossary](https://ecapital.com/financial-term/non-recourse-credit-guarantee-fee/); freight-factoring guide: 0.25 to 0.5 percentage points more than recourse on an otherwise identical contract — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/checkpoint/recourse-vs-non-recourse-factoring/); other vendor guides cite 0.3–1% and 0.3–1.5% of invoice value — [Expertsure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/business-finance/recourse-and-non-recourse-factoring/); [Drip Capital India](https://www.dripcapital.com/en-in/resources/finance-guides/recourse-vs-non-recourse-factoring); [SMB Compass, 2026](https://www.smbcompass.com/recourse-vs-non-recourse-factoring-2/). All are marketing sources; none is Philippine.
- Standalone TCI typical cost 0.1%–just over 1.0% of turnover (Atradius); 0.25–0.50% worked example (Allianz Trade); 0.1–0.4% (broker) — [Atradius Group](https://group.atradius.com/our-solutions/credit-insurance); [Allianz Trade factoring explainer](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_TW/resources/resources-archive/factoring.html); [AccountsReceivableInsurance.net](https://www.accountsreceivableinsurance.net/competitive-landscape-trade-credit-insurance/)
- Advance rates under non-recourse typically 85–95% — [eCapital Canada FAQ](https://ecapital.com/en-ca/?p=32476); [TAB Bank](https://www.tabbank.com/resource-center/blog/non-recourse-factoring/)
- Emerging-market practice: recourse used because buyer risk is hard to assess (World Bank, 2005) — [World Bank WPS3593](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/844291468321884034/pdf/wps3593.pdf)

### Inferences
- Illustrative arithmetic (my calculation, not sourced): on a PHP 50m insured turnover book at a 0.5% premium, annual premium is PHP 250k — cheap in percentage terms, but insurer minimum premiums (unknown for PH; commonly set in the low tens of thousands of USD elsewhere, UNSURE) and the insurer's refusal to cover unrated SME buyers mean coverage would attach only to the large buyers the factor could already underwrite itself. The insurance therefore buys *capital relief/credibility*, not risk transfer on the hard part of the book.
- A partial-recourse structure (e.g., factor bears first-loss to X% or insolvency-only cover, seller bears dilution and everything else) is the realistic intermediate: it is how most "non-recourse" is actually written in the comparators, and it needs no insurer.
- Pricing: a Philippine small factor would need to charge the non-recourse surcharge on top of already-high EM discount rates; whether sellers will pay 25–100 bp extra per invoice for insolvency-only cover on blue-chip buyers (whose insolvency risk they perceive as nil) is doubtful; the value proposition for sellers is off-balance-sheet treatment and bank-line headroom, which matters mainly to audited mid-caps, not micro-SMEs.

### Gaps
- No Philippine factoring rate cards (recourse or non-recourse) were found; no Philippine TCI minimum premium.
- No credit-loss data to calibrate a first-loss tranche.

## Overall synthesis for the report writer

1. Insured non-recourse on domestic SME buyers: not feasible for a small operator as of Nov 2026 (no evidence of insurer appetite, no public guarantee, thin buyer data).
2. Anchor-approved reverse factoring (non-recourse to the seller, risk = anchor insolvency): feasible in principle, but the anchors are already served by BPI, UnionBank and now Security Bank/ADB; a small factor's niche is second-tier anchors and sub-PHP 5m tickets the banks ignore, where the "non-recourse" is only as good as the anchor's credit and the factor's own balance sheet.
3. Export factoring via FCI: the cleanest true non-recourse channel, but requires FCI membership and an import-factor line; no current Philippine FCI member confirmed.
4. Partial recourse / insolvency-only cover with full seller liability for dilution, disputes, set-off and fraud is the honest label for what most emerging-market "non-recourse" actually is, and is the recommended structure to design around.
5. Watch items: SB Corp Receivables Financing (announced Jan 2025, unconfirmed), BIR EIS go-live 31 Dec 2026, DPWH arrears clearance, Security Bank–ADB program anchors.
