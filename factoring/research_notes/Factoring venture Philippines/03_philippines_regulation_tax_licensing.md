# Philippines: legal, regulatory, tax and licensing framework for a factoring / receivables-financing business (as of 4 November 2026)

Scope note: Philippines only. Dates are given for every rule. Where sources conflict, both are shown. Nothing here is legal advice; items marked **[LAWYER CHECK]** are points a Philippine lawyer must confirm before money moves. Web-search budget ran out near the end of this research, so a few items (flagged in Gaps) rest on a single secondary source or could not be verified against the primary text.

## 1. Which entity and licence: Financing Company (RA 8556) vs Lending Company (RA 9474) vs bank vs unregulated buyer of receivables

### Takeaway
Factoring and discounting of accounts receivable is expressly within the statutory definition of a "financing company" under RA 8556, so the regulated route is an SEC-licensed **financing company** (stock corporation + SEC Certificate of Authority). A lending company (RA 9474) is defined by lending from own funds and is not the statutory home for factoring. Operating a recurring receivables-purchase business without SEC authority is a criminal offence under RA 8556 §14. New financing companies now need **₱15 million** paid-up capital nationwide under SEC MC 20 s.2026 (effective 1 Aug 2026), which also lifted the 2021-2026 moratorium on new online lending platforms; the moratorium never applied to registering new financing/lending companies as such.

### Cited Findings
- RA 8556 §3(a) defines financing companies as corporations "primarily organized for the purpose of extending credit facilities to consumers and to industrial, commercial, or agricultural enterprises, by direct lending or by discounting or factoring commercial papers or accounts receivable, or by buying and selling contracts, leases, chattel mortgages, or other evidences of indebtedness, or by financial leasing" — [RA 8556, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8556_1998.html)
- RA 8556 §7: the SEC "shall not register the articles of incorporation of any financing company" unless satisfied with compliance; §7(c) bars anyone from holding out as a financing company "unless so authorized under this Act". §14 penalises anyone who engages in financing business "without authority from the Securities and Exchange Commission" with a fine of ₱10,000–₱100,000 and/or imprisonment up to six months, and extends liability to officers/employees who knowingly file false statements — [RA 8556, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8556_1998.html)
- RA 8556 §4: the SEC enforces the Act except insofar as the BSP has supervisory authority under RA 7653 over financing companies with quasi-banking functions; §9 lets financing companies engage in quasi-banking and money-market operations only "with the prior approval of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas", and the same for trust operations and foreign-currency loans — [RA 8556, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8556_1998.html)
- RA 8556 §5 empowers the Monetary Board to prescribe maximum rates for "purchase discounts", lease rentals, fees and charges of financing companies — [RA 8556, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8556_1998.html)
- RA 9474 §3(a) defines a "lending company" as a corporation lending money from its own capital or from funds sourced from "not more than nineteen (19) persons"; it excludes banks, investment houses, savings and loan associations, financing companies, pawnshops, insurance companies and other entities "already regulated by law". §4: "No lending company shall conduct business unless granted an authority to operate by the SEC." §5: minimum paid-in capital ₱1,000,000 (SEC may set higher). §12 penalties: fine ₱10,000–₱50,000 and/or imprisonment of six months to ten years — [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html)
- RA 9474 §3(c) defines a quasi-bank by reference to borrowing from 20 or more lenders for relending or "purchasing of receivables and other obligations" — i.e. funding a receivables book from 20+ lenders is quasi-banking requiring BSP authority — [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html)
- RA 9474 §9(c) authorises the SEC to regulate, restrict or prohibit lending companies' "purchases or sales of receivables with or without recourse basis" — [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html)
- Nicolas & De Vega Law (article dated 18 June 2026): "the typical corporate vehicle used for [invoice discounting / factoring] is a financing company"; the Supreme Court in *Great Asian Sales Center Corp. v. CA*, G.R. No. 105774 (2002) recognised discounting of receivables as a transaction contemplated by the Financing Company Act; the firm's view is that for "a recurring business" the regulated route is "commonly to organize or invest in a domestic financing company" — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)
- Same article: SEC-OGC Opinion No. 24-26 (2024), applying §9(d) of the RA 8556 IRR, states total credit to any single borrower generally may not exceed **30% of the financing company's net worth**; SEC-OGC Opinion No. 24-37 (2024) says financing companies covered by the Revised Code of Corporate Governance need at least five directors — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)
- Two-step licensing: a financing company first registers as a corporation (Certificate of Incorporation) and then obtains a separate Certificate of Authority (CA) from the SEC before operating; the CA fee is reported as 1/10 of 1% of paid-up capital, not less than ₱10,000 for financing companies (₱2,000 for lending) — [Respicio commentary](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/how-to-apply-for-an-sec-certificate-of-authority-in-the-philippines); SEC's own portal statement — [checkwithsec.sec.gov.ph](https://checkwithsec.sec.gov.ph/disclaimer.html)
- SEC Resolution No. 544 s.2016 cut the basic documentary requirements for a financing-company CA from 23 to 15 documents — [Digest.ph reproducing SEC notice](https://www.digest.ph/corporate/sec-reduced-requirements-for-financing-and-lending-companies); [Inquirer 2016](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=214838)
- A CA lapses if operations do not begin within 120 days of grant: the SEC revoked Fast Coin Lending's CA in Jan 2023 for failing to start lending within that window — [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/384137/sec-cancels-license-of-fast-coin-lending)
- **SEC MC No. 20 s.2026** (signed 7 July 2026 by Chair Francis Lim; effective 1 Aug 2026): "Guidelines Prescribing Prudential Disclosure and Market Conduct Requirements for Financing and Lending Companies and Lifting the Moratorium on Online Lending Platforms". It replaces MC 10 s.2021 (the OLP moratorium in force since 5 Nov 2021). Minimum paid-up capital for **new financing companies ₱15 million and new lending companies ₱5 million**, "uniformly across the country ... regardless of the main office location or the number and locations of physical branch offices"; cap of five OLPs per company; each OLP is a distinct borrower-facing brand/app — [Philstar, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/07/10/2541007/moratorium-new-online-lending-firms-lifted-august)
- MC 20 tiering for companies operating OLPs: FCs ₱20M (1 OLP) / ₱40M / ₱60M / ₱80M / ₱100M (5 OLPs); LCs ₱10M / ₱20M / ₱30M / ₱40M / ₱50M; one CA per entity covers principal office, branches and all platforms; loan proceeds may not be released without borrower's explicit confirmation of final terms; phone contacts may not be treated as guarantors absent written consent; existing companies keeping their platform count need not add capital, but have one year from effectivity to meet the tier if they expand, otherwise must cut platforms — [CloudCFO](https://cloudcfo.ph/blog/sec-lifts-online-lending-platform-moratorium). Fintechnews reports the same ₱15M FC base (no OLP) rising to ₱100M at five OLPs, plus mandatory Credit Information Corporation registration for OLP operators, and that "lifting the moratorium doesn't mean automatic approval" — [Fintechnews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/72397/lending/sec-online-lending-platforms-moratorium-lifted/). (Fintechnews' LC arithmetic — "₱5M base rising by ₱10M per platform to ₱50M" — does not reconcile; CloudCFO's ₱10M-per-OLP table does. Treat the primary circular as controlling.)
- The 2021–2026 moratorium applied to the recording of new **online lending platforms**, including new OLPs of existing FCs/LCs; press reports describe it only in those terms — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/07/10/2541007/moratorium-new-online-lending-firms-lifted-august); [Fintechnews.ph](https://fintechnews.ph/72397/lending/sec-online-lending-platforms-moratorium-lifted/). Searches for a moratorium on new FC/LC company registrations found only revocations/enforcement, not a freeze (e.g., SEC had revoked the primary registration of 2,084 lending companies for RA 9474 non-compliance as of 2023; SEC MC 19 s.2023 gives delinquent firms six months to comply) — [Manila Bulletin 2023](https://mb.com.ph/2023/8/13/sec-revokes-populus-lending-license); [Tribune, June 2025](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/06/16/sec-shuts-down-401-lenders)
- Pre-MC 20 statutory floors (RA 8556 §6 as amended by RA 10881): ₱10M Metro Manila and first-class cities, ₱5M other cities, ₱2.5M municipalities; "the Securities and Exchange Commission may adjust said minimum paid-up levels" — [RA 10881, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2016/ra_10881_2016.html)

### Inferences
- A B2B factoring business buying or discounting Philippine receivables on a recurring basis should plan on a financing-company CA; the lending-company route does not fit the statutory definition (no discounting/factoring language, and §9(c) signals SEC may restrict LC receivables purchases). An unlicensed "pure purchaser" structure faces RA 8556 §14 criminal exposure and the SEC's broad "holding out" prohibition; no source found suggests the SEC treats outright purchase of receivables as outside the Act. **[LAWYER CHECK]** whether a single, non-recurring purchase or an offshore purchaser with no Philippine presence is outside RA 8556.
- Budget capital at ₱15M (no app) or ₱20M+ (with one borrower-facing app) for a new entity formed after 1 Aug 2026, plus net-worth headroom because of the 30%-of-net-worth single-borrower limit (a ₱15M company could not take more than ₱4.5M exposure to one client).
- Funding the book from ≥20 lenders (notes, crowdfunding, etc.) triggers quasi-banking and BSP licensing; stay ≤19 funders or use equity/bank lines.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the primary text of SEC MC 20 s.2026 (sec.gov.ph PDFs returned 403/404); figures rest on three press/advisory summaries that agree on the ₱15M/₱5M base but differ on LC tier arithmetic.
- Current CGFD documentary checklist, statutory processing time and whether eSPARC is the filing channel were not found (only the 2016 15-document reform). Practitioners should request the current checklist from SEC CGFD.
- The 1999 IRR of RA 8556 (and any 2019+ amendment) was not retrieved; the 30% single-borrower limit is cited via an SEC-OGC opinion summarised by a law firm.
- No SEC opinion was found squarely addressing whether a one-off or non-recourse "true sale" purchase of receivables requires a CA.

## 2. Foreign ownership and foreign directors/officers

### Takeaway
Since RA 10881 (17 July 2016) both financing companies and lending companies "may be owned up to one hundred percent (100%) by foreign nationals", subject only to constitutional limits on land. A US citizen can therefore own 100% and sit on the board/serve as president; the corporate secretary must be a Filipino resident and the treasurer a resident.

### Cited Findings
- RA 10881 (approved 17 July 2016) amended RA 9474 §6: "A lending company may be owned up to one hundred percent (100%) by foreign nationals"; and RA 8556 §6 so that financing companies "may be up to 100% foreign-owned". Where loans are secured by land, a foreign-majority company may bid at the foreclosure sale and take possession but cannot take title, and may transfer its rights to qualified Filipinos within five years; §3 repeals nationality requirements for adjustment companies, investment houses, lending and financing companies "subject to the provisions of this Act and the Constitution" — [RA 10881, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2016/ra_10881_2016.html)
- Pre-2016 caps, for context: original RA 8556 §6 required ≥40% Filipino voting stock (i.e., 60% foreign max) and reciprocity; original RA 9474 §6 required Filipino majority (49% foreign max) — [RA 8556, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8556_1998.html); [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html). Several secondary sites still quote the old 40%/60% limits (e.g. [Accountaholics](https://accountaholicsph.com/requirements-for-setting-up-a-lending-or-financing-company-in-the-ph/)); these are superseded.
- Law-firm confirmation that 100% foreign ownership of financing companies is now allowed, with the land caveat — [NDV Law, 2026](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/); [NDV Law on lifting restrictions](https://ndvlaw.com/lifting-foreign-ownership-restrictions-in-the-philippines/)
- SEC-OGC Opinion No. 14-05: a 100% foreign-owned corporation in a non-nationalised activity "is entitled to have foreign officers" and foreign directors; citizenship requirements for board/officers apply only if the activity is subject to foreign-ownership restrictions — [SEC Opinion 14-05 (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/413235205/Opinion-No-14-05-pdf)
- The corporate secretary must be a Philippine citizen and resident; the treasurer must be a resident (citizenship depends on sector/SEC practice); under the Anti-Dummy Law foreigners cannot be officers in corporations subject to foreign-equity limits — [Respicio commentary](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-foreign-director-serve-as-corporate-secretary-or-treasurer-2); [Triple i Consulting, 2026](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/can-foreigner-corporate-treasurer-philippines/); [NDV Law on Anti-Dummy nominee risk](https://ndvlaw.com/the-anti-dummy-law-in-the-philippines-why-filipino-nominee-directors-and-stockholders-risk-criminal-prosecution/)
- AML coverage used to turn on >40% foreign participation or ₱10M capital; SEC MC 4 s.2021 removed that threshold so all FCs/LCs are covered persons regardless of foreign equity (see §7 below) — [SEC MC 4 s.2021 (ADB copy)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/philippines-2021-mc-no-04.pdf)

### Inferences
- No Filipino partner is legally required for a financing company. A Filipino-majority structure would only be relevant to hold land (not needed for factoring) or for market/relationship reasons. Nominee-Filipino "dummy" structures carry criminal risk and are unnecessary here.
- A US citizen founder present in Manila for six months would still need an appropriate visa/work authorisation to act as an officer; corporate officers (president, secretary, treasurer) are exempt from the Alien Employment Permit per one source, but immigration status is a separate matter **[LAWYER CHECK]**.

### Gaps
- The Foreign Investment Negative List treatment was not pulled from the primary list; the RA 10881 statutory text is dispositive, but confirm the current (12th) RFINL contains no contrary entry.
- Work visa (9(g)/SVEG) requirements for a foreign officer were not researched.

## 3. Assignment of receivables: perfection, enforceability, PPSA, registry, priority, anti-assignment clauses

### Takeaway
Two overlapping regimes apply. Under the Civil Code (Arts. 1624–1635) an assignment binds the debtor only once the debtor has knowledge/notice (Art. 1626) and binds third persons only if in a public (notarised) instrument (Art. 1625). The Personal Property Security Act (RA 11057, 2018) expressly brings **outright buyers of accounts receivable** within "security interest" for registration and priority, so a factor should also register a notice in the LRA's Personal Property Security Registry (operational since Feb 2025); priority runs from time of registration. PPSA §10 voids contractual anti-assignment clauses for trade receivables.

### Cited Findings
- Civil Code Art. 1624: assignment of credits is perfected under Art. 1475 (meeting of minds on object and price). Art. 1625: an assignment "shall produce no effect as against third persons" unless it appears in a public instrument (or, for real property, is recorded in the Registry of Property). Art. 1626: a debtor who pays the creditor before having knowledge of the assignment is released. Art. 1627: assignment carries accessory rights (guaranties, mortgages, pledges, preferences). Art. 1628: a good-faith assignor warrants the existence and legality of the credit at the time of sale but not the debtor's solvency unless stipulated or insolvency was prior and public; liability limited to price received plus expenses; bad-faith assignor liable for all damages. Art. 1629: a solvency guarantee without stated duration lasts one year (or one year from maturity). Art. 1634: a debtor may extinguish a litigated credit by reimbursing the assignee the price paid plus costs and interest within 30 days of demand — [Civil Code, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html)
- Supreme Court (1989) doctrine: the debtor's consent is not necessary; what the law requires is notice to the debtor — [Chanrobles, 1989 decision](https://chanrobles.com/cralaw/1989augustdecisions.php?id=643)
- RA 11057 (PPSA) §3(j): "security interest" is a property right in collateral securing an obligation "regardless of whether the parties have denominated it as a security interest" and "includes the right of a buyer of accounts receivable"; §3(c) "grantor" includes "a transferor in an outright transfer of an accounts receivable"; §3(i) "secured creditor" "for the purposes of registration and priority only ... includes a buyer of account receivable" — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- PPSA §5–6: a security interest is created by a written security agreement signed by the parties. §12: perfection by registration of a notice, possession, or control. §16–17: priority among competing interests is determined by "time of registration of a notice or perfection by other means", regardless of order of creation — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- PPSA §10: a security interest in a receivable is effective notwithstanding an agreement between grantor and account debtor limiting the grantor's right to create it; "Any stipulation limiting the grantor's right to create a security interest shall be void", though the grantor remains liable to the debtor for breach; §10 covers receivables from supply/lease of goods or services (other than financial services), construction/real-property contracts, and IP sale/lease/licence — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- PPSA §48(a): on default the secured creditor may, without judicial process, instruct the account debtor to pay it directly and apply the proceeds after deducting reasonable collection expenses; on request it must give the account debtor evidence of its interest — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- PPSA §66 repeals, among others, Chattel Mortgage Law §§1–16, Civil Code Arts. 2085–2123, 2127, 2140–2141, 2241, 2243, 2246–2247, and "Section 13 of Republic Act No. 5980, as amended by Republic Act No. 8556" (the Financing Company Act's registry provision) — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- PPSA §55(d)/§57/§68: the "transitional period" runs from effectivity until the Registry is established and operational; implementation is conditioned on the Registry — [RA 11057, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html)
- Registry status: the LRA launched the Personal Property Security Registry (PPSR) on 13 Feb 2025 with DOF, DOJ and the Bankers Association; registry at https://ppsr.lra.gov.ph/; "Accounts receivable" is listed among registrable property types; registrants create individual or juridical-entity accounts; six notice types (register, search, amend, terminate, certify, auction); fees apply but amounts not stated — [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/land-registration-authority-launches-the-personal-property-security-registry/); [LRA PPSR announcement, 2 Feb 2025](https://ppsr.lra.gov.ph/2025/02/02/introducing-the-personal-property-security-registry-ppsr-a-fresh-chapter-for-property-security-and-transparency/). An earlier 2021 "launch" (25 Mar 2021) was described by one firm as not yet fully operational — [GQ Law](https://www.gqlaw.com.ph/Home/ArticleSingle/6)
- SEC MC 18 s.2019 and MC 20 s.2026 require borrower notification/consent for collection contacts (see §6), which interacts with notice-of-assignment practice — [SEC MC 18 s.2019 (ADB copy)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/philippines-2019-mc-no-18.pdf)
- Market practice documents for a Philippine factoring programme: master receivables purchase/factoring agreement, deed of assignment per batch, with-recourse undertaking, notice of assignment to account debtors, security documents — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/factoring-companies-the-sec-regulatory-setup-for-foreign-investment-in-invoice-discounting-in-the-philippines/)

### Inferences
- Belt-and-braces perfection for a factor: (1) written assignment/purchase agreement; (2) notarise it (public instrument) to satisfy Art. 1625 against third persons; (3) register a PPSR notice to fix priority against competing assignees and the seller's secured lenders; (4) serve notice of assignment on the account debtor so payment to the seller no longer discharges (Art. 1626) and to enable direct collection (PPSA §48). Disclosed factoring is legally cleaner than undisclosed.
- Because PPSA treats a buyer of receivables as a "secured creditor" only "for registration and priority", the true-sale/insolvency characterisation remains a Civil Code/FRIA question (see §9) **[LAWYER CHECK]**.
- Anti-assignment clauses in Philippine commercial supply contracts should not defeat the factor's interest (PPSA §10), but the seller may be in breach of its customer contract; government contracts are different (see §9).

### Gaps
- PPSA IRR (2019) text and LRA fee schedule were not retrieved; the exact PPSR fee and whether the transitional period has been formally declared ended (and the re-registration deadline for pre-PPSA interests) could not be confirmed.
- No post-2018 Supreme Court decision applying PPSA to competing receivables assignees was found.

## 4. Taxes

### Takeaway
A financing company is a non-bank financial intermediary not performing quasi-banking functions: its interest, discounts and similar income are subject to **5% gross receipts tax (GRT)** under NIRC §122 (1% / 0% for longer maturities) instead of VAT; corporate income tax is 25% (20% for small corporations) under CREATE; DST of ₱1.50 per ₱200 applies to debt instruments (NIRC §179, pro-rated for terms under a year), while the Supreme Court held in *Philacor* (2013) that a mere assignment of promissory notes is not itself subject to DST. How a factoring discount is characterised (interest vs purchase discount) matters for GRT timing and withholding and must be confirmed by a tax adviser.

### Cited Findings
- NIRC §122: "a tax of five percent (5%)" on gross receipts of "all finance companies, as well as ... other financial intermediaries not performing quasi-banking functions" from interest, discounts and other items treated as gross income; income from lending/financial leasing is taxed by remaining maturity: ≤2 years 5%, >2–4 years 3%, >4–7 years 1%, >7 years 0%; pretermination re-reckons the maturity; the Commissioner may impose the same tax on "persons performing similar financing activities". §121 applies the same schedule to banks and NBFIs performing quasi-banking — [NIRC Title V, Chanrobles](https://chanrobles.com/legal6title21.htm)
- BIR RMC 69-2003 addresses the GRT base under §§121–122; "passed-on" GRT received by a financing company forms part of its §122 taxable base — [RMC 69-2003 (Lawyerly)](https://dev.lawyerly.ph/laws/47497); [BusinessWorld via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/business-world/20170612/281552290838257)
- NIRC §179 as amended by TRAIN (RA 10963, effective 1 Jan 2018): DST of "One peso and fifty centavos (₱1.50) on each Two hundred pesos (₱200)" of the issue price of debt instruments; for terms under one year, "a proportional amount in accordance with the ratio of its term in number of days to three hundred sixty-five (365) days"; "only one documentary stamp tax shall be imposed on either loan agreement, or promissory notes issued to secure such loan" — [RA 10963, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2017/ra_10963_2017.html)
- *Philacor Credit Corp. v. CIR*, G.R. No. 169899 (6 Feb 2013): a finance company was not liable for DST on the assignment of promissory notes; DST on debt instruments attaches to issuance/renewal, not assignment, and the Court contrasted provisions that expressly tax assignments (§§176, 178, 198) — [Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/39367); [digest](https://jur.ph/jurisprudence/digest/philacor-credit-corp-v-commissioner-of-internal-revenue)
- NIRC §198 taxes assignments or transfers of any mortgage, lease or policy of insurance, and renewals/continuances of evidence of indebtedness, at the same rate as the original instrument — [digest.ph DST regulations](https://www.digest.ph/taxation/revised-documentary-stamp-tax-regulations) (secondary; verify)
- The Supreme Court has treated instructional letters, journal and cash vouchers evidencing intercompany advances as loan agreements subject to DST (broad §179 application) — [Philstar tax column](https://www.philstar.com/business/2017/06/19/1711667/whats-name-revisiting-doctrine-documentary-stamp-taxes-and-intercompany-advances)
- CREATE MORE (RA 12066, Nov 2024) offers a 20% CIT rate to registered business enterprises under the Enhanced Deductions Regime; a secondary tracker states CREATE MORE did not change DST rates — [Business Registration Philippines](https://businessregistrationphilippines.com/taxes-philippines-guide-for-businesses-investors/); [Respicio DST table](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/documentary-stamp-tax-payment-process-in-the-philippines)
- DST returns for monthly filers are due within five days after the close of the month of the taxable document — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/how-to-compute-documentary-stamp-tax-in-the-philippines)
- RA 9474 §3(c) / BSP: borrowing from 20+ lenders is quasi-banking; interest on such "deposit substitutes" attracts final withholding tax regimes designed for banks/quasi-banks — [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html)

### Inferences
- Economics: on a 60-day invoice discounted at, say, 2% flat, the factor's 5% GRT applies to the discount income (not to the face value), and no VAT is charged to the client (services subject to percentage tax are VAT-exempt); confirm with the BIR/adviser whether a "purchase discount" on a true sale is "interest/discount from lending activities" (5%) or "other items of gross income" (also 5% under §122) — either way 5% in the ≤2-year bucket.
- DST planning: if the factoring facility is documented as a loan/advance with a promissory note from the client (with-recourse), §179 DST of 0.75% annualised (pro-rated by days) applies to each drawdown; if documented as an outright assignment of invoices, *Philacor* supports no §179 DST on the assignment itself, but §198 may bite if any mortgage/lease/insurance policy is assigned alongside **[LAWYER/TAX CHECK]**.

### Gaps
- No BIR ruling or CTA case was found specifically characterising factoring discounts (true sale vs loan) for income-tax or GRT purposes. Search budget ran out before this could be pursued.
- Withholding-tax treatment of interest/discount paid by clients to a financing company (creditable EWT rates under RR 2-98 as amended) was not verified.
- Local business tax (LGU) on financing companies and the BIR registration/ATP requirements were not researched.
- The primary text of NIRC §198/§199 (exemptions) was not retrieved (lawphil TRAIN page truncated).

## 5. Interest-rate and fee rules; Truth in Lending; RA 11765; usury

### Takeaway
There is no general interest ceiling (Usury Law suspended since 1983; courts strike only "unconscionable" rates). The SEC's caps (MC 3 s.2022; recalibrated by MC 14 s.2025 effective 1 April 2026: 6% nominal/month, 12% effective/month, 5% penalty/month, 100% total-cost cap) apply only to **unsecured general-purpose loans ≤ ₱10,000 with tenor ≤ 4 months**, so B2B receivables finance is outside them. The Truth in Lending Act requires written disclosure of finance charges (including "discounts") and the simple annual rate. RA 11765 defines "financial consumer" to include juridical persons, so SME/B2B clients are covered by the SEC's consumer-protection rules (MC 5 s.2023).

### Cited Findings
- Central Bank Circular 905 (adopted 22 Dec 1982, effective 1 Jan 1983) lifted Usury Law ceilings; the Supreme Court holds it "simply suspended the latter's effectivity" and that interest "whenever unconscionable may still be declared illegal" (e.g. *Spouses Castro v. Tan*; 6%/month called "outrageous and inordinate") — [Batasnatin doctrine note](https://batasnatin.com/doctrine/civil-credit-usury-suspension-bsp-circular-905); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/guide-to-usury-laws-and-interest-rate-limits)
- Legal interest for forbearance/judgments is 6% p.a. per *Nacar v. Gallery Frames* (G.R. 189871, 2013) implementing BSP MB Circular 799 (21 June 2013) — [Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36569); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/legal-interest-rate-limits-and-usury-law-philippines). (One source attributes the 6% to Circular 794; most cite 799.)
- SEC MC 3 s.2022 (effective 3 Mar 2022) implemented BSP Circular 1133 (2021): for unsecured general-purpose loans ≤ ₱10,000 with tenor ≤ 4 months, nominal rate ≤ 6%/month, effective rate ≤ 15%/month, late penalty ≤ 5%/month, total cost ≤ 100% of principal; fines ₱25k/₱50k (LC) and ₱50k/₱100k (FC) for first/second offence, CA suspension/revocation on third — [SEC press release, 1 Mar 2022](https://www.sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2022PR_SEC-IMPLEMENTS-INTEREST-RATE-CAP-ON-LOANS-OFFERED-BY-LENDING-FINANCING-COMPANIES-THEIR-ONLINE-LENDING-PLATFORMS-03012022.pdf); [MC 3 s.2022 text (ADB copy)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/philippines-2022-mc-no-03.pdf); [Ocampo & Suralvo](https://www.ocamposuralvo.com/2022/03/11/sec-sets-ceilings-on-interest-rates-and-other-fees-charged-by-lending-companies-financing-companies-and-their-online-lending-platforms/)
- SEC MC 14 s.2025 (issued 10 Dec 2025; applies to loans entered into, restructured or renewed from 1 April 2026): same scope (unsecured, general-purpose, ≤ ₱10,000, ≤ 4 months); EIR ceiling lowered to 12%/month; NIR 6%/month; penalties 5%/month; total cost cap 100%; anti-circumvention bans loan splitting, tenor shifting, sham collateral; sanctions ₱50,000 first offence, up to ₱1M and/or 60-day suspension second, revocation third; issued under RA 11765 §6(a) — [NDV Law explainer, 2 Jan 2026](https://ndvlaw.com/ceilings-on-interest-rates-for-unsecured-loans-not-exceeding-p10000-an-explainer-on-the-recalibrated-ceilings-on-interest-rates-and-other-fees-charged-by-financing-companies-and-lending-companies-s/); [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/969232/sec-lowers-interest-rate-caps-for-loans-up-to-p10-000/story/); [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/12/12/interest-rate-caps-for-small-short-term-loans)
- Truth in Lending Act (RA 3765, 1963): "credit" includes loans, mortgages, conditional sales, leases and "any purchase, or other acquisition of" obligations or claims arising from them; "finance charge" includes "interest, fees, service charges, discounts"; §4 requires a written statement before consummation of cash price, down payment, amount financed, finance charge in pesos and as a simple annual rate on the unpaid balance; §6 civil penalty of the greater of ₱100 or twice the finance charge (max ₱2,000 per transaction, one-year prescriptive period) plus attorney's fees; criminal fine ₱1,000–₱5,000 and/or 6–12 months — [RA 3765, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1963/ra_3765_1963.html). RA 9474 §7 makes TILA and the Consumer Act expressly applicable to lending companies — [RA 9474, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9474_2007.html)
- RA 11765 (signed 6 May 2022) §3(a): "financial consumer" is a person or entity, "natural or juridical", who is a purchaser, lessee or recipient (or prospective) of a financial product or service; §3(c) financial products include credit; §4 applies to all financial products offered by any financial service provider; §6 gives BSP/SEC/IC rule-making (including reviewing "reasonableness of interest and fees"), market-conduct examination, enforcement (fines, disqualification of officers, CDOs, disgorgement) and adjudication of purely civil money claims ≤ ₱10,000,000; §8 requires clear disclosure before contracting, a cooling-off policy if the regulator requires one, and the borrower's right to prepay (fees must be disclosed); §15 criminal penalties 1–5 years and/or ₱50,000–₱2,000,000; §16 administrative fines (for investment fraud ₱50,000–₱10M per instance plus daily fines; up to 3× profit) — [RA 11765, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2022/ra_11765_2022.html)
- SEC MC 5 s.2023 (issued 25 Apr 2023, effective 14 May 2023) implements RA 11765 for SEC-supervised FSPs including financing and lending companies: board oversight, a Consumer Protection Risk Management System, standards on transparency/responsible pricing, fair treatment, no abusive collection, data privacy and information security, a complaints-assistance mechanism, and (if the SEC requires it) a cooling-off period of not less than three business days; SEC may restrict excessive interest/fees, order disgorgement and sue on consumers' behalf — [Legal500 / Quisumbing Torres summary](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/philippines/consumer-protection/sec-issues-regulations-to-implement-financial-consumer-protection-act)
- Enforcement examples: June 2023 CDOs against six lenders for unfair collection; June 2024 CDOs against six FCs/LCs for failing to submit the annual Impact Evaluation Report (due 15 Jan each year from 2023) — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/corporate/2024/06/26/604142/cease-and-desist-orders-issued-vs-6-financing-lending-firms); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2023/6/30/sec-orders-6-lending-firms-to-stop-unfair-collection-methods)

### Inferences
- Pricing a B2B factoring facility at commercial rates (e.g. 1.5–3%/month equivalent) is lawful, but a court can still reduce a rate it finds unconscionable; keep disclosure clean and rates defensible.
- Because RA 11765's "financial consumer" includes juridical persons, SME clients of a financing company fall under MC 5 s.2023 obligations (disclosure, complaints desk, fair collection), even though the ₱10,000 rate caps do not apply to them.
- TILA disclosure applies to discounting (the Act lists "discounts" as finance charges and "acquisition of claims" as credit); provide a TILA disclosure statement for each facility.

### Gaps
- Whether the SEC has exercised the cooling-off power for financing companies' commercial credit was not confirmed.
- Primary text of MC 5 s.2023 and MC 14 s.2025 not retrieved (sec.gov.ph blocked).

## 6. Collection and enforcement: SEC MC 18 s.2019, Data Privacy, courts, arbitration, practical reality

### Takeaway
Collection conduct is tightly policed: SEC MC 18 s.2019 bans threats, shaming, false statements, calls before 6 a.m./after 10 p.m. and contacting anyone in a borrower's contact list other than named guarantors/co-makers, with the FC fully liable for outsourced collectors; NPC Circular 20-01 (2020, amended 2022-02) bars harvesting phone/social-media contacts. In court, money claims up to ₱1,000,000 go through final-and-unappealable small-claims procedure (since 11 April 2022) and ₱1–2M through summary procedure; a standard commercial dispute took ~962 days and cost 31% of the claim under Doing Business 2020. Arbitration under RA 9285 is available if contracted.

### Cited Findings
- SEC MC 18 s.2019 (dated 19/20 Aug 2019, published 23 Aug 2019, effective 15 days after publication), addressed to "all financing and lending companies": FCs, LCs and third-party service providers "must observe good faith and reasonable conduct"; unfair practices are (a) use or threat of violence or criminal means, (b) threats of action that cannot legally be taken, (c) obscenities/insults, (d) disclosure or publication of borrowers' names/personal information, (e) communicating false loan information including failing to say the debt is disputed, (f) false representation or deceptive means, (g) contact before 6:00 a.m. or after 10:00 p.m. unless the account is >15 days past due or the borrower consented, (h) "Notwithstanding the borrower's consent, contacting the persons in the borrower's contact list other than those who were named as guarantors or co-makers". §2 lists permitted disclosures (consent, credit bureaus/other lenders, court order, collection agencies/counsel, service providers, insurers). §3: outsourced collectors are agents and "ultimate responsibility ... remain[s] with the FCs and LCs". §4: collectors must disclose their true identity; a customer-service unit is required. §5 penalties: LC ₱25,000 / ₱50,000; FC ₱50,000 / ₱100,000 for first/second offence; third offence fine up to ₱1M, 60-day suspension or CA revocation; violations counted per loan per complainant; progression lapses after 3 years — [SEC MC 18 s.2019 (ADB LPR copy, read from scanned PDF)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/philippines-2019-mc-no-18.pdf)
- MC 20 s.2026 adds that phone contacts cannot be treated as guarantors or pursued unless they gave written consent to that role — [CloudCFO](https://cloudcfo.ph/blog/sec-lifts-online-lending-platform-moratorium)
- NPC Circular 20-01 (14 Sep 2020; amended by NPC Circular 2022-02 on 1 Dec 2022): lending/financing companies may collect only personal data needed for KYC and creditworthiness; may not require access to or copy phone, e-mail or social-media contacts for collection or harassment; must dispose of lists obtained in violation; must adopt retention policies for denied applicants and fully paid borrowers; NPC has sanctioned a lender whose app requested contact-list access at application (NPC SS 21-008, 2023) — [NPC Circular 20-01](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/NPC-Circular-No.-20-01.pdf); [NPC Circular 2022-02](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/NPC-Circular-No.-2022-%E2%80%93-02-Amending-20-01-Loan-Related-Transactions.pdf); [NPC decision](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NPC-SS-21-008-2023.09.26-O.pdf); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1319398/lenders-beware-national-privacy-commission-strengthens-guidelines-on-processing-of-borrower-information)
- Rules on Expedited Procedures in the First Level Courts (A.M. No. 08-8-7-SC, approved 1 Mar 2022, effective 11 Apr 2022): small claims for payment/reimbursement of money up to ₱1,000,000 (exclusive of interest and costs) nationwide; decisions are "final, executory and unappealable"; claims over ₱1M up to ₱2M proceed in first-level courts under summary procedure — [Supreme Court announcement](https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/?p=32628); [Respicio 2026](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/what-is-the-small-claims-court-limit-in-the-philippines-in-2026); [Batasnatin overview](https://batasnatin.com/laws/expedited-overview-am-08-8-7-sc-2022)
- World Bank Doing Business 2020: enforcing a standardised commercial contract in the Philippines took **962 days**, cost **31% of claim value**, judicial-quality score 7.5/18, rank 152nd; East Asia-Pacific average ~580 days; Singapore 164 days — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/easing-doing-business-in-the-philippines/); [Rappler](https://rappler.com/voices/thought-leaders/analysis-point-of-law-will-ease-of-doing-business-law-do-trick); [Doing Business PHL profile (PDF)](https://croatian.doingbusiness.org/content/dam/doingBusiness/country/p/philippines/PHL-LITE.pdf)
- FRIA Section 18(c): a rehabilitation stay order does not bar enforcement against sureties, solidary co-debtors, third-party/accommodation mortgagors and L/C issuers (relevant to with-recourse structures and personal guarantees) — [RA 10142, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_10142_2010.html)
- Arbitration: the Alternative Dispute Resolution Act (RA 9285, 2004) governs domestic and international commercial arbitration in the Philippines — [RA 9285, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2004/ra_9285_2004.html) (statute cited for existence; details not researched)

### Inferences
- For a B2B factor, most collection is against account debtors (corporates), but MC 18/NPC rules still govern any contact with individuals (sole proprietors, guarantors). Written collection policies, 6 a.m.–10 p.m. windows, agent-identity disclosure and a complaints desk are required from day one.
- Design facilities so individual invoices or facility balances fall within the ₱1M small-claims ceiling where possible; larger disputes should carry an arbitration clause (or at least venue/venue-waiver clauses) and personal/corporate guarantees that can be pursued despite a rehabilitation stay.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 empirical data on average small-claims disposition time was found.
- Court filing-fee schedules and the cost of execution (sheriff's fees) were not researched.

## 7. AML/KYC: AMLA coverage, AMLC registration, customer due diligence

### Takeaway
All financing and lending companies are AMLA "covered persons" via SEC supervision; since SEC MC 4 s.2021 the old 40%-foreign/₱10M-capital threshold is gone, every FC/LC must register with the AMLC's online reporting portal, run a risk-based ML/TF Prevention Program under SEC MC 16 s.2018, train staff and keep proof for SEC examiners.

### Cited Findings
- SEC MC 16 s.2018 (2018 AML/CFT Guidelines for SEC-covered institutions) as amended by **SEC MC 4 s.2021**: removed the requirement that FCs/LCs have >40% foreign participation or ≥₱10M paid-up capital to be covered; "all financing and lending companies are now covered, regardless of foreign equity or paid-up capital"; must register with the AMLC online reporting system (unregistered firms given two months to submit proof to SEC's AML Division) and adopt a comprehensive risk-based Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Program aligned with the AMLA, TFPSA and their IRRs — [SEC MC 4 s.2021 (ADB copy)](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/philippines-2021-mc-no-04.pdf); [Manila Bulletin, Apr 2021](https://mb.com.ph/2021/4/5/sec-all-lending-firms-must-register-with-amlc); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=372993)
- SEC 2024 notice: all financing and lending companies must submit proof of AMLC registration; failure to register/update is penalised under MC 16 — [SEC Notice, 3 Jun 2024](https://sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/2024Notice-on-AMLC-Registration_03Jun2024.pdf); [SEC Notice 2023](https://www.sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2023Notice_AMLC-REG.pdf)
- SEC 2024 FAQ: proof of AML training must be kept available for SEC examiners under §11.5 of MC 16 — [SEC FAQ on AMLC training](https://sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024Notice-on-FAQs-on-AMLC-Training-Requirement_updated.pdf)
- Penalties under the 2018 Guidelines reported as fines of ₱10,000 to ₱1 million (snippet truncated) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2021/4/5/sec-all-lending-firms-must-register-with-amlc)
- Overview of the AMLC Registration and Reporting Guidelines — [Tookitaki summary](https://www.tookitaki.com/compliance-hub/amlc-registration-and-reporting-guidelines-an-overview) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Budget for an AML compliance officer, a written MTPP, AMLC portal registration (before launch), CTR/STR reporting capability and annual training. For B2B factoring, CDD must cover both the client (seller) and, for disclosed facilities, verification of account debtors.

### Gaps
- Primary text of SEC MC 16 s.2018, the AMLC Registration & Reporting Guidelines, and current CTR thresholds were not retrieved.

## 8. Credit insurance and guarantees

### Takeaway
PhilGuarantee (the state guarantor) runs MSME loan-guarantee programmes (historically 50–80% cover, 1% p.a. fee, up to ₱50M per lender) and in Dec 2025 began guaranteeing loans of a private financing company, so a licensed financing company could seek PhilGuarantee cover. ## 9. Other relevant law: E-Commerce Act, BIR e-invoicing, Warehouse Receipts, Bulk Sales Law, FRIA, Government Procurement

### Takeaway
E-invoices and e-signatures are legally recognised (RA 8792). The BIR's mandatory e-invoicing for large taxpayers, exporters, e-commerce and CAS users now has a 31 Dec 2026 deadline (RR 26-2025 extending RR 11-2025). FRIA's stay order suspends enforcement but does not impair liens; pre-commencement transfers within 90 days for inadequate consideration are presumptively rescindable — a direct risk to under-priced factoring purchases from a distressed seller. Government payables are assignable only with the procuring entity's written consent and COA processing. The Bulk Sales Law (Act 3952) remains on the books but covers goods, not receivables.

### Cited Findings
- RA 8792 (E-Commerce Act, 2000) §6–7: data messages and electronic documents "shall not be denied legal effect, validity or enforceability" solely because electronic; an electronic document is "the functional equivalent of a written document" if integrity and reliability are maintained; §8 electronic signatures equivalent to handwritten if the method identifies the party and is reliable; §9 presumptions; §16 contracts may be formed electronically — [RA 8792, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2000/ra_8792_2000.html)
- BIR e-invoicing: RR 11-2025 (Feb 2025) gave covered taxpayers (large taxpayers, exporters, e-commerce businesses, CAS/CBA users) until 14 Mar 2026; RR 26-2025 (5 Sep 2025) moved the first-wave deadline to **31 Dec 2026**; RMC 98-2026 (22 Sep 2026) issued implementing rules; electronic sales reporting under §237-A awaits BIR infrastructure; CREATE MORE provides an allowable deduction for e-invoicing adoption — [Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/the-ber-months-are-here-the-countdown-to-the-e-invoicing-deadline-on-31-december-2026/); [PwC PH](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2025/paperles-invoicing-and-sales-reporting.html); [Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/vat/philippines-deadline-for-mandatory-structured-e-invoicing-extended/); [Tribune, 23 Sep 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/23/bir-e-invoicing-deadline-slated)
- FRIA (RA 10142): §16(q) commencement order includes a stay suspending "all actions or proceedings ... for the enforcement of claims against the debtor"; §17(c) voids post-commencement set-off; §18 exceptions (incl. sureties/solidary debtors/third-party mortgagors); §58 transactions made to defraud or unduly prefer creditors may be rescinded, with a disputable presumption for (a) inadequate consideration within 90 days before commencement, (b) accelerated payments within 90 days, (c) security given within 90 days, (d) transactions giving a creditor more than its pro-rata share while insolvent; §59 receiver or creditor may sue; §60 stay "shall not be deemed in any way to diminish or impair the security or lien of a secured creditor" though enforcement is suspended; §113 liquidation order terminates contracts unless assumed and bars separate suits on unsecured claims, 180-day foreclosure bar; §114 secured creditor may waive security and prove, or maintain it (agreed valuation, liquidator sale, or foreclosure); §133 Civil Code concurrence and preference applies — [RA 10142, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_10142_2010.html)
- Government contract receivables (RA 9184 as amended by RA 12009, 2024; GPPB standard bidding documents; COA Circular 2012-001; PD 1445 §106): assignment is conditionally permitted, not prohibited; standard PBDs carry anti-assignment clauses requiring the procuring entity's prior written consent; without consent an assignment is void as against the government though binding between assignor and assignee; notice under Art. 1626 and COA documentation (proof of assignment, tax clearances, affidavits) are needed; *PNB v. CA* G.R. 107508 (1997) and *DBP v. CA* G.R. 100937 (1994) upheld disclosed/approved assignments of government receivables — [Respicio, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/government-contracts-as-collateral-is-assignment-or-pledge-of-contract-receivables-allowed) (secondary; the article carries an AI-assistance disclaimer)
- Bulk Sales Law (Act 3952, 1932) regulates sale/transfer/mortgage/assignment "of goods, wares, merchandise, provisions, or materials in bulk"; exempts transfers by executors, receivers, assignees in insolvency; repeal bills (HB 7942, 18th Congress; HB 4950, 19th Congress) were filed but no enacted repeal was found — [digest.ph](https://www.digest.ph/laws/bulk-sales-law); [HB 4950](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_19/HB04950.pdf); [Senate LDR on HB 7942](https://ldr.senate.gov.ph/bills/house-bill-no-7942-18th-congress-republic)
- Warehouse Receipts Law (Act 2137) governs negotiable warehouse receipts; PPSA security interests may be perfected in negotiable documents by possession or registration — [RA 11057 §12, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11057_2018.html) (Act 2137 itself not researched)

### Inferences
- True-sale risk: because PPSA labels the receivables buyer a "secured creditor" only for registration/priority, a factoring purchase could be attacked in rehabilitation either as a secured loan (stayed) or, if bought at a deep discount within 90 days of a client's filing, as a §58 transfer "for inadequate consideration". Price at market, document the discount rationale, and register in the PPSR to at least hold secured-creditor status (§60/§114) **[LAWYER CHECK]**.
- Factoring government receivables (LGU/agency payables) requires the agency's written consent and COA-compliant documentation before purchase; this is slow but the legal pathway exists.

### Gaps
- No FRIA case law applying §58 to receivables sales / factoring was found.
- RA 12009 (New Government Procurement Act) and its 2025 IRR were not read in primary form; the assignment position rests on one commentary.

## Summary checklist for the founder (Jan–Jun 2027 window)

1.
