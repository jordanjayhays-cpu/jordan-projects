# International market analysis template

Nov 5, 2026 · @Jordan

## How to use this template

This is the eleven-question market analysis from the IE course, rebuilt as a working tool for a family business deciding whether to take one product into one new country. The output is a two-page answer and a go / no-go score in section 4.

- One product, one country per copy. A second country is a second copy, so the scores compare.
- Every number gets a year, a source and a confidence tag: **H** (official data or own sales), **M** (industry estimate or triangulation), **L** (guess). An L on a question that drives the decision is a reason to pause, not a reason to pad.
- Fill it in two passes: a desk pass in a day using section 5, then a field pass (five to ten calls with importers, distributors, retailers and two or three end customers) that confirms or kills the desk numbers.
- Write answers as sentences with the number in them, not as adjectives. "Large market" is not an answer. "Roughly USD 42 million of imports in 2024, growing 8% a year" is.
- Bracketed text is a blank. *Note* lines are guidance; delete them from the client version.
- Budget: 8 to 12 hours of desk work and 6 to 10 interviews for a first-pass answer. If it needs more, the question is probably too broad.

## 1. Market size

The four questions build on each other: industry sales set the ceiling, buyer count sets the shape, the trend sets the direction, and your own sales estimate is the only one that is a decision rather than a fact.

| Question | Write the answer as | How to get it | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 Estimated industry sales for the year | "\[Product category\] sales in \[country\] were about \[currency amount\] in \[year\], of which imports were \[amount\]. Source: \[name\]." | Top-down: imports of the HS code (UN Comtrade or ITC Trade Map) plus local production if any. Cross-check with one industry report and one retailer's shelf count. Bottom-up: buyers x units per buyer x price. Use both; if they differ by more than 2x, say why. | \[H / M / L\] |
| 1.2 Estimated sales for your company for the year | "Year 1: \[amount\], Year 3: \[amount\], assuming \[share\]% of \[segment\] through \[channel\]." | Never a share of 1.1. Build it from the channel: number of distributors or retailers you can realistically sign in year 1, their expected order size and reorder frequency. Then sanity-check as a share of imports: a new foreign brand rarely passes 2 to 5% of category imports in year 3. | \[H / M / L\] |
| 1.3 How many potential buyers are there likely to be? | B2B: "\[number\] importers / distributors / end users, of which \[number\] fit our minimum order." B2C: "\[number\] households or people in \[segment\], reachable through \[number\] retail doors." | B2B: customs import records by consignee (paid services such as ImportGenius or Panjiva, or the embassy commercial section), trade association member lists, exhibitor lists of the main trade fair. B2C: population x urban share x income band from the national statistics office; retail doors from the top chains' store counts. | \[H / M / L\] |
| 1.4 Is demand likely to grow or shrink? | "\[Grow / flat / shrink\] at about \[x\]% a year through \[year\], driven by \[two drivers\]. Main threat: \[one\]." | Five-year import trend (Comtrade), GDP per capita trend (World Bank), the category's growth in a comparable neighbour, and what the interviews say is changing (regulation, a new local producer, a tariff, a demographic shift). | \[H / M / L\] |

*Note: for a family business the honest 1.2 is usually small and that is fine. The point of 1.1 to 1.3 is to show that even a 1% share pays for the effort. If it does not, stop here.*

*Note: landlocked or small markets often show imports routed through a neighbour (Mongolia via China, Gulf states via the UAE). Check the mirror data, the neighbour's exports to your target, before trusting the target's own import figure.*

## 2. The product

This section answers whether the market will let the product in. Competition decides the price you can charge, innovation fit decides how much explaining you must do, and the resistance list decides what the launch budget actually buys.

### 2.1 Are there a lot of products already in the market that will compete with yours?

List the five to ten products a buyer would see next to yours. Fill one row per competitor from a shelf check (physical or online marketplace) and the import data.

| Competitor | Origin | Price to end buyer (\[currency\] per \[unit\]) | Channel | Estimated share or presence | Their edge | Their weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \[Local brand\] | \[Country\] | \[0.00\] | \[Supermarket / distributor / online\] | \[x% or "all major chains"\] | \[Price, habit, local service\] | \[Quality, range\] |
| \[Imported brand\] | \[Country\] | \[0.00\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |
| Your product | \[Country\] | \[0.00 landed, duty paid\] | \[Planned channel\] | 0 | \[...\] | \[Unknown brand, higher price\] |

Write the conclusion in one sentence: "The market is \[crowded / moderately served / open\]; our landed price sits at \[x\]% of the leader's and our point of difference is \[one thing\]."

### 2.2 Assessment of the product as an innovation

The question is compatibility with the market's technology level: does the product fit how people in this market already buy, use, power, store and dispose of things? Score each line 1 (poor fit) to 5 (perfect fit).

| Compatibility test | Question to answer | Score 1 to 5 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infrastructure | Does it need power, water quality, internet, cold chain or road conditions the market has? | \[ \] | \[...\] |
| Skills | Can the buyer install, use and maintain it without training you must provide? | \[ \] | \[...\] |
| Complementary products | Do the consumables, spare parts, formats or software it relies on exist locally? | \[ \] | \[...\] |
| Standards and certification | Does it meet the market's electrical, food, labelling or safety standard as sold, or does it need re-certification? | \[ \] | \[...\] |
| Relative advantage | Is the benefit over what they use now obvious in under a minute? | \[ \] | \[...\] |
| Trialability | Can a buyer try a small quantity before committing? | \[ \] | \[...\] |

Total \[ \] of 30. Under 18 means the product must be adapted before launch, not marketed harder.

### 2.3 Problems and resistance to product acceptance

Based on 2.1 and 2.2, list every reason a buyer would say no and what you will do about it. Keep the ones you cannot answer; they go into the score in section 4.

| Resistance | Who raises it | Severity (H / M / L) | Response | Cost of the response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \[Price above local leader\] | \[Retail buyer\] | \[H\] | \[Smaller pack size / position as premium\] | \[...\] |
| \[Unknown brand\] | \[End customer\] | \[M\] | \[In-store sampling, distributor's own brand endorsement\] | \[...\] |
| \[Certification missing\] | \[Customs / regulator\] | \[H\] | \[Test and register before first shipment, 4 months\] | \[...\] |
| \[Habit: they buy the category weekly at wet markets, not supermarkets\] | \[End customer\] | \[M\] | \[Change channel\] | \[...\] |
| \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |

*Note: the most common mistake in this section is listing only the resistance you have an answer for. Interview two buyers who said no to a similar import and write down their actual words.*

## 3. The market

This section turns "the country" into the two or three places and the one channel you will actually sell in.

### 3.1 Geographical region(s)

"We will start in \[city or region\], which holds \[x\]% of the country's \[population / category sales / target buyers\], then \[second region\] in year \[n\]."

| Region | Population (\[year\]) | Share of category demand or income | Logistics access (port, airport, rail, road) | Your contact or partner there | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \[Capital region\] | \[0 m\] | \[x%\] | \[Main port, 2 days from hub\] | \[Name or none\] | \[1\] |
| \[Second city\] | \[0 m\] | \[x%\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[2\] |
| \[Rest of country\] | \[0 m\] | \[x%\] | \[Served from capital by distributor\] | \[...\] | \[later\] |

### 3.2 Transportation and communication available in the region(s)

Fill only what the product needs. A shelf-stable consumer good needs the first three rows; machinery or services need the last two as well.

| Item | Fact for this market | Implication for the launch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inbound route and time | \[Sea to \[port\], \[n\] days, then \[road / rail\] \[n\] days; or air to \[airport\]\] | \[Order lead time, safety stock\] |
| Landed cost build-up | \[Freight \[x\]% + duty \[x\]% + VAT \[x\]% + inland \[x\]% of FOB\] | \[Price to distributor\] |
| Last-mile and storage | \[Distributors' warehouse standard, cold chain yes / no, delivery to provinces by \[mode\]\] | \[Packaging, pack size\] |
| Communication with buyers | \[Email / WhatsApp / WeChat / Viber / phone; language; time zone offset\] | \[Who answers, in what language, by when\] |
| Payment rails | \[Bank transfer norms, letter of credit practice, card and e-wallet share\] | \[Contract clause 4 and 5\] |

### 3.3 Consumer buying habits: where, how, and why do consumers buy the product

| Question | Answer with evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Where | "\[x\]% through \[modern retail / traditional trade / online / direct B2B\], the rest through \[...\]. Top three outlets or platforms: \[...\]." |
| How | "Bought \[weekly / seasonally / once\], in \[pack size or order size\], paid by \[cash / card / credit terms of n days\], decided by \[who in the household or company\]." |
| Why | "Chosen on \[price / brand trust / origin / availability / relationship\]. Country of origin \[helps / hurts / neutral\] because \[...\]." |
| Who influences | "\[Retailer recommendation, social platform, trade fair, government tender, professional body\]." |
| When | "\[Peak season, budget cycle, holiday, harvest\]." |

*Note: in most emerging markets the first buyer is the importer or distributor, not the consumer. Answer this table twice if so: once for the distributor (why would they take on a new line) and once for the end customer.*

### 3.4 Product feature preferences

What buyers in this market want the product to be, in their order of priority, versus what you ship today.

| Feature | What local buyers prefer | What you offer today | Gap | Fix and cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| \[Language and labelling\] | \[Local language mandatory by law\] | \[English only\] | \[Yes\] | \[Sticker or reprint, \[cost\]\] |
| \[Specification or variant\] | \[Voltage, flavour, scent, size, material\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |
| \[Price point\] | \[Under \[amount\] per unit\] | \[\[amount\]\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |
| \[Origin and brand story\] | \[Values "made in \[country\]"\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |
| \[Service and warranty\] | \[Local repair, 12 months\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |

*Note: the field pass is where this table gets filled. Ask each interviewee to rank the features, not to rate them; ranking forces trade-offs.*

## 4. Scoring and go / no-go

Score each of the eleven answers 1 to 5, weight them, and read the result against the rule below. The weights favour the questions a family business cannot fix with money: demand, buyers, resistance and habits.

| # | Question | Score 1 to 5 | Weight | Weighted | Confidence (H / M / L) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Industry sales large enough that 1% pays for the entry cost | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 1.2 | Own year-3 sales cover the fixed cost of serving the market | \[ \] | 3 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 1.3 | Enough reachable buyers that losing one does not end the market | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 1.4 | Demand growing | \[ \] | 3 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 2.1 | Competition leaves a price or position gap | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 2.2 | Innovation compatibility total (30 = 5, 18 = 3, under 12 = 1) | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 2.3 | Resistance items all have an affordable response | \[ \] | 3 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 3.1 | A first region is clear and you have a contact in it | \[ \] | 1 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 3.2 | Logistics and payment rails work at your volumes | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 3.3 | Buying habits fit your channel without changing them | \[ \] | 3 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
| 3.4 | Feature gaps are small or cheap to close | \[ \] | 2 | \[ \] | \[ \] |
|  | **Total (maximum 125)** |  |  | **\[ \]** |  |

Decision rule:

- **90 or above, no L on a weight-3 question:** go. Write the entry plan and the contract (use the international contract template).
- **70 to 89:** go to a paid pilot only: one distributor, one region, one container or one season, with the three lowest scores as the pilot's test questions.
- **Below 70, or any weight-3 question scored 1 or 2:** no, or not yet. Record the one fact that would change the answer and the date to re-check.

*Note: the weights are a starting point. Change them once, before scoring, and write down why. Changing them after scoring is how a weak market gets a green light.*

Free first, paid only where the free source runs out. Every row names the question it feeds.

*Note: for a Placewell-type service business the "product" is a labour category and the "imports" are work permits issued. Replace Comtrade with the target country's immigration or labour ministry statistics and the trade fair with the industry's employer association.*

## 6. Worked example: a US natural soap brand into Mongolia

This shows the depth expected after the desk pass only. It uses the August 2026 Nomin research in the jordan-projects repo (nomin folder); figures marked UNSURE or \[to fill\] are what the field pass must close. It is an illustration, not a recommendation.

| # | Answer after the desk pass | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 Industry sales | \[To fill: Mongolia imports of HS 3401 soap and HS 3304 skincare, latest year, from UN Comtrade; check China's mirror exports since over 90% of Mongolia's cargo enters via Tianjin.\] | L until pulled |
| 1.2 Own sales | Year 1: one importer, two or three pallet orders. \[To fill once a line sheet and a landed cost exist.\] Rule of thumb from the research: an LCL pallet US to Ulaanbaatar lands at roughly USD 800 to 1,500 all legs, UNSURE pending two or three forwarder quotes. | L |
| 1.3 Buyers | B2B: a handful of supermarket import arms and distributors: Nomin Holding (vendor funnel at nomin@nomin.net, no named buyer found), Tavan Bogd (represents Colgate-Palmolive), Good Price (stocks Kirkland). B2C: USDA FAS puts potential consumers of US consumer goods at 600,000 to 800,000 of about 3 million people (2018 guide). | M |
| 1.4 Demand trend | Growing: a young population that "likes American brands" (export.gov), Kirkland private label already carrying premium cachet in Ulaanbaatar supermarkets, long winters driving health and personal-care spend. Threat: a Chinese or Korean brand filling the natural-soap niche first. Growth rate \[to fill from the five-year import series\]. | M |
| 2.1 Competition | Big US and global household brands are already present through distributors (Kleenex, Huggies, Pampers, Gillette, Colgate, WD-40, 3M). The natural and organic soap niche is the gap: Dr. Bronner's lists no Mongolian distributor and is openly recruiting exclusive country distributors (verified on its own site, 2026). Landed price versus local leader: \[to fill\]. | M |
| 2.2 Innovation fit | Infrastructure 5, skills 5, complementary products 5, standards 3 (cosmetic labelling in Mongolian commonly required, UNSURE on current rules), relative advantage 3 (natural positioning needs explaining), trialability 4 (small packs). Total 25 of 30. | M |
| 2.3 Resistance | Unknown brand (M): ride on the distributor's existing shelf relationships and in-store sampling. Price above local soap (H): position next to Kirkland and imported premium, not next to local bar soap. Labelling (M): sticker in Mongolian at the distributor. Lead time 6 to 9 weeks door to door (M): one season's stock per order. Supplements-style registration does not apply to soap, but confirm cosmetic rules with a customs broker. | M |
| 3.1 Region | Ulaanbaatar first; it holds roughly half the population and nearly all modern retail. Rest of country via the distributor later. | H |
| 3.2 Transport and communication | Sea US West Coast to Tianjin 15 to 25 days, bonded transit, rail to Ulaanbaatar about 5 days; 6 to 9 weeks door to door. Trial shipments by air consolidation. Duty about 5% plus VAT 10% (to confirm). Do not build on the personal-parcel exemption: sources conflict (about USD 290 versus about USD 50) and resale under it is not legal. Buyers communicate by email and phone; UB is 8 hours ahead of Madrid. | M |
| 3.3 Buying habits | Distributor first: importer of record holds any registrations and wants a full offer (line sheet, wholesale price, certificates, freight plan) rather than a cold email. End customer: premium imported goods bought in modern supermarkets; US origin is a positive signal. \[Interviews to fill: who decides, pack size, pay terms.\] | M |
| 3.4 Feature preferences | Expected: Mongolian-language label, mid-size packs, scent and "natural" claims that translate, winter-skin benefit messaging. \[Field pass to rank.\] | L |

Desk-pass score: around 70 to 75 of 125 with three L items, so the rule in section 4 says pilot only: one distributor, one pallet, one season, with 1.1, 1.2 and 3.4 as the pilot's test questions.

*Note: the field pass for this example is five calls: Nomin's vendor desk, Tavan Bogd, Good Price's buyer, one Ulaanbaatar freight forwarder, one customs broker. That is the week of work that turns the Ls into Ms.*
