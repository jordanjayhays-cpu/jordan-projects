# International contract template

Nov 5, 2026 · @Jordan

## How to use this template

This is a standard cross-border sale-of-goods contract in the eight-section structure from the IE course (Parties, Object, Transport, Payment, Guarantees, Courts, Legislation, Risks). It is not legal advice. Before any real use, a lawyer in the governing-law country checks it.

- Text in \[square brackets\] is a blank to fill.
- Where a clause offers Option A / Option B, keep one and delete the other.
- Lines marked *Note* are drafting guidance. Delete them from the signed version.
- Services deals (manpower supply, BD representation) use the same skeleton: Object becomes the scope of services, Transport becomes deployment and logistics, and Guarantees of Payment shrink to advance plus retainer.
- Keep the numbering. Counterparties and lawyers refer to clauses by number.

This International Sale Contract (the "Contract") is made on \[date\] between:

|  | Seller | Buyer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal name | \[Company name\] | \[Company name\] |
| Legal form and registration no. | \[e.g. Corporation, SEC Reg. No.\] | \[e.g. LLC, Reg. No.\] |
| Registered address | \[Street, City, Country\] | \[Street, City, Country\] |
| Tax / VAT ID | \[TIN or VAT number\] | \[TIN or VAT number\] |
| Represented by | \[Name, title\] | \[Name, title\] |
| Authority to sign | \[Board resolution / power of attorney dated ...\] | \[Board resolution / power of attorney dated ...\] |
| Notices to | \[Email and postal address\] | \[Email and postal address\] |
| Bank for payments | \[Bank, SWIFT, IBAN or account no.\] | n/a |

1.1 Each party confirms it is validly existing and that the signatory has authority to bind it.

1.2 Notices are valid when sent by email to the addresses above, with a copy by courier for termination or claims. A change of address takes effect five business days after written notice.

1.3 Neither party may assign this Contract without the other's written consent, except that the Seller may assign its right to receive payment to a bank, factor or insurer (see clause 5).

*Note: get a copy of the counterparty's registration certificate and the signatory's authority before signing. In many emerging markets a contract signed by someone without a board resolution or power of attorney is unenforceable.*

## 2. Object

2.1 The Seller sells and the Buyer buys the goods described in Annex 1 (the "Goods"):

| Item | Description and specification | HS code | Quantity | Unit | Unit price (\[currency\]) | Total (\[currency\]) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \[Product, brand, model, grade, standard it meets\] | \[000000\] | \[0\] | \[pcs / kg / pallets\] | \[0.00\] | \[0.00\] |
| 2 | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] | \[...\] |
|  |  |  |  |  | **Total contract price** | **\[0.00\]** |

2.2 Price basis: the prices above are \[Incoterm, e.g. FOB Manila / CIF Tianjin\] under Incoterms 2020 and \[include / exclude\] packing, export duties and taxes in the Seller's country.

2.3 Quality: the Goods conform to the specifications in Annex 1, to \[named standard, e.g. ISO / FDA / CE\], and to the sample approved by the Buyer on \[date\], which both parties have initialled.

2.4 Tolerances: quantity may vary by plus or minus \[5\]% per item; the invoice is adjusted to the quantity actually shipped.

2.5 Inspection: the Buyer may inspect before shipment at its own cost through \[SGS / Bureau Veritas / Intertek / named inspector\]. The inspection certificate is final as to quantity and visible condition at shipment. Claims for hidden defects must be notified in writing within \[30\] days of arrival with photographs and the inspector's report.

2.6 Delivery schedule: \[single delivery by date\] / \[deliveries per Annex 2 call-off schedule\]. Time of delivery is \[not\] of the essence.

2.7 Packaging and marking: export-grade packaging suitable for \[sea / air / rail\] transport and \[climate, e.g. humid tropical\] conditions, marked with \[Buyer's order number, destination, gross and net weight, handling symbols\]. Shipping marks per Annex 1.

*Note: this is where most disputes start. Write the specification so a third party with no context could test the goods against it. For consumer products (Nomin-type deals), attach the label artwork and the destination country's labelling rules as an annex.*

## 3. Transport

3.1 Delivery term: \[Incoterm\] \[named place\] Incoterms 2020 (ICC Publication 723). The Incoterm decides who books and pays for carriage, who insures, where risk of loss passes, and who clears customs.

| Incoterm | Seller delivers when | Risk passes | Main carriage paid by | Insurance | Export / import clearance | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EXW | Goods at Seller's premises | At Seller's door | Buyer | Buyer | Buyer / Buyer | Buyer has a strong freight forwarder; Seller wants zero logistics |
| FCA | Goods handed to Buyer's carrier | On handover | Buyer | Buyer | Seller / Buyer | Container and air shipments; cleanest term for most exporters |
| FOB | Goods on board the vessel | On board | Buyer | Buyer | Seller / Buyer | Bulk or breakbulk sea freight only |
| CFR | On board; Seller pays freight to destination port | On board | Seller | Buyer | Seller / Buyer | Seller has better freight rates |
| CIF | As CFR plus Seller insures (minimum Institute Cargo Clauses C) | On board | Seller | Seller (min. cover) | Seller / Buyer | Buyer wants a landed quote; letters of credit often require it |
| CPT / CIP | As CFR / CIF for any mode; CIP needs Clauses A cover | On handover to first carrier | Seller | CIP: Seller (all risks) | Seller / Buyer | Multimodal: sea plus rail into landlocked markets (e.g. Tianjin to Ulaanbaatar) |
| DAP | Goods at named place, not unloaded | At named place | Seller | Seller (in practice) | Seller / Buyer | Buyer handles import duty but wants door delivery |
| DDP | Goods at named place, duty paid | At named place | Seller | Seller | Seller / Seller | Only if Seller has an import licence and tax registration in the Buyer's country |

3.2 Mode and route: \[sea / air / rail / road\], from \[port or airport of loading\] to \[port or place of destination\], via \[transit points\]. Transhipment \[is / is not\] allowed. Partial shipments \[are / are not\] allowed.

3.3 Carrier and forwarder: appointed by \[Seller / Buyer\] per clause 3.1. The appointing party gives the other the booking details at least \[7\] days before loading.

3.4 Insurance: cargo insurance for \[110\]% of the invoice value under Institute Cargo Clauses \[A / B / C\], from \[warehouse to warehouse\], naming \[Buyer / Seller\] as loss payee. Policy and certificate delivered with the shipping documents.

3.5 Shipping documents the Seller delivers to the Buyer \[or to the bank under clause 5\]:

- Commercial invoice (original plus \[2\] copies)
- Packing list with weights, dimensions and marks
- Transport document: \[clean on-board bill of lading / air waybill / rail consignment note\]
- Certificate of origin issued by \[chamber of commerce\]
- Inspection certificate per clause 2.5
- Insurance certificate per clause 3.4 (if Seller insures)
- &#91;Phytosanitary / health / halal / CE / conformity certificate as required by the destination\]

3.6 Documentary lead time: the Seller sends scanned copies within \[2\] business days of shipment and originals by courier within \[5\] business days. Late documents that cause demurrage or storage at destination are for the Seller's account.

3.7 Delay: if delivery is late by more than \[14\] days for reasons attributable to the Seller, the Buyer may claim liquidated damages of \[0.5\]% of the delayed Goods' price per week, capped at \[5\]%. After \[60\] days of delay the Buyer may cancel the delayed portion.

*Note: the named place after the Incoterm matters as much as the term. "FCA Manila" is ambiguous; "FCA Seller's warehouse, \[address\], Manila" or "FCA Manila International Container Terminal" is not. Never use FOB for containers; use FCA.*

## 4. Terms of payment

4.1 Currency: all amounts are in \[USD / EUR / other\]. Payment is made in that currency by SWIFT transfer to the Seller's account in clause 1. Bank charges: Seller's bank for the Seller, Buyer's and intermediary banks for the Buyer.

4.2 Payment schedule (keep one option):

**Option A: advance plus balance**

| Milestone | Share of price | Due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signature of Contract | \[30\]% | Within \[5\] business days of signature |
| Pre-shipment inspection passed | \[0\]% | \[n/a\] |
| Copy of transport document presented | \[70\]% | Within \[5\] business days of presentation |

**Option B: documentary letter of credit**

100% by irrevocable \[and confirmed\] documentary letter of credit, payable at sight \[or at 60 days from bill of lading date\], issued by \[Buyer's bank\] in favour of the Seller, \[confirmed by a first-class bank in the Seller's country\], subject to UCP 600, opened no later than \[21\] days before the agreed shipment date and valid for \[21\] days after the latest shipment date for presentation. Documents per clause 3.5. Letter of credit charges in the Buyer's country for the Buyer, in the Seller's country for the Seller.

**Option C: open account with security**

100% within \[30 / 60\] days of the invoice date, provided the Buyer has delivered the security in clause 5 (bank guarantee, standby letter of credit or credit-insurance approval). Without that security, Option A applies.

4.3 Invoicing: the Seller invoices on \[shipment / each milestone\]. Invoices state the Contract number, Incoterm, HS codes and the Buyer's purchase-order number.

4.4 Set-off: the Buyer may not withhold or set off any amount against claims under this or any other contract without the Seller's written agreement.

4.5 Late payment: interest at \[1\]% per month (or the maximum allowed by the governing law, if lower) from the due date, plus recovery costs. The Seller may suspend further deliveries while any amount is overdue by more than \[10\] days.

4.6 Taxes: each party bears the taxes imposed on it by its own country. If the Buyer's country requires withholding tax on the payment, the Buyer grosses up so the Seller receives the full invoice amount, and provides the withholding certificate.

*Note: the choice of option is the real negotiation. A first deal with an unknown buyer in an emerging market is Option A or B; Option C only after a track record or with insurance. If the deal is in USD and the buyer earns local currency, also read clause 8 on FX risk.*

## 5. Guarantees of payment

5.1 Security: as a condition of any credit under clause 4.2 Option C, the Buyer delivers \[one or more of the following\] before the first shipment:

| Instrument | What it is | Who issues | Covers | Typical cost (indicative, verify) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Advance payment | Cash before shipment | Buyer | The advanced share | None | First deals, small tickets |
| Confirmed letter of credit | Bank pays against compliant documents; confirming bank takes issuing-bank and country risk | Buyer's bank plus confirming bank | 100% of shipment | 0.5 to 2% of value plus confirmation fee | Unknown buyer, risky country |
| Standby letter of credit (ISP98) | Bank pays on Seller's written demand of default | Buyer's bank | Agreed amount for the credit period | 0.5 to 1.5% a year | Repeat shipments on open account |
| Bank guarantee on first demand (URDG 758) | As standby, outside the US | Buyer's bank | Agreed amount | 0.5 to 1.5% a year | Repeat shipments; EU and Middle East counterparties |
| Trade credit insurance | Insurer pays if Buyer becomes insolvent or defaults | Seller buys (Atradius, Coface, Allianz Trade, Credendo) | 80 to 90% of the loss | 0.1 to 1% of insured turnover | Portfolios of buyers; enables non-recourse factoring |
| Export factoring (two-factor, FCI) | Seller sells the receivable; import factor underwrites the Buyer | Seller's factor | 80 to 90% advance, buyer insolvency risk | 1 to 3% of invoice plus interest | Seller needs cash now; Buyer is a solid corporate |
| Parent company guarantee | Buyer's parent guarantees payment | Buyer's parent | Agreed amount | None | Buyer is a thin subsidiary of a strong group |
| Retention of title | Seller stays owner until paid | Contract clause | Recovery of goods, not cash | None | Only where the Buyer's law recognises it |

5.2 Retention of title: title to the Goods passes to the Buyer only on payment in full. Until then the Buyer stores the Goods separately and identifiably, insures them, and does not pledge them. If the Buyer resells before paying, the Buyer holds the resale proceeds for the Seller.

5.3 Assignment of receivables: the Seller may assign, sell or pledge its right to payment under this Contract to a bank, factor or insurer without the Buyer's consent. On written notice of assignment, the Buyer pays only the assignee, and the Buyer acknowledges the assigned invoice within \[5\] business days. The Buyer waives any set-off against the assignee for claims unrelated to the assigned invoice.

5.4 Credit limit: the Seller may at any time, acting reasonably, set or reduce the open-account limit for the Buyer based on the Buyer's payment record or the insurer's approved limit, and ship the excess on Option A or B terms.

5.5 Financial information: on request, not more than \[twice\] a year, the Buyer provides its latest audited financial statements, and notifies the Seller within \[10\] days of any change of control, insolvency filing or event that materially affects its ability to pay.

*Note: clause 5.3 is the one that makes the receivable financeable. Without the Buyer's advance consent and acknowledgement, a factor in the Philippines (or anywhere) will treat the invoice as recourse only, if it will fund it at all. Costs in the table are indicative ranges from market practice, not quotes. Check the Buyer's country recognises retention of title and first-demand guarantees before relying on them.*

## 6. Courts

6.1 Escalation ladder: before starting any proceeding, the parties follow these steps.

1. Written notice of the dispute with the facts and the remedy sought.
2. Negotiation between the signatories within \[15\] days of the notice.
3. Negotiation between the parties' chief executives (or owners) within a further \[15\] days.
4. &#91;Optional\] Mediation under the \[ICC Mediation Rules / WIPO / local centre\], seat \[city\], within \[45\] days.
5. If still unresolved, clause 6.2.

6.2 Final resolution (keep one option):

**Option A: international arbitration (recommended for cross-border deals)**

All disputes arising out of or in connection with this Contract are finally settled under the Rules of Arbitration of the \[International Chamber of Commerce / Singapore International Arbitration Centre / Hong Kong International Arbitration Centre / Dubai International Arbitration Centre\] by \[one / three\] arbitrator(s) appointed in accordance with those Rules. Seat of arbitration: \[Singapore / Paris / Hong Kong / Dubai\]. Language: English. The award is final and binding and may be enforced in any court with jurisdiction.

**Option B: courts of one country**

The courts of \[city, country\] have exclusive jurisdiction over any dispute arising out of this Contract. Each party waives any objection to that venue.

6.3 Interim relief: either party may seek injunctions or asset-preservation orders from any competent court without breaching clause 6.2.

6.4 Costs: the losing party bears the costs of the proceeding and the winner's reasonable legal fees, unless the tribunal or court decides otherwise.

6.5 Small claims: disputes below \[USD 25,000\] are decided by a sole arbitrator under the \[ICC Expedited Procedure / SIAC Expedited Procedure\] on documents only.

| Factor | Arbitration | Local courts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enforcement abroad | New York Convention (170+ states) makes awards enforceable almost everywhere | Judgments often not enforceable in the other party's country |
| Neutrality | Neutral seat, parties choose arbitrators | Home-court advantage for the local party |
| Speed and cost | 12 to 18 months; cost high for small claims | Varies; Philippine commercial suits average about 2.6 years (World Bank Doing Business 2020) |
| Confidentiality | Private | Public record |
| Best for | Deals over roughly USD 50,000 or with an unfamiliar jurisdiction | Both parties in one country, or very small claims |

*Note: an arbitration clause is only worth having if both countries have ratified the New York Convention; the Philippines, Mongolia, Spain, the UAE and Australia all have. Pick a seat neither party is from.*

## 7. Legislation

7.1 Governing law: this Contract is governed by the laws of \[country or state, e.g. England and Wales / Singapore / Spain / the State of New York\], excluding its conflict-of-laws rules.

7.2 CISG (keep one option):

- **Option A (apply it):** the United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG) applies to this Contract and prevails over the governing law where they conflict.
- **Option B (exclude it):** the CISG does not apply to this Contract.

7.3 Incoterms: trade terms are interpreted under Incoterms 2020. Documentary credits are governed by UCP 600, standby credits by ISP98, demand guarantees by URDG 758, each as published by the ICC.

7.4 Mandatory rules: nothing in this Contract excludes rules of the Seller's or Buyer's country that apply regardless of the chosen law, including export controls, sanctions, customs, product safety, labelling, data protection and anti-corruption law.

7.5 Compliance: each party confirms it complies with applicable anti-bribery law (\[US FCPA / UK Bribery Act / local equivalent\]) and sanctions regimes (\[UN / EU / OFAC\]), and that neither it nor its owners are sanctioned persons. A breach is a material breach allowing immediate termination.

7.6 Licences and permits: the Seller obtains export licences; the Buyer obtains import licences, product registrations and any foreign-exchange approvals needed to pay. Failure to obtain a licence is not force majeure for the party responsible for it.

7.7 Language: this Contract is signed in English. Translations are for convenience only. \[If a local-language version is legally required, state which version prevails.\]

7.8 Entire agreement and amendments: this Contract with its annexes is the entire agreement and replaces prior offers and emails. Amendments are valid only in writing signed by both parties.

7.9 Severability: an invalid clause is replaced by a valid one closest to its purpose; the rest of the Contract stands.

*Note: the governing law and the court or arbitration seat should match or at least be compatible. Choosing English or Singapore law with ICC or SIAC arbitration is the common neutral package. CISG applies automatically between parties in CISG states (Spain, the US, Australia, Mongolia are in; the Philippines, the UAE and the UK are not), so decide explicitly rather than by accident. Verify the current CISG membership list before relying on this.*

## 8. Risks

8.1 Allocation: each risk below sits with the party named, through the clause named. Fill the last column per deal.

| Risk | What goes wrong | Who bears it | Clause | Mitigation chosen for this deal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial: buyer does not pay | Insolvency, refusal, dispute as a pretext | Seller | 4, 5 | \[Advance / LC / insurance / factoring\] |
| Commercial: goods not as ordered | Wrong spec, quality, quantity | Seller | 2.3 to 2.5 | \[Inspection certificate, approved sample\] |
| Transport: loss or damage in transit | Theft, water, breakage, delay | Per Incoterm (3.1) | 3.1, 3.4 | \[Insurance level and who buys it\] |
| Economic: exchange rate | Contract currency moves against the payer | \[Buyer if invoiced in Seller's currency; Seller if in Buyer's\] | 4.1, 8.2 | \[Hedge, split currency, adjustment band\] |
| Economic: inflation and input costs | Seller's costs rise before delivery | Seller | 8.3 | \[Price validity period, index clause\] |
| Economic: interest rates and credit cost | Financing the payment term gets dearer | Party giving credit | 4.5 | \[Interest on late payment\] |
| Political: transfer and convertibility | Buyer's central bank blocks FX or payment | Seller | 5.1, 8.4 | \[Confirmed LC, political-risk cover, offshore payment\] |
| Political: tariffs, bans, sanctions | New duties, import bans, embargoes | \[Buyer for import duty; Seller for export control\] | 7.4 to 7.6, 8.4 | \[Change-in-law clause, cancellation right\] |
| Political: expropriation, war, unrest | Delivery or payment becomes impossible | Both | 8.5 | \[Force majeure, insurance\] |
| Legal: unenforceable contract | Wrong signatory, local law voids clauses | Both | 1.1, 6, 7 | \[Authority check, lawyer review, arbitration\] |
| Legal: intellectual property | Counterfeit goods, trademark seizure at customs | Seller | 2.3 | \[Brand authorisation letter\] |
| Operational: counterparty fraud | Fake company, diverted payment instructions | Both | 1, 4.1 | \[Verify bank details by phone, registry check\] |
| Operational: customs and documents | Wrong HS code, missing certificate, demurrage | Per Incoterm and 3.5 to 3.6 | 3.5, 3.6 | \[Named forwarder, document checklist\] |

8.2 Currency adjustment: if the \[USD/local\] exchange rate published by \[central bank\] on the payment date differs by more than \[5\]% from the rate on the signature date, the parties \[share the difference equally / renegotiate the price for undelivered Goods within 15 days, failing which either party may cancel the undelivered portion\].

8.3 Price validity: prices are firm for Goods shipped within \[90\] days of signature. For later shipments the Seller may adjust prices for documented changes in \[raw material index / freight index\] above \[5\]%.

8.4 Change in law: if after signature a new tariff, tax, licence requirement, sanction or exchange control makes performance materially more expensive or illegal for a party, that party may request renegotiation. If no agreement within \[30\] days, either party may terminate the unperformed part without liability, except that paid advances for undelivered Goods are refunded.

8.5 Force majeure: neither party is liable for failure caused by events beyond its reasonable control that it could not have avoided (war, riot, epidemic, natural disaster, government order, port closure, strikes other than the party's own staff), provided it notifies the other within \[7\] days with evidence and uses reasonable efforts to resume. Payment obligations for Goods already delivered are not excused. If the event lasts more than \[90\] days, either party may terminate the affected part.

8.6 Hardship: if events not covered by 8.5 make performance excessively onerous for a party, the parties renegotiate in good faith under the \[ICC Hardship Clause 2020\]. Failing agreement within \[30\] days, the affected party may terminate the unperformed part.

8.7 Limitation of liability: neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss or loss of profit. Each party's total liability is capped at \[100\]% of the Contract price, except for fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal injury, or breach of clause 7.5.

8.8 Termination for cause: a party may terminate on written notice if the other commits a material breach not cured within \[15\] days of notice, becomes insolvent, or breaches clause 7.5.

*Note: for a Philippine-to-foreign deal, the usual gaps are FX (peso-earning buyers paying USD), transfer risk in the buyer's country, and documents. For goods into Mongolia the land route via China adds transit and customs risk; CIP Ulaanbaatar with Clauses A insurance and a confirmed LC is the conservative set-up. Political-risk cover is available from export credit agencies and from the same private insurers as trade credit cover.*
