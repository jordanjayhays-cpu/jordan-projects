# The third ingredient — what to take from Próspera, and what will get you killed

Written 2026-09-22. Jordan: *"mix hong kong, and prospera in roatan with bali vibes in this place
of Bantayan."*

## The one-line answer

**Copy Próspera's product. Do not copy Próspera's politics.**

Próspera built a genuinely good product — a jurisdiction you can join online for $130 a year,
register a company in under 24 hours, and operate at an effective 1% business tax. Over 200
businesses signed up.

It did that by buying a carve-out *from* the Honduran state. The Honduran state took it back.
That half of the model is currently on fire and should not be imported to the Philippines.

The Philippines already has the legal version: **PEZA**. Slightly worse tax, vastly better
survival odds.

## The three ingredients, separated

| Ingredient | What it actually supplies | Why it's in the mix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bali** | The feeling, the design language, the existing demand | Why anyone wants to come |
| **Hong Kong** | Infrastructure that works + low-tax business gravity | Why it functions once they're there |
| **Próspera** | **Jurisdiction as a product** — membership, e-residency, fast company formation, regulatory clarity | **How you sell it before you've built it** |

Próspera is the one that answers the question the other two don't: *what do you sell on day one,
when you own no land and have built nothing?*

Answer: **membership.** Próspera had 443 e-residents paying $130/year before it had a city.

## What Próspera actually is (verified 2026-09-22)

| | |
| --- | --- |

| Legal vehicle | ZEDE (Zone for Employment and Economic Development) |

**The pricing structure is the insight.** Three tiers, the cheapest of which requires you to be
nowhere near the place. That is how a city with 79 full residents has 200 companies.

## The five mechanics worth stealing

### 1. Sell membership, not real estate
Real estate needs land, capital and permits. Membership needs a website and something worth
joining. Próspera's entire funnel starts at $130 and never requires you to get on a plane.

### 2. Tier the on-ramp
Digital tier → resident tier → physical tier. Each one is a step, and most people never take the
last one. That is fine — the ones who don't still pay.

### 3. Make setup speed the headline feature
"Register a company in 24 hours" is a concrete, checkable, boring-sounding claim that converts
better than any amount of beach photography. The Philippines' default company setup is slow and
painful. Being demonstrably faster is a real product.

### 4. Sell regulatory *clarity*, not regulatory absence
Próspera's opt-in framework is marketed as freedom, but what businesses actually buy is knowing
what the rules are. In the Philippines you can't opt out of Philippine law — but you *can* be the
place that has already done the paperwork, knows the answer, and hands it to you in writing.
That's the honest version of the same product.

### 5. Build something physically good
Próspera's buildings are architecturally serious. It signals permanence. A beach project that
looks temporary attracts temporary people.

## The three things that will get you killed if you copy them

### 1. The sovereignty carve-out. This is the big one.

Timeline:

- **April 2022** — Honduran Congress repeals the ZEDE law **unanimously**.
- **20 September 2024** — Honduran Supreme Court declares ZEDEs unconstitutional, *ab initio*.
- **February 2025** — Próspera's CAFTA-DR arbitration against Honduras survives preliminary
  objections. Reported claim: **~$11 billion**. *(Figure widely reported; treat as UNSURE.)*
- **March 2026** — new President Nasry Asfura signs the ICSID Convention; Honduras moves to rejoin.
- **15 September 2026** — Próspera appoints its own *fiscal civil* (civil prosecutor) to enforce its
  own rules. Honduran civil society publicly calls it a coup against national sovereignty.

**Read that last line again.** Próspera is now in the position of enforcing its own law inside a
country whose Congress and Supreme Court have both voided its legal basis. Whatever happens in
arbitration, *that* is not a business model. It's litigation with a beach view.

**The lesson is not "don't build a zone."** It's: **the host state has to want you there, and the
legal instrument has to be the state's own.** PEZA is exactly that — the Philippine government's
own instrument, ~400 operating zones, proclaimed by the President rather than struck down by a
court.

### 2. Crawfish Rock

Próspera's neighbouring village is Crawfish Rock, a historic Garifuna community. They say they were
never consulted. When the village lost running water, Próspera connected it to its own tank, then —
by the villagers' account — proposed unfair pricing and cut the taps when they tried to restore
their old system. The community council president has led opposition for four years.

**Bantayan has ~147,000 residents across three municipalities.** That is not a village you can
out-manoeuvre. It's a constituency with three mayors, and under Philippine law the barangay and
municipal level have real veto power in practice.

The Crawfish Rock mistake — arriving as an outside jurisdiction and treating the existing
population as an obstacle — is the single fastest way to make this project impossible.
**The infrastructure you build has to serve the existing island, or it will not get built.**
That's not ethics advice, it's the permitting reality.

### 3. The libertarian branding

Próspera's positioning made local politics an adversary by design. That branding travels badly and
buys nothing in the Philippines. Sell "a beach town that works" — not an ideology.

## PEZA vs ZEDE — the trade, stated plainly

| | Próspera / ZEDE | PEZA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business tax | **~1% effective** | 5% SCIT on gross income, after a 4–7 yr tax holiday |
| Legal basis | Carve-out from the state — **voided by Congress and the Supreme Court** | The state's own statute, ~400 zones operating |
| Regulation | Opt in to any country's rules | Philippine law, no opt-out |
| Who grants it | ZEDE committee | **Presidential proclamation** on PEZA recommendation |
| Condition | None | **≥70% of services exported** |
| Survival odds | In arbitration | Routine |

**PEZA's 5% is worse than Próspera's 1%. It is also still there in ten years.** For a project
measured in decades that is not a close call.

## What this changes in the plan

**E-residency moves from "someday" to Phase 0.** The Próspera lesson is that you can have paying
members before you have a building. Jordan's version, honestly framed:

- A **founding membership** sold to the YouTube audience — annual fee, real benefits (first access
  to units, the company-setup service, the community, discounted stays).
- **Not** a legal residency or a visa. Jordan cannot grant either, and implying otherwise is fraud.
  The value is the service layer and the queue position, and it must be described as exactly that.
- The service layer already exists as three companies: **Placewell** hires, **Vaya** handles visas
  and relocation, **Amigo Sales** sells. Próspera had to build its concierge arm from scratch.
  Jordan already owns it.

**And the corporate pitch gets its real shape.** Not "come to our beach." It's:

> Register here, 5% tax, we do the paperwork, we hire your staff, we handle the visas — and your
> team actually wants to live there.

Próspera can offer clauses one through four. It cannot offer the fifth, because nobody wants to
move to a jurisdiction that's suing its own host country.

## What to verify before any of this is quotable

1. **The ~$11bn arbitration figure** — widely reported, not confirmed from the filing. UNSURE.
2. **Whether a PEZA enterprise can register inside an *existing* ecozone** rather than creating a
   new one. If yes, the corporate half starts years earlier than Phase 3. **UNVERIFIED and it is
   the highest-value question in this document.**
3. **PEZA's minimum land, capital and infrastructure standards** for accrediting a new IT park.
4. **Bantayan's Less Developed Area status** — decides whether the tax holiday runs 4 or 7+ years.
5. **Próspera's resident numbers** — 1,793 total vs 443 e-residents + 79 full residents don't
   reconcile. One of those counts is measuring something else. Don't cite either in public.

Items 2–4 are **one hour with a Philippine corporate lawyer.** There is already a board task for a
lawyer hour before the January flight — this is what that hour is for.
