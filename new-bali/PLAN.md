# New Bali — how to bring in nomads and corporations

Written 2026-09-22. Jordan: *"I want to bring in digital nomads and corporations into the area."*

## The insight

These are not two marketing campaigns. They are **one sequence**, and the order is not negotiable.

**Nomads are cheap to attract and prove nothing on their own.** They arrive for three months, pay
rent, and leave. A hundred of them is a lifestyle business.

**Corporations are hard to attract and change everything.** One company relocating twenty staff
beats a hundred nomads on revenue, and it stays for years instead of weeks.

But a corporation will not move people to an island on a promise. It needs to see that humans
already live there happily, with power that stays on and internet that works.

**So the nomads are not the business. They are the proof that makes the corporate pitch credible.**
They are also the cheapest proof available.

---

## The corporate lever: PEZA

This is the real unlock and it is the literal Philippine translation of "Hong Kong."

Hong Kong's draw was never the skyline. It was low tax and easy company setup. The Philippines has
a direct equivalent: the **Philippine Economic Zone Authority**.

A PEZA-accredited ecozone or IT park gives registered companies:

| Incentive | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **5% Special Corporate Income Tax** | On gross income earned, instead of regular corporate tax |
| **Income Tax Holiday** | **4–7 years**, full exemption — **longer in Less Developed Areas** |
| Duty-free importation | Capital equipment, machinery, spare parts |
| Local tax exemption | Exempt from local government imposts, fees, licences, taxes |

**The qualifying condition:** the company must export at least **70%** of its services — meaning it
serves clients outside the Philippines. For BPO, IT, design, engineering and back-office work, that
is the normal case.

**Bantayan would almost certainly qualify as a Less Developed Area**, which lengthens the tax
holiday. Being remote is an advantage here, not a handicap.

### What this means for the pitch

You are not selling a beach to corporations. You are selling:

> A 5% tax rate, a multi-year tax holiday, English-speaking staff hired for you, and your team
> actually wants to live there.

That last clause is the differentiator. Every PEZA IT park in Manila and Cebu offers the first
three. None of them offer the fourth.

### Why PEZA and not a Próspera-style carve-out

Próspera bought its 1% tax rate by carving a zone *out* of Honduran sovereignty. Congress repealed
the ZEDE law unanimously in April 2022; the Supreme Court voided it as unconstitutional *ab initio*
on 20 September 2024. Próspera now enforces its own rules inside a country that has legally
abolished its basis, and is in CAFTA-DR arbitration.

PEZA's 5% is worse than Próspera's effective 1%. **It is also the Philippine state's own
instrument, with ~400 zones operating and a presidential proclamation behind it rather than a
Supreme Court ruling against it.** For a project measured in decades that is not a close call.

### The catch, stated plainly

An ecozone must be **proclaimed by the President** on PEZA's recommendation. That is a real
process, not a form. Private developers do build and accredit IT parks — Cebu IT Park is the
obvious precedent — but it takes capital, a developer partner and time.

**UNVERIFIED:** the minimum land area, capital and infrastructure standards PEZA requires to
accredit a new IT park. This must be established before the corporate half is more than an idea.

## The nomad lever: the visa, which is not live yet

**Executive Order 86, signed April 2025**, created a Philippine digital nomad visa.

Expected shape: **2 years** (1 year, renewable once), income from outside the Philippines of around
**USD 2,000/month**, work only for overseas clients and employers.

**But as of September 2026 the Bureau of Immigration does not list it.** No application form, no fee
schedule. The rollout is incomplete.

**Do not build the nomad pitch on it yet.** Right now people arrive on tourist visas and extend,
which already works and is what every nomad in the Philippines does.

**The opportunity:** when the visa does launch, there will be a scramble for attention. Being the
place that is already built, already has a waitlist and already ranks for "Philippines digital
nomad" is worth more than any launch-day campaign. That argues for building the audience *now*,
before the visa makes it competitive.

## The phases

### Phase 0 — now to January. Audience, then membership. Costs a weekend.
- **Shoot the video.** The pitch is no longer "beach coliving." It is *the beach city Bali should
  have been* — Bali's feeling, without Bali's traffic, water crisis and power cuts.
- Stand up an email list. Currently a recorded gap.
- **Define the founding membership** — the Próspera mechanic. An annual fee for first access to
  units, the company-setup service, the community, discounted stays. **Not a residency, not a
  visa** — Jordan cannot grant either, and implying otherwise is fraud. See `PROSPERA-MODEL.md`.
- Target: a waitlist number you can say out loud to a landowner.

**No land, no capital, no legal structure required.** This phase is entirely unblocked.

**Why membership belongs here and not in phase 3:** Próspera had 443 e-residents paying $130/year
and 200+ registered companies while holding 79 full physical residents. Revenue does not have to
wait for buildings.

### Phase 1 — January to June, on the ground. Verify.
Four questions, none about land area:

1. **Infrastructure.** Actual power reliability, water supply, internet, typhoon exposure. The whole
   pitch is infrastructure and all that is recorded is "~25 Mbps."
2. **Title.** Who owns what, across three municipalities and 147,000 residents.
3. **RA 12252.** What counts as a qualified investment for the 99-year lease, and at what threshold.
4. **PEZA.** What it takes to accredit a new IT park, and whether Bantayan can clear it.

Questions 3 and 4 are **one hour with a Philippine property and corporate lawyer.** There is already
a board task for a lawyer hour before flying.

### Phase 2 — one property. Nomads only.
A single building, done properly. Power, water and connectivity engineered rather than hoped for,
because that is the entire brand promise and the first guest who loses power for a day will say so
publicly.

This is the proof. It is also the content engine for phase 0's audience.

### Phase 3 — PEZA accreditation and the first corporate tenant.
Only attempt this once phase 2 demonstrably works. The first corporate tenant is the hardest sale
and the one that changes the economics.

**Jordan's unfair advantage here:** the corporate offer already exists as three companies.
**Placewell** hires the staff. **Vaya** handles visas and services. **Amigo Sales** sells it.
No other beach-town developer has that.

---

## Why this order and not the reverse

Chasing corporations first means selling an empty island against Cebu IT Park, which has flights,
hospitals, universities and a decade of operating history. You would lose.

Chasing nomads first costs a weekend and produces the one thing Cebu IT Park cannot copy: proof that
people *want* to be there.

---

## What would make this fail

1. **Promising infrastructure before measuring it.** The pitch dies the first time the power goes
   out and a guest posts about it.
2. **Waiting for the nomad visa.** It may slip further. Build the audience on tourist-visa reality.
3. **Attempting PEZA before there is anything to show.** Accreditation is slow, expensive, and
   pointless without demonstrated demand.
4. **Doing a Crawfish Rock.** Próspera's neighbouring Garifuna village says it was never consulted,
   then fought the project over water access for four years. Bantayan has ~147,000 residents across
   three municipalities and three mayors. Infrastructure that serves only the development and not
   the island does not get permitted. See `PROSPERA-MODEL.md`.
5. **Selling anything that sounds like a residency or a visa.** Membership is a service and a queue
   position. Describe it as exactly that.

## Accept when

There is a published video, an email list with real signups, and a written answer from a Philippine
lawyer on RA 12252 thresholds and PEZA accreditation requirements. Everything after that is a
decision with numbers attached.
