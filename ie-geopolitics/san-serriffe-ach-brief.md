## 2. Professor's own hints on diagnosticity (Session 9 transcript)

These are paraphrased from what was said in class. They are the strongest signal of how the paper will be graded.

- The VPN is consistent with both hypotheses. He said explicitly: it is not diagnostic, you don't care, even if your gut says it matters.
- Strangelove's retirement: he is several years short of retirement age. Ask why. The PG officers in the photo could just be old colleagues. The scuba story prompts a question: does he actually hold a dive licence. Find out.
- The defector: four possibilities. Genuine and informed. Genuine but misinformed before leaving. A plant. A fantasist. He is vague on the facility, which is the part you most need.
- The new untested HUMINT source: suspicious or truthful, who knows. Rate the source, not the story.
- Curveball analogy: a defector who tells you what you want to hear can be partly "confirmed" by honest people who saw something innocent (hydrogen trailers). Beware convenient confirmation.
- The intercepted "hide it, hide it" call in Iraq sounded like a smoking gun and was not. Evidence that sounds guilty is not the same as evidence that discriminates.
- Absent evidence: if H1 is true, what would you expect to see and do you see it. His example: monkey imports. Also pollution control and water filtration orders, overpressure and HEPA equipment, new construction, nice housing compounds, traffic patterns, suppliers refusing on-site training.
- Red-on-red deception: regimes fool their own people and officers. A source can be honest and wrong.
- Expensive or clever intelligence is not automatically diagnostic. Do not over-weight the satellite or the honey trap.
- The good police officer eliminates suspects by disproving means, motive and opportunity. Favour the hypothesis with the least evidence against it, not the most for it.
- Use the ACH on LLMs too: ask the model to be the devil and disprove your thesis.

From the NYT article (Spiegelhalter): Cromwell's law. Keep some probability back for being wrong. Use it in the confidence wording of the key judgement.

## 4. Draft ranking (for the group to argue over)

Rule: rank by the fewest and weakest inconsistencies, not the most support.

Evidence against H2 (there is no programme): E3 (reliable diplomat), E15 (technicians gone, documented), E17 (early retirement, documented), supported by E11, E4, E18, E19.
Evidence against H1 (there is a programme): E16 (untested source), E20 (untested defector, doubted by liaison), weakly E2 and E12.

Observation: the two items that most contradict H1 come from the two least credible sources, and both tell the same convenient story. The items that contradict H2 come from a reliable source and from documented open-source anomalies that an innocent state would have no reason to produce.

Draft key judgement: it is likely (55 to 70%) that San Serriffe retains a small covert BW research effort, most plausibly around Strangelove and the departed technicians, while the declared production capability has been destroyed.

Items carrying the conclusion: E3, E17, E15.

The one observation that would flip it: locating the departed lab technicians in genuine foreign civilian employment, together with Strangelove verifiably living idle on the coast. If both check out, H2 becomes the least-inconsistent hypothesis.

Second flip risk: if E3's diplomat turns out to be fed by the Durkish channel, E3 and E4 are one item at credibility 4 and the case for H1 weakens sharply.

Cromwell's law: keep probability back. The sources that would most change the picture (E16, E20) are exactly the ones we cannot yet test.
