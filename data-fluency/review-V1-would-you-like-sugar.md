Checked against: Final Group Assignment brief, Session 2–3 design rules, Session 7 storytelling rules, Group 3 nutrition xlsx (260 items).

## Verdict
The more rigorous of the two decks. Every number on every slide reproduces from the dataset (12 claims checked, 12 correct). Audience is stated on slide 1, sources and limitations are on slide 8, colour follows the highlight-palette rule exactly. Gaps: the story reads as six findings in a row rather than an arc with a climax, the "ban" conflict is asserted not sourced, and the dashboard is never mentioned.

## Top 3 risks, fix first
1. **No climax.** Headers say "Serving 1 of 6 … 6 of 6". That is dashboard order (most important first, supporting after), which Session 7 calls indirect delivery. Live = narrative arc. Pick slide 3 ("every item over the limit comes in a cup") as the aha and say so out loud: "This is the moment the ban stops making sense." Slides 4–6 then become "and here is what to do instead."
2. **The conflict is asserted.** "A fast-food ban treats every item the same." Is there a real proposal? If yes, cite it on slide 1. If hypothetical, say "Imagine the council is weighing a ban" and add the stakes in one line: a ban hits 150 items the data does not flag. Session 7: the hero must face a problem with consequences.
3. **No dashboard bridge.** Same as V3. Slide 8 lists Looker Studio under tools but the live demo is never cued. Add one line on slide 2: "All 260 dots are in the dashboard, filter by category."

## What the lectures would praise
- Highlight palette done right: one colour per slide (pink = sugar, blue = sodium), everything else grey. Pink/blue is colourblind-safe. Session 3 exactly.
- Key on every slide, labels at the mark, no legend hunting.
- Z-pattern: headline top-left, chart right, key bottom-left.
- Unconventional plots that fit: strip plot with a threshold line (slide 3), slope chart for swaps (slide 6), unit comparison (slide 7). The brief asks for this.
- Limitations column on slide 8 ("What this data can't tell us"). Almost no student deck has this. Keep it.
- AI use disclosed on slide 8.
- 8 slides for 8–10 min = about 1 min each. Right pace.

## Session 3 rules being bent
- **Cups as containers (slide 4).** The cup tapers, so filled area is not proportional to the average. The fill line is what carries the number, so say "read the line, not the pink area." Pictogram risk: low, defensible.
- **"r = 0.92" (slide 5).** The audience is a city council. Session 7's Challenger lesson: jargon kills the message. Replace with "almost a straight line: double the calories, double the salt."
- **Regression line past the data (slide 5).** Trim it to the last food point.
- **Table on slide 7.** Four rows of text is a lot for direct delivery. Keep, but the presenter reads only the Measure column aloud.

## Small things
- WHO 50 g (10% of energy) is the firm guideline; V3 used the 25 g conditional one. Fine, but if both decks ever share a room, pick one.
- "1.5 days of sodium" for 3,600 mg is 1.57. Fine.
- Slide 1 dots are all grey. Consider pre-colouring the 110 flagged items faintly so slide 7 is a reveal, not a surprise.
