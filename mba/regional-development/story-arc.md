# Eight Patents: story arc (12-year-old version)

Sources: Data Fluency Ses. 1, 7 (Granola + Notion) · Storytelling & Public Speaking Ses. 3-4, 5-6 (Granola) · Sugar deck as model · Assignment 2 brief.

## The one message (say it 5 times)

**Know-how is bought, not born.**

Kid version: two kids start school level. One does homework every night. Forty years later, one is far ahead. It wasn't talent. It was homework. The good news: homework can start today.

## Characters

- **Mexico and Korea:** the two kids.
- **The IDB:** the hero. They can change the ending.
- **Us (the consultants):** the guide.

## Rules this follows (from your notes)

- **One message, said at least 5 times, with slightly different words each time.** "If you only remember one thing…" (Storytelling Ses. 5-6)
- **Opening:** a hook first (a striking number), then what we'll cover, then why the IDB should care. (Storytelling Ses. 5-6)
- **Closing:** summarize, say what they gained, and come back to the opening hook. Never end on "Thank you". (Storytelling Ses. 5-6)
- **3 to 4 parts at most.** Flow by time: past, present, future. (Storytelling Ses. 5-6)
- **Headlines, not labels. One idea per slide. Never more than 2 slides in a row with the same layout.** (Storytelling Ses. 3-4)
- **A story needs conflict and change.** Here the conflict is that Korea pulled away; the change is the IDB starting the homework. (Storytelling Ses. 3-4)
- **The hero is the client. Hook with a surprising number. Climax is the "aha". Close with what to do.** (Data Fluency Ses. 7)
- **Live delivery means less text on slides.** The presenter carries the story. (Data Fluency Ses. 7)
