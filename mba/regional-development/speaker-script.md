# Eight Patents: speaker script

Group 1 · Ernst & Young (EY) for the IDB · 20 minutes, 5 speakers, then Q&A.

One message, said at least 5 times: **Know-how is bought, not born.**

| Speaker | Slides | Part | Target time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker 1 | 1–5 | Title, Executive summary, Regional context | 4 min |
| Speaker 2 | 6–10 | Diagnostic: four causes and the climax | 4.5 min |
| Speaker 3 | 11–13 | International benchmarks: Korea, Costa Rica | 3.5 min |
| Speaker 4 | 14–17 | Policy recommendations: the three steps | 4 min |
| Speaker 5 | 18–19 | Implementation, Conclusion, then hosts Q&A | 2.5 min |

Hand-offs happen at section breaks: after slide 5, 10, 13 and 17. Each hand-off line is at the end of that slide's notes.

## Slide 1 · Speaker 1

Speaker 1 · about 30 seconds. Open with the hook, out loud, before any introduction: "Last year, a whole country filed eight patents." Then click.

## Slide 2 · Speaker 1

Speaker 1 · Executive summary · about 1 minute 30.
"We are Group 1 from Ernst & Young. We were commissioned by the Inter-American Development Bank, the IDB, the development bank that lends to governments across Latin America and the Caribbean. The IDB asked us one question: why has growth in Mexico and Central America been so disappointing?" (Full names once; "EY" and "the IDB" after this.)
Three clicks: the problem is on screen. Click: the cause. Click: the fix. Click: the ask.
"The problem: eight patents, a whole country, one year. The cause: know-how can be bought, and nobody here invested in it. (Message #1) The fix: bring in the builders: foreign firms that open permanent plants and labs in second cities and train local firms and workers. Our ask: fund it in one country, this year."

## Slide 3 · Speaker 1

Speaker 1 · Regional context · about 1 minute. Two clicks.
Wait for all eight dots. "Last year, the whole of Guatemala filed eight patents. Eight. For 18 million people."
Click: the eight shrink into a speck as Korea's dots fill the slide. "The same year, South Korea filed 195,786. And Korea has fewer people than Mexico."

## Slide 4 · Speaker 1

Speaker 1 · about 1 minute. The dots spread into 123 countries.
"Does this matter? Across 123 countries, the ones that invent more are richer. Ten times more patents per person goes with about 2.7 times the income. Korea is top right. Our subregion is bottom left."
Be honest if asked: "This is correlation, not proof. Patents are the thermometer. The engine is know-how, and growth economics links know-how to growth: Romer, and Hausmann and Hidalgo on economic complexity."

## Slide 5 · Speaker 1

Speaker 1 · about 1 minute.
"This is the disappointing growth the IDB asked about. Since 2000, income per person in Mexico grew just 0.3% a year: 7% in 24 years. Guatemala and Honduras about 1.5%, below the world average. Korea, 3.1%."
"One exception: Costa Rica. Remember it." If asked about Panama: it grew fast on the canal, logistics and finance, a special case, so we left it off this chart.
Hand-off: "So why? [Speaker 2] has the diagnosis."

## Slide 6 · Speaker 2

Speaker 2 · Diagnostic · about 45 seconds.
"Four causes. First: the region barely pays for research. Korea spends almost 5% of its economy on research every year; the OECD average is about 3%; Latin America, under 0.7%. Most of South America and the Caribbean is low too, and our subregion is at the bottom: Mexico 0.25%, Guatemala almost zero. That is a policy choice, written into a budget."

## Slide 7 · Speaker 2

Speaker 2 · about 45 seconds.
"Second: few people are paid to invent. Korea has about 9,500 researchers per million people. Chile has about 700. Every country in our subregion is below Chile: Mexico 438, Guatemala 1.4." If time: "That is about 26 researchers in the whole of Guatemala."

## Slide 8 · Speaker 2

Speaker 2 · about 1 minute 15.
"Third: the capital races ahead and the rest of the country is left behind. Jobs, universities and investment pile up in the capital, and young people and money leave the regions. Economists call it backwash."
"Look at the gap. In Korea, the capital region is only 1.3 times richer than the poorest region. In Costa Rica, 2.5 times. In Mexico, Mexico City is 5.9 times richer than Chiapas."
If asked: "Mexico's richest region is actually Campeche, because of oil, so we compare the capital." OECD Regional Database, 2021.

## Slide 9 · Speaker 2

Speaker 2 · about 1 minute.
"Fourth: hiring someone officially costs extra, because payroll taxes pay for health and pensions. So firms stay small and off the books. Across our subregion, about 6 in 10 workers are informal, against about 1 in 4 in Chile. Honduras and Guatemala are the worst; Costa Rica the best." (ILOSTAT, 2025; Santiago Levy's argument.)

## Slide 10 · Speaker 2

Speaker 2 · the climax · about 45 seconds. Slow down.
"Four causes, one pattern. Know-how can be bought, and nobody here invested in it." (Message #2)
Hand-off: "Is this destiny? No. [Speaker 3] shows who has done it."

## Slide 11 · Speaker 3

Speaker 3 · International benchmarks · about 1 minute. Two clicks on this slide.
On entry: 1960 to the mid-1980s, Mexico ahead. "Were we always behind? No. In 1960, Mexico was four times richer than Korea."
Click 1: the lines cross. "Then Korea caught up. Around 1988, they swapped."
Click 2: the chart pulls back to 2024. Let it move. "Today Korea is 3.6 times richer." Pause. "So what changed?"

## Slide 12 · Speaker 3

Speaker 3 · about 1 minute 15. One click on this slide.
On entry the chart draws 1980 to 1985, zoomed in. "In 1980, the two filed patents at the same level: Mexico 704, Korea 1,241. Two kids, same classroom."
Click: the chart pulls back to 2024 and the title changes. Let it move, then: "Then Korea did its homework. Its patents grew 158 times. Mexico's, 1.7 times. That is what turned Korea from the poorer country into the richer one."

## Slide 13 · Speaker 3

Speaker 3 · about 1 minute.
"And it has been done next door. Costa Rica went after foreign factories on purpose, through its agency CINDE. FDI brings technology and know-how, and local firms and workers learn from it." (Message #3: know-how can be bought.) ⚠ Check the CINDE details.
Hand-off: "So what should the IDB do? [Speaker 4]."

## Slide 14 · Speaker 4

Speaker 4 · Policy recommendations · about 45 seconds.
"So what should the IDB do? Not chase patents. Patents are the thermometer, not the medicine. The medicine is know-how, and the fastest way to get it is to bring in the builders: foreign firms that open permanent plants and labs here, not consultants, and train local firms and workers. Three moves, one for each cause."

## Slide 15 · Speaker 4

Speaker 4 · about 1 minute 15. Fixes causes 1 and 2.
"One: invite foreign firms to build here: a real plant or lab that stays. One condition: they train local suppliers, hire formally, and partner with a local university. An IDB-funded investment team picks the firms and writes the conditions. Costa Rica did this with CINDE; Chile did something similar for salmon." Why now: factories leaving China are choosing new homes.

## Slide 16 · Speaker 4

Speaker 4 · about 1 minute. Fixes cause 3.
"Two: put them in second cities, not the capital. The capital is already full. Second cities linked to their towns spread the jobs instead of draining them." Example cities to name if asked: [group to choose, e.g. Querétaro or Mérida in Mexico].

## Slide 17 · Speaker 4

Speaker 4 · about 1 minute. Fixes cause 4.
"Three: make formal hiring cheaper. Pay for health and pensions from general taxes, not wages. Then local firms can come onto the books and become suppliers to the foreign firms." Say the cost first: "It costs money before it saves money."
Hand-off: "[Speaker 5] on how we roll it out."

## Slide 18 · Speaker 5

Speaker 5 · about 1 minute 15.
"Governments last four years; these programmes don't. So the cheapest, fastest step goes first, and each step has to earn the next. Every year we publish three scores. We are not promising a growth number." If asked: inclusive, because it reaches towns and informal workers; sustainable, because know-how lasts.

## Slide 19 · Speaker 5

Speaker 5 · Conclusion and next steps · about 1 minute. The eight dots are back.
"Eight inventions. In 1980 Korea started where Mexico was. The difference was homework. Know-how is bought, not born. (Message #4) So bring in the builders, in one country, this year. In the next 90 days: pick the pilot country and its second city, shortlist the first foreign firms, and set the baselines."
Pause. No "thank you". "We'd love your questions." Speaker 5 hosts Q&A.

## Q&A plan (Speaker 5 hosts and passes each question on)

Rules: repeat the question, answer in one or two sentences with PREP (point, reason, example, point), hand back. Never guess a number: say we will check it and follow up.

| Likely question | Owner | Answer in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Korea had US support and a military government. Is it a fair comparison? | Speaker 3 | Agreed, one country is not a perfect model. That is why the plan copies cases from this region: Costa Rica, Chile, Medellín. |
| Patents miss a lot. Firms file abroad. | Speaker 1 | True. Patents are the simplest count we can compare across countries. Research spending and researchers point the same way. |
| Is informality really caused by payroll taxes? | Speaker 2 | It is one main cause in Levy's work, not the only one. ILOSTAT shows the size of it: about 6 in 10 workers across our subregion. |
| Why do capitals matter? Seoul is huge too. | Speaker 2 | Seoul is big, but Korea's capital region is only 1.3× richer than its poorest region. Mexico City is 5.9× richer than Chiapas (OECD, 2021). |
| Do more patents really cause growth? | Speaker 1 | Not on their own. Across 123 countries, inventing goes strongly with income (correlation 0.75) but only weakly with later growth. Patents are the thermometer; know-how is the engine (Romer; Hidalgo and Hausmann). |
| Why is Panama missing from the growth chart? | Speaker 1 | It grew 3.9% a year, but on the canal, logistics and finance: a special case the rest of the subregion cannot copy. It is in every other chart. |
| Why measure growth from 2000? | Speaker 1 | Honduras' 1990s data has a break that fakes very fast growth, so we use 2000 to 2024 for everyone. |
| Won't foreign firms just take the profits out? | Speaker 4 | Some profit will leave. That is why the deal has conditions: local suppliers, formal hiring, a university partner. The know-how stays even when profits go. |
| Why not put research centres in Mexico City? | Speaker 4 | The capital already soaks everything up (cause 3). Second cities spread the jobs and the learning. |
| What does it cost? | Speaker 4 | Costing is the first 90-day step, built with IDB project data. We will not quote a number we cannot defend. |
| Which country first? | Speaker 4 | [Group decides before Thursday.] |
| Why will a government keep this going after an election? | Speaker 5 | Cheapest, fastest step first, and three public scores every year, so each step earns the next. |
| How is this sustainable and inclusive? | Speaker 5 | Inclusive: it reaches the towns and the informal workers. Sustainable: growth built on know-how lasts, unlike a commodity boom. |
