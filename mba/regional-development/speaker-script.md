# Eight Patents: speaker script

Group 1 · Ernst & Young (EY) for the IDB · 20 minutes, 5 speakers, then Q&A.

One message, said at least 5 times: **Know-how is bought, not born.**

| Speaker | Slides | Part | Target time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker 1 | 1–4 | Hook and executive summary | 4 min |
| Speaker 2 | 5–9 | Past, and present: the evidence | 4 min |
| Speaker 3 | 10–12 | Climax and the two reasons | 3.5 min |
| Speaker 4 | 13–16 | Future: Costa Rica and the three steps | 4 min |
| Speaker 5 | 17–18 | Roll-out, close, then leads Q&A | 3 min |

## Slide 1 · Speaker 1

Leave this up while the room settles. Do not say "good morning" yet. Your first words are the hook on the next slide; introductions come on slide 4.

## Slide 2 · Speaker 1

Click, then wait for all eight dots before you speak. About 45 seconds on this slide.

"Last year, the whole of Guatemala filed eight patents. Eight. For 18 million people."

If needed: a patent is a new invention someone registers so they can use it. It is the simplest count of new ideas a country produces.

## Slide 3 · Speaker 1

About 30 seconds.

"The same year, South Korea filed 195,786." Pause two seconds. Let the number land.

"And Korea has fewer people than Mexico." (Korea about 52 million, Mexico about 130 million.)

## Slide 4 · Speaker 1

About 2 minutes. This is the executive summary.

1. Introduce the group: "We are Group 1 from EY. The IDB commissioned us to answer one question: why has growth in Mexico and Central America been so disappointing?"

2. "If you remember one thing today: know-how is bought, not born." (Message #1)

3. Map, one line each: the past (they started level), the present (Korea paid to learn), the future (what the IDB can do).

4. Why the IDB should care: "You get a practical plan for inclusive growth that lasts. You can fund it this year and check its scores every year."

Hand over: "[Speaker 2] takes us back to 1980."

## Slide 5 · Speaker 2

About 45 seconds.

"Go back to 1980. Mexico filed 704 patents, Korea 1,241. Close. Two kids, same classroom, similar grades."

Point out: same scale for both, so you can compare them by eye.

## Slide 6 · Speaker 2

About 1 minute. Click, and let the chart move before you talk over it.

The chart opens on 1980 to 1985, when the two are level. Then it pulls back to 2024.

"Then Korea started doing its homework. Korea's patents grew 158 times. Mexico's grew 1.7 times."

If anyone asks about a year, hover the chart. It shows both countries for that year.

## Slide 7 · Speaker 2

About 45 seconds.

"What was the homework? Korea spends almost 5% of its whole economy on research, every year. Mexico spends 0.3%."

"That's not luck. It's a line in a budget that someone chose to write."

## Slide 8 · Speaker 2

About 45 seconds.

"And Korea pays people to invent. For every researcher in Mexico, Korea has about 22."

Spice if you have time: "Guatemala has about 26 researchers in the whole country." (1.4 per million × 18.4 million people.)

## Slide 9 · Speaker 2

About 45 seconds.

"Did the homework pay off? In 1960, Mexico was four times richer than Korea. Around 1988 they swapped. Today Korea is 3.6 times richer than Mexico."

Hand over: "So what happened here? [Speaker 3]."

## Slide 10 · Speaker 3

The climax. Slow down. About 45 seconds.

"So this is not bad luck, and it's not culture. Know-how is bought, and nobody here bought it." (Message #2)

Pause. "Why not? Two reasons."

## Slide 11 · Speaker 3

About 1 minute 15.

"First reason: the capital cities pull in the money, the people and the talent, and send very little back."

"Economists call this backwash. Myrdal named it in 1957. Everyone else calls it leaving."

Link to the course: growth poles (Perroux) are meant to spread growth. Here they only pull it in.

## Slide 12 · Speaker 3

About 1 minute 15.

"Second reason: hiring someone officially costs extra. Payroll taxes pay for health and pensions. So businesses stay small and off the books, and small hidden businesses never learn anything new."

This is Santiago Levy's argument. Don't quote an informality percentage. We haven't pulled it yet.

Hand over: "So what do we do about it? [Speaker 4]."

## Slide 13 · Speaker 4

About 1 minute.

"Good news: a neighbour already did it. Costa Rica went after foreign factories on purpose, through its investment agency CINDE. FDI brings technology and know-how, and local firms and workers learn from it."

"Know-how can be bought." (Message #3)

⚠ Check the CINDE details before Thursday.

## Slide 14 · Speaker 4

About 1 minute.

"Step one: build a learning team. It picks one new industry, proves it works, hands it to local firms, and then steps away. Its whole job is to make itself unnecessary."

"Chile did exactly this with salmon, through Fundación Chile."

Why now: factories are leaving China and choosing new homes. The region can be ready for them.

## Slide 15 · Speaker 4

About 1 minute.

"Step two: make hiring cheaper. Pay for health and pensions from general taxes, not from wages. Then businesses can grow out in the open, the same firms we saw stuck behind the wall."

Say the cost before anyone asks: "It costs money before it saves money."

## Slide 16 · Speaker 4

About 1 minute.

"Step three: connect the towns outside the big city, with roads, internet and suppliers, so growth spreads instead of pooling in the capital."

Course link: a growth pole only works if local firms learn to supply the big plant and the towns around it get connected. Those are the two steps that usually get skipped.

"Medellín's cable cars did this for neighbourhoods the city had written off."

Hand over: "[Speaker 5] on how we roll it out."

## Slide 17 · Speaker 5

About 1 minute 30.

"Governments last four years. These programmes don't. So the cheapest, fastest step goes first, and each step has to show results that protect the next one."

"Every year we publish three scores: inventions per person, compared with Chile and Brazil, not Korea; how far the poorest region trails the richest; and how many workers are on the books."

"We are not promising a growth number. A number we can't defend is worth less than an honest blank."
If asked about the brief's words: inclusive, because it reaches the towns and the informal workers. Sustainable, because growth built on know-how lasts, unlike a commodity boom.

## Slide 18 · Speaker 5

Close. About 1 minute. Bring it back to the opening.

"Eight inventions. In 1980 Korea started where Mexico was. The difference was homework."

Summarize in one breath: same start, different homework, a plan to start ours.
Next steps, one line: "In the next 90 days: pick the pilot country, set the three baselines, and cost the team with IDB project data."

"Know-how is bought, not born." (Message #4) "Fund the learning team, in one country, this year."

Pause two seconds. Do not say "thank you". Then: "We'd love your questions." You lead the Q&A and hand questions to whoever owns that part.

## Slide 19 · Backup (any speaker in Q&A)

Use this only in Q&A. Likely questions:

• "Korea had US support and a military government." Agree. One country is not a perfect model. That's why the plan copies regional cases (Chile, Costa Rica, Medellín).

• "Firms file patents abroad." True. Patents are the simplest comparable count, not the whole picture.

• "What does it cost?" Costing is still open and gets built with IDB project data. Say so plainly.

• "Which country first?" Pick one as a group before Thursday.
