# Memento Mori

When a Roman general came home victorious, they gave him a parade.

Crowds. Gold. The whole city chanting his name.

And they put a man in the chariot behind him whose only job was to lean close, all day, and repeat one sentence into his ear:

*Remember that you will die.*

Not as a threat. As maintenance.

Because a man who forgets he is mortal starts believing the parade is the point. He gets careless. He mistakes a good afternoon for a permanent condition.

We do the opposite now. We hide it. Death is something that happens in a building, to someone else, later.

And the result isn't that we feel better. It's that time stops meaning anything.

If there's always more of it, nothing you do today matters much. You can defer. You can scroll. You can wait for the version of your life that starts properly next month.

The Stoics weren't being morbid. They were being practical.

You cannot value something infinite. The whole reason a day is worth anything is that you have a finite number of them, and you don't know the number.

Remember that you will die.

Not to frighten you.

To make this afternoon real.
