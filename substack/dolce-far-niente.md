# Dolce far niente

Italian. *The sweetness of doing nothing.*

Not rest. Not a break. Not a weekend that exists so Monday can happen again.

Doing nothing, and it being sweet.

Notice how uncomfortable that is to read. Something in you is already asking what it's for.

That's the tell.

We have kept the word rest but changed its meaning. Rest is now recovery — an input, so you can produce again. You sleep to perform. You take a holiday to avoid burning out. Even the stillness is instrumental.

Which means we no longer have a word for time that isn't leading anywhere.

*Dolce far niente* is not laziness. Laziness is a failure to do something you meant to do. This is different. There is nothing you meant to do.

Sitting outside. Watching the street. A coffee going cold because you forgot about it.

The Romans had *otium* — free time, but not empty. The condition in which thought happened at all. It was the productive part of life. The rest was just business.

We inverted it, and then we wondered why nobody has an interior life.

You don't need to earn an afternoon.

Some hours are just yours.
