# The Courage To Be Disliked

Most of what you call your personality is a strategy for being approved of.

Adler's claim, and it's harder than it sounds.

Not that you want to be liked — everyone knows that. That the *shape* of you has been quietly built around it. Your opinions have edges filed off. Your ambitions are sized to be understood. You have a version of yourself for your family, and it isn't a costume, it's load-bearing.

The uncomfortable part of Adler is that he thought this was a choice. Not a wound, not something done to you. A decision you keep making, daily, because the alternative costs more.

Because the alternative is being disliked.

Not hated — that's easy to be righteous about. Just quietly thought less of. Considered difficult. Left slightly outside.

That's the actual price of living the way you'd live if nobody were watching, and it is a real price. Adler doesn't pretend otherwise.

He says freedom is not the absence of constraint. It's the willingness to be disliked by people whose approval you would prefer to keep.

You cannot have both.

Most people, given the choice, choose the approval and call it being reasonable.

Which is allowed.

Just don't also call it freedom.
