## The thesis (this is what makes it a report and not a description)

> **In April 2026 the Philippines was awarded the flagship industrial investment of its generation
> — a 4,000-acre AI-native Economic Security Zone under the US-led Pax Silica Initiative. It went
> to Luzon. It did not go to Cebu, the country's incumbent electronics-export hub. Metro Cebu's
> binding constraints are not industrial; they are urban. Water, mobility and fragmented
> metropolitan governance are now a measurable cost to national competitiveness, not a quality-of-
> life issue.**

This is the argument the rubric rewards: it prioritises rather than describes, it identifies a root
cause rather than symptoms, and it runs a single line from diagnosis through to reform agenda.

**Critical caveat — argue this, do not assert it.** The Luzon siting has obvious practical drivers
that have nothing to do with Cebu's failings: BCDA already controls a large contiguous land bank at
New Clark City, and the Luzon Economic Corridor links Subic, Clark and Manila. **No source says
Cebu was considered and rejected.** The honest form of the argument is counterfactual and
conditional: *whatever the siting rationale, Metro Cebu could not host a 4,000-acre power- and
water-intensive hub on its current infrastructure, and that is a diagnosable urban-policy failure.*
Written that way it is defensible. Written as "Cebu lost it because of congestion" it is
unsupported and a marker will say so.

## Why Pax Silica is the right spine

It supplies what most student urban reports lack: **a dated, official-source, high-stakes external
event that makes the five-pillar framework concrete.**

| | Detail | Source quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiative | **Pax Silica** — US-led coalition to secure the silicon supply chain: critical minerals, energy inputs, semiconductors, AI infrastructure, logistics | US Department of State |
| Launched | Inaugural summit, Washington DC, **12 December 2025**, convened by Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg | State Dept |
| Founding partners | Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Netherlands, UK, Israel, UAE, Australia | State Dept |
| Philippines joins | **April 2026**, as the **13th member** | PNA / press |
| The zone | **4,000 acres**, within the **Luzon Economic Corridor** (reported as New Clark City); the **first AI-native industrial acceleration hub** under Pax Silica | State Dept, 16 April 2026 |
| Timeline | Contract negotiations **2026** → planning **2027** → construction possibly early **2028** | BCDA, via press |
| Why the Philippines | 2nd-largest nickel producer; **largest exporter of nickel ore** since Indonesia's 2020 raw-ore ban; also copper and cobalt | Press |

**And the Cebu hook:** Metro Cebu's export economy runs on the **Mactan Economic Zone (MEPZ)** in
Lapu-Lapu City — ~150 hectares, 200+ locators, semiconductor assembly and test (On Semiconductor;
historically National Semiconductor, NEC, Timex). Cebu is already a silicon-supply-chain city. That
is precisely what makes its exclusion from the country's Pax Silica hub analytically interesting.

**Verify before writing:** MEPZ employment numbers and Cebu's share of national electronics exports
are **not yet sourced**. PEZA annual reports and PSA regional accounts are the places to get them.
These two numbers are load-bearing for the argument — without them "Cebu is the incumbent hub" is
an assertion.

## Mapping to the required structure

### 1. Introduction (~300 words)
Cebu in one paragraph: second metropolitan area of the Philippines, Metro Cebu population projected
**3.8 million by 2030** and near **5 million by 2050** (JICA). Why it matters: it is the country's
test of whether secondary-city growth can be managed, and the Pax Silica decision made that test
economically legible. State the thesis.

### 2. Assessment of the current situation (~700 words)
Use the five pillars **selectively** — the brief explicitly permits this. Suggested weighting:

| Pillar | Weight | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supporting city economies | **Heavy** | MEPZ, PEZA, BPO, tourism, the Pax Silica frame |
| Safe and sustainable urban environment | **Heavy** | Water, sanitation, flooding, solid waste |
| Core elements of the city system | **Heavy** | Mobility, and metropolitan governance fragmentation |
| Progressive land and housing markets | Medium | Informal settlement, coastal land pressure |
| Pro-poor policies | Medium | Fold into the above rather than a standalone section |

### 3. The three binding constraints (~800 words) — and how they interact

1. **Water and sanitation.** JICA's Metro Cebu Sewerage Master Plan (started 2024, due 2027) targets
   raising wastewater treatment to as much as **90%** — which tells you how low the base is. Metro
   Cebu has documented groundwater and supply stress.
2. **Mobility and the governance to deliver it.** The Cebu BRT and the Cebu Monorail are the
   case study: decades of planning, repeated redesign and delay. This is where a transport failure
   is really a *governance* failure.
3. **Metropolitan governance fragmentation.** Metro Cebu spans multiple LGUs with no binding
   metropolitan authority; Mega Cebu / the Metro Cebu Development and Coordinating Board is
   coordinative, not executive. **UNSURE: confirm the exact LGU count and the MCDCB's legal powers
   before writing — this is the pivot of the whole argument.**

**The interaction is the marks.** Fragmentation is the root: water and transport are both
metro-scale systems being decided at municipal scale. Say that explicitly.

### 4. Policy recommendations (~900 words)
Four at most. The rubric demands five things per recommendation — **objective, why appropriate
here, benefits and trade-offs, implementation constraints including political feasibility, and
time horizon**. Four recommendations × five elements ≈ 900 words. Five recommendations will make
each one thin, and thin is what loses marks here.

Candidate agenda, sequenced:

| # | Reform | Horizon | The hard trade-off to name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Give the metropolitan body executive authority over water, wastewater and trunk transport | Medium | LGUs lose autonomy and revenue control; this is the politically hardest item and should be said so |
| 2 | Water carrying-capacity planning and metered bulk supply before further zone expansion | Short | Constrains development approvals in the near term |
| 3 | Complete one mass-transit spine and tie land-use intensification to it | Long | Capital cost; displacement along the corridor |
| 4 | Move MEPZ's value-add up the chain — test/packaging toward design and AI-adjacent services — using PEZA incentives and Cebu's BPO labour pool | Medium | Requires power and connectivity Cebu does not yet reliably have; exposed to US policy shifts |

Recommendation 4 is where the Pax Silica spine closes the loop: Cebu's realistic play is not to
compete for a 4,000-acre hub it cannot power, but to become the **skills and services tier** of a
supply chain anchored in Luzon.

**Table 1 — Metro Cebu against national and peer benchmarks.** Rows: population and growth rate,
