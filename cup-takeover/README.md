# Cup Takeover — startups rent a café's cups for three hours

**Jordan, 2026-10-01:** marketing on coffee cups for startups. They pay for the branding for, say,
three hours, pay for the cups, put their brand and a QR code on them, and the coffee place co-brands.

---

## The arithmetic first, because it kills the obvious version

### The incumbent category exists, and its pitch does not survive scrutiny

Coffee sleeve advertising is a real industry — AdQuick, Blue Line Media, Encompass Media, Adzze all
sell it. Their published numbers:

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| $0.50 – $1.50 per sleeve | industry sellers |
| **"CPM of $1–$4, a fraction of Facebook local ($11.20) or Google Display ($6.90)"** | industry sellers |
| "Recall rates exceeding 80%" | industry sellers, **no study cited** |
| **Minimum purchase $30,000 per market** | industry sellers |

**The first two claims are mutually inconsistent by two orders of magnitude.** CPM is cost per
thousand impressions. If a sleeve costs $0.50 and reaches one coffee drinker, that is a **$500 CPM.**
To get to the advertised $2 CPM you need **roughly 250 impressions per sleeve** — every cup seen by
250 people.

Be generous and say a held cup is seen by 5 people. Then:

> **$0.50 per sleeve ÷ 5 impressions = $100 CPM.**
>
> **That is roughly nine times worse than Facebook local, not a fraction of it.**

The category sells itself on an impression multiplier that does not exist. **Do not repeat their CPM
claim to a buyer who can do arithmetic — and a startup founder can.**

### The QR code will not save it

Verified benchmarks for passive print placements:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Scan rate, passive print (posters, print ads) | **1–5%** |
| High-performing print campaigns | 3–8% |
| **QR codes that get ZERO scans** | **34%** |
| Median scans per QR code | **2** |

**So a 3-hour takeover at 300 cups yields 3 to 15 scans.** That is the honest number.

**Three scans is not a marketing outcome, and the founder will know by Tuesday.** Any version of this
that sells scans has one customer and no second month.

### And the $30,000 minimum kills the stated model outright

The incumbents require $30,000 per market because cup advertising only works as mass media. **A
seed-stage startup does not have $30,000 for cups.** The category is built for brands with media
budgets, which is the opposite of the buyer in this idea.

## Key Assumptions Check

*CIA Tradecraft Primer technique, applied per the frameworks library.*

| # | Assumption the idea rests on | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Startups will pay for brand exposure | **Weak.** Startups pay for *users* and *proof*. Exposure is what they are told to want by people selling exposure |
| 2 | A coffee cup generates meaningful impressions | **FAILS.** See the arithmetic. ~5 views per cup |
| 3 | The QR code converts | **FAILS.** 1–5%, median 2 scans, a third get zero |
| 4 | Cafés want co-branding | **Untested and probably conditional.** A café's cup is its own branding; handing it to a stranger for three hours has a real cost to them |
| 5 | Printed cups can be produced in small runs affordably | **UNVERIFIED and it is the live question.** Print economics are dominated by setup at 300 units. **Quote this before anything else** |
| 6 | Three hours is a meaningful unit | **Weak as media. Strong as an event.** Nobody buys three hours of reach. People do buy a launch moment |

**Assumption 2 and 3 failing together means the product is not advertising.** The idea survives only
if what is being sold is something other than impressions.

---

## What survives: it is an event product, not a media product

The reframe the numbers force:

> **Nobody will buy three hours of coffee-cup reach. A startup might buy a launch moment it can
> photograph.**

A seed-stage startup has a launch week, a funding announcement, a product release — and almost no
money for anything that photographs well. What it actually needs is **content and a story**, not
impressions.

So the deliverable changes:

| Media product *(dies)* | Event product *(survives)* |
| --- | --- |
| 300 branded cups | 300 branded cups **plus a photographer** |
| "80% recall" | 10–15 usable social assets the founder owns |
| 3–15 QR scans | **The café's own post to its own followers** |
| CPM comparison | A dated, located, real-world activation |
| Unmeasurable | Measurable: posts, reach, saves |

**The cups become the set, not the medium.** That is a defensible product because the output is
undeniable: the founder has photographs of their logo in a real café in a real city, and the café
posted about it.

## Three versions

### SAFE — one café, one startup, one afternoon, done by hand
- Find **one** Madrid café and **one** startup with a launch in the next six weeks.
- 300 cups, a photographer for ninety minutes, the café posts once.
- Price it as a package, not as media.
- *Tradeoff:* a service business with your hands in it, no leverage. **But it is the only version that tests assumption 5 and assumption 1 for real money.**

### BOLD — the launch-day activation product, sold off the Niah list  ← **recommended if this gets a slot**
- Productise it: **"Launch Day in a Café"** — cups, sleeves, a table card, a photographer, 12 assets, the café's post. One fixed price.
- **The buyer list already exists.** `niah_prospects` holds **893 researched event and startup contacts** that have never been contacted. Niah sells to exactly this buyer. This is the same list with a cheaper, more concrete offer.
- Three café partners on a standing rate so supply is not a per-deal negotiation.
- *Tradeoff:* needs three cafés signed and a photographer on call before you can sell it. But it reuses 893 prospects that are sitting idle, which no other idea in the estate does.

### WEIRD — the café sells it, you take the margin
- You never sell to startups at all. You sign cafés, give them a one-page menu and a QR ordering link, and **the café upsells its own regulars** — half of whom in a Madrid co-working district *are* the startups.
- You supply cups and take a cut.
- *Tradeoff:* the strongest version if it works, because supply becomes the salesforce and you have no outreach burden at all — which is precisely the constraint ACH identified. The risk is that cafés will not sell anything, and a café that does not sell is a dead channel you cannot chase.

## Top 3 ways this goes wrong

1. **You sell it as advertising.** Then the founder measures scans, gets three, and tells other
   founders. **Mitigation: never quote impressions, never quote the industry's CPM, never promise QR
   performance. Sell the assets and the café's post, which are deliverable with certainty.**
2. **Small-run cup printing is uneconomic.** If 300 cups cost €400 because of plate setup, the margin
   disappears. **Mitigation: get three printer quotes before you speak to a single café or startup.
   This is the cheapest kill-test available and it costs one afternoon.**
3. **Cafés say no.** A café's cup is its own brand asset and three hours of it is a real concession.
   **Mitigation: ask two cafés before building anything. If both say no, the idea is dead and you have
   spent nothing.**

## Does it earn a slot? Honestly, not yet

The ACH analysis concluded the binding constraint in this estate is **the last mile — a human step
that does not happen** — and that there are already **22 projects built and not in use.** Under
Reinertsen's WIP logic, adding a 42nd project while 22 sit idle makes the queue worse, not better.

**So this is logged, not started.** Two things would change that:

- It is **one of very few ideas here that reuses an idle asset** — the 893 Niah prospects.
- Its kill-test is **genuinely cheap**: three printer quotes and two café conversations. Under a day,
  and it either dies or it does not.

**Recommendation: do the kill-test, do not build.** If cup printing is cheap and one café says yes,
it earns a slot when something else ships.

1. **Three printer quotes** for 300 single-colour printed cups, Madrid, small run. *(Claude can do

2. **Two café conversations.** Would you let a startup brand your cups for three hours, and what

3. **One startup conversation.** Would you pay €1,000 for cups, a photographer and the café's post on
   your launch day? *(Jordan only. One conversation.)*

**If steps 1–3 all come back positive, run the SAFE version once. If any one fails, it is dead and it
cost you three conversations.**
