## Half B — the line you must not cross, and why

### What the law says

- **Exchange Act §15(a)(1)** makes it unlawful for an unregistered "broker" to effect transactions in
  securities, **or to induce or attempt to induce the purchase or sale of any security**.
- **§3(a)(4)(A)** defines a broker as a person "engaged in the business of effecting transactions in
  securities for the account of others."
- **Transaction-based compensation is the single most important factor.** The SEC's long-standing
  position is that it gives the finder a "salesman's stake" and is a hallmark of broker activity.
- **The SEC's own Guide to Broker-Dealer Registration states plainly: "placement agents are not
  exempt from broker-dealer registration."**

### Four things people get wrong about this

1. **"It's a one-off, so it's fine."** No. One-time compensation can still be transaction-based
   compensation, and that is the core issue. The SEC has also taken the position that accepting a
   flat fee more than once can require registration.
2. **"There's a finders exemption."** There is not. The SEC **proposed** one in October 2020, received
   90+ overwhelmingly critical comment letters, and **never adopted it.** It cannot be relied on. A
   no-action request on exactly this point (Brumberg, Mackey & Wall, 2010) was **denied**.
3. **"We'll disclose it to investors."** Disclosure does not cure it. Broker-dealer registration is a
   **structural requirement, not a disclosure obligation.**
4. **"Only the fund is exposed."** Both sides are. The finder faces SEC sanctions and potential
   criminal exposure; the fund faces aiding-and-abetting or causing a §15(a) violation; and investors
   may acquire **rescission rights** — the right to demand their money back.

### The consequence that would hurt you most

**An unregistered finder's fee agreement is unenforceable.** If a fund decides not to pay you, you
cannot sue to collect. You would do the work, generate the capital, and have no legal claim to the fee.

### The legitimate route, and it is cheap

If capital introduction is a business you actually want, register with a **sponsoring broker-dealer**:

| Item | Reported cost |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsoring BD fee | ~**$6,000/year** (varies by firm) |
| **Series 82** exam (private securities offerings representative — exactly this activity) or Series 7 | $100 – $400 |
| Time to register | **4 – 8 weeks** |

Against placement fees of **6–10% of capital raised**, that is inexpensive. Series 82 is the
narrowly-scoped licence for precisely this work. **UNVERIFIED: current sponsoring-BD pricing and
whether a US person resident abroad can register this way — ask the lawyer.**

## The structure that works

```
Kinsol LLC (Colorado)
  └── contracts with the fund for SOURCING services
        - flat retainer or per-qualified-company fee
        - no carry, no percentage of capital, no success fee on investment
        - fund does all diligence, decisions and documentation
  └── Amigo Sales delivers the outreach as subcontractor (white-label)
        - consistent with the Kinsol-Amigo billing agreement, clause 2.4
```

**Capital introduction stays out of this entity and out of this engagement** until the lawyer hour
happens and, if you want that business, until a Series 82 and a sponsoring BD are in place.

## Top 3 ways this goes wrong

1. **A fund asks you to "also help us close an LP or two" and you say yes.** This is how it happens —
   not by deciding to be a placement agent, but by accommodating a client. **Mitigation: a written
   line in the engagement letter that Kinsol does not solicit investors, and a scripted answer for
   when you are asked.**
2. **The fee is structured as a percentage of anything.** Even on the sourcing side, "1% of what you
   deploy into companies we found" starts to look transaction-based. **Mitigation: retainer or
   per-company, never percentage.**
3. **It becomes a fifth lane while nothing earns.** You have 27 built and 13 live projects and zero
   revenue. **Mitigation: this is a service sold off an existing machine to a named fund, or it is
   not started. No build, no website, no deck until one fund says a number.**
